# Chapter 1 — *The Blood Relic's tithe*
### Cyran | POV

I stand on a rib. Not a metaphorical one, though the city does love those. A real, arched fragment of god-bone, curving over the street like the broken cage of some titanic chest, slick with condensation and the greasy sheen of marrow vapor. The wind—what little of it can be bothered to stir at this hour—slides over my face, cool and damp, bringing with it the distant echo of market bells, the murmur of sleepless sinners, and underneath it all, that low, constant creak of settling bones that means the Reliquary hasn’t yet decided to collapse in on itself and end our collective misery.

My boots are already ruined.

I tip my weight forward on the balls of my feet, leather armor creaking softly, and peer down into the fog-choked artery of the street below. Lamps burn low, swallowed in the marrow-haze so that everything glows faintly red and green, as if the city is holding its breath beneath stained glass. Somewhere out there in the murk, a feral god-spawn is chewing its way through the late-night population like a child loose in a confectioner’s.

My goal is elegant in its simplicity: find the beast, kill it before it kills anyone else, and make sure the right people notice so they carve a few more Merit Marks into my ledger instead of my hide. Freedom by inches, paid in blood and performance.

If I were a better monster, I wouldn’t enjoy this part so much.

A shriek knifes through the night—high, human, and far enough away that if I misjudge the distance, I’ll be peeling what’s left of them off the cobbles later. My jaw tightens before my thoughts catch up, body already shifting, weight gathering. The sound ricochets around the ribcage arches, but there’s a clarity to terror if you know how to listen.

Left. Two streets down, one level lower. Near the cistern access. Of course.

“Lazy thing,” I murmur to no one, straightening the line of my cuff where a scuff of grime mars the black leather. “Couldn’t have picked somewhere with better lighting?”​

The shriek cuts off.

That’s the clock starting.

I step off the rib.

For one suspended heartbeat there’s only air, damp and cold against my face, my coat flaring behind me like a curtain at the close of a show. Then my boots kiss bone again as I land on the sloped roof of a calcified townhouse, knees bending to take the force, fingers brushing stone for balance. Not a sound, not even the clatter of loosened grit. The Vigil likes its Relics graceful. Showy. If we must be monsters, we are at least expensive ones.

The mist licks at my cheeks as I run, low and fast along the roofline, shadows slipping past shadows. Rot rides the air first—a sweet-sour stench of old garbage and meat left too long at the altars. Then ozone, sharp and wrong, like lightning trapped in a jar. And underneath that, the bright, thin tang of fresh fear-sweat.​

Ah. There you are.

I angle my path toward it, boots finding the familiar rhythm of patrol along the ribcage’s spine: leap, land, slide, push off another spur of bone. Below, the street curls between towering arcs of rib, their inner curves strung with bioluminescent moss that throbs a dull, exhausted green. The light paints everyone who passes beneath it in the same sickly hue, no matter their sins. Convenient.

As I vault a narrow gap between structures—a vertebra-bridge, worn smooth by centuries of carts—my gaze snags on a shrine carved into the crook of a rib. The dead god’s face is stylized, all hollow eyes and bleeding sigils, mouth open in an eternal, silent command. Someone’s tucked a wilted bouquet of vein-flowers into its jaw, their petals bruised to black.​

I land beside it and, on impulse, lift two fingers to my lips, then tap the statue’s stone cheek in a parody of piety. “Your child’s misbehaving,” I murmur. “I’ll handle it.”

The mist shifts. A shape—too many limbs, too much joint where there should be smooth curve—skitters along the wall below and vanishes into the yawning circle of a cistern hatch.​

“Of course,” I sigh. “Sewers. My boots thank you.”

I drop.

The hatch rim is slick with grease and something that shines too darkly to be normal water. My gloved fingers bite into bone as I swing down, then release, letting myself slide feet-first into the tunnel. The world narrows to wet stone scraping my shoulders and the rush of air past my ears, the chute angling sharply until gravity does most of the work. The temperature rises by slow degrees, cold damp giving way to a cloying, humid warmth that settles under my armor like another skin.

The smell hits me halfway down.

Not the thin, bright ribbon of mortal blood—though that’s here too, smeared along the wall as if someone’s hand dragged in passing—but the thicker, older scent that lives down in the bones of this place. Iron and salt and something electric that buzzes against my tongue when I breathe through my mouth.

God-blood.

What’s left of the Hollow Titan’s heart congealed in cisterns and pools beneath the district, still pulsing on some ancient, stubborn rhythm that has nothing to do with living bodies. The cisterns are supposed to be sealed, monitored, taxed in whispers and fumes by the Contract Lords. They tithe power like everything else in this city—every prayer, every execution, every intimate little signature meant to feed the Prime Contract sleeping under our laws.​

I’m not meant to be here unsupervised.

The chute spits me out onto a slick stone ledge, boots skidding, arms briefly pinwheeling. I catch myself on a pillar, fingers digging into ancient chisel marks, and push upright. The chamber opens around me, vast and echoing. The ceiling disappears into shadow; the floor is more suggestion than certainty, broken by great, circular pools where the Titan’s blood has collected in slow, viscous lakes.

They glow. Faintly, but enough—a sullen, dark red that throbs in time with a heartbeat I can feel in the backs of my teeth.

I swallow. My own hunger stirs, lazy and interested, stretching like a cat before deciding whether the prey is worth the effort. The Thirst is never gone; it’s simply polite enough most nights to stay in the corner of the room, pretending it isn’t watching me.

Tonight, it leans forward.

“Well,” I say, voice sounding a touch too bright even to my own ears. “Isn’t this festive.”

A splash answers me from the far side of the nearest pool. Heavy. Intentional.

My hands know what to do while the rest of me prevaricates. They move to my thighs, fingers unbuckling the straps that hold my daggers in place. Bone hilts—polished smooth with use, carved with inlaid sigils that lick cold against my palms when I draw them free. Twin blades, perfectly balanced, edges catching the bloodlight in clean, pale arcs. I spin them once, more for the feel than the spectacle; even alone, I prefer to give myself a show worth watching.

Instead of creeping, I start humming. An aria I can’t quite forget, all long, tragic notes and foolish lovers. The sound threads through pillars and pump mechanisms, bounces off stone, announces me. If it’s thinking about ambush, it can think again.

Something skitters around a column ahead, claws scratching stone. I get a brief flash of movement: jointed limbs flexing too far, plates of bone where skin should be, a smear of black ichor along the path. Then it’s gone again, circling.

“You’ve been busy,” I call, stepping onto the narrow lip of stone that runs along the pool. The blood below glows dully, close enough that if I tilted a hand I could trail my fingers through it. I don’t. “Citizens are filing complaints. Noise, property damage, partial consumption.”

I pick my steps with care. The stone is treacherous, every surface filmed with condensation and bone-dust. I keep my weight forward and light, ready to shift. My eyes track the ripple of shadows ahead. The Thirst paces quietly, content for now to watch.

The creature shows itself at the edge of the light.

It unfolds out of the dark, too many legs splayed wide to brace its bulk between two pillars. Carapace slick and patchy, chunks of mismatched bone pressed into raw, pulsing flesh. Its head can’t decide on a shape—two jaws trying to occupy the same space, teeth jutting inward and outward at once, dripping slime. Four eyes, then six, then I stop counting, because they all land on me.

The smell of sulfur hits with its breath. My eyes water.

“Oh, that’s unfortunate,” I say. “Who let you out half-finished?”

It screams at me, a sound like boiling kettles and metal scraped over glass. The vibration shivers down my spine. Then it lunges.

Claws slam into the walkway where I’d been standing. Stone explodes into shards that ping off my armor. I’m already sideways, weight flipped, left foot planting, right heel sliding. The swipe hisses past my chest close enough that one hooked talon scrapes my coat.

Fast. Sloppy. All appetite, no finesse.

We circle each other on the thin run of stone. It clacks and hisses, limbs scrabbling for purchase. I keep sliding just out of reach, small shifts of hips and shoulders turning near-misses into air. Each pass, my blades test a different angle—nick at a joint there, slice across the soft tissue under a plate here. Black ichor streaks its flanks, hissing where it drips into the god-blood.

It shudders, shakes droplets everywhere. One spatters my cheek, viscous and hot. I feel the sting of acid start and fade as skin knits under it.

The beast rears higher, limbs spread to either side for balance, and brings both front claws down like hammers. The impact cracks a pillar. Dust and bits of bone filter from above, sprinkling the pool and my shoulders in fine white grit.

“Careful,” I say between notes of the aria, dancing back. “That’s load-bearing.”

It doesn’t appreciate the joke. Its next swipe comes in lower, a scything arc toward my knees. I jump, boots hitting the raised edge of a maintenance platform, stone slick underfoot. My shoulders slam into a rusted pipe. The monster’s claws screech along metal, sparks spitting.

The pipe judders. Old bolts groan. A trickle of something thin and cold leaks from a seam and runs down the back of my neck.

“Absolutely not,” I mutter, shivering.

I push off the pipe and drop back to the walkway as the creature recovers. It’s breathing harder now, sides heaving unevenly where I’ve cut into it. One leg drags, half-severed tendons flapping. It gathers itself for another charge anyway.

Behind it, in the shadows along the far wall, something small moves. A quick, jerky flinch.

I catch it out of the corner of my eye—just enough: pale face, wide eyes, body pressed into the dark hollow behind a pump mechanism. A child. Knees hugged to chest, fingers clenched tight in worn fabric.

For a heartbeat, my focus splits.

The creature smells the fear. It pivots so fast its bad leg nearly gives. Its head jerks, jaws yawning toward the pump, sulfur-breath turning in that direction. The sound it makes now is different; hungry, eager.

“No,” I say, and the word punches out of me sharper than any blade.

Playtime ends.

It surges toward the recess. I move. There’s no room for curve and flourish now, only straight lines. I sprint along the lip of the pool, stone slick under my boots, arms pumping. Every pulse of god-blood underfoot counts off distance: three strides, two, one—

The walkway ends ahead in a broken drop. I don’t stop.

I plant my last step on the edge and jump, letting the momentum carry me under the sweep of its next claw. The hem of my coat snaps, catching a few errant barbs. My body twists in the air; the world inverts, monster below, red pool sliding by in my peripheral vision.

For a fraction of a second, I’m above its back, weightless. Then gravity remembers me.

I come down hard, boots slamming onto the knotted ridge at the base of its neck. The impact drives its front end toward the stone with a shriek. I ride the drop, knees bending, and ram both blades down into the thick cluster of bone and gristle under my boots.

There’s resistance, dense and rubbery. I snarl, put my shoulders into it, driving until I feel something crack. A jolt runs through the creature—the shudder of a severed connection. Its limbs flail in a wild, weakening storm, claws carving gouges in the stone inches from the pump housing.

“You stay away from them,” I hiss through gritted teeth.

It twitches, keens, then collapses sideways in a heavy, wet slide. I leap off just before it crushes the walkway, land in a stagger, one hand skidding along the wall for balance. The whole cavern vibrates for a moment with the impact. Ripples shudder across the pools.

Then stillness, except for the ragged rasp of my breath.

The knives stay buried in bone until I’m sure it’s done. Its legs give a few last, meaningless spasms; ichor oozes around the hilts and drips into the blood below. When the last tremor fades, I grip the handles and yank them free, breath hissing between my teeth at the strain in my shoulders.

I wipe one blade on a relatively clean patch of my sleeve. The smear it leaves glistens black in the red light. My cheek throbs where acid splashed; when I touch the spot with gloved fingers there’s only a line of faintly warmer skin, already healed.

A small sound comes from the recess. Not a scream this time—more like air shuddering out of tight lungs.

I turn slowly. No sudden moves.

The child is still there, of course he’s still there. The city never misses an opportunity to improve its tragedies. He’s curled into himself in the narrow space behind the pump housing, as if the machinery might swallow him whole if he presses hard enough. Up close, he’s even smaller than that first blurred glimpse suggested—eight, maybe nine at most, all coltish limbs and sharp angles as if someone sketched a boy and forgot to fill him in.

His shirt hangs off one shoulder, seams blown and hastily mended in uneven stitches. The fabric might once have been blue; now it’s a tired, murky grey, stiff with old salt and newer grime. Bare knees jut from ragged shorts, knobbly and bruised, a fresh scrape glistening wet along one shin where he must’ve gone down hard. His toes are naked on the cold stone, nails bitten to the quick, skin gone blotchy with chill and filth.

His hair is the color of old walnut shells, hacked short with no attention to charm or symmetry, bits of it sticking up like he’s been running fingers through it for hours trying to wake from a nightmare that won’t end. It clings damply to his forehead now, sweat or mist or both. And his eyes—

His eyes are too big for his face, dark as spilled ink, whites showing all the way around the irises. They shine too bright in that grubby little face, catching the god-blood’s glow in a way that makes them look fevered. He stares at the corpse. Then at me.

For one, absurd instant I think, irrationally, that perhaps he’s not afraid. That perhaps the story his terrified brain will tell itself is simple: monster, savior, grateful tears, a lifetime of fond whispers about the Vigil’s pale hound who pulled him from the jaws of death.

Then he flinches as my eyes meet his. His fingers claw deeper into his own sleeves, trying to fold himself into nothingness. The motion pulls his shirt higher, exposing a flash of too-thin wrist ringed in the pale pressure-mark of some grown-up’s grip, already shading toward bruise.

Ah. There it is.

Not just fear, but the sharp, particular horror reserved for things that wear a man’s shape and move like something else entirely. For creatures whose eyes catch the bloodlight like a cat’s, whose teeth, when they smile, do not quite stop where human teeth should.

The echo of the kill is still singing in my bones. The god-blood’s hum threads through it, an undercurrent that whispers mine in a voice older than language. The Thirst prowls restlessly, annoyed at being denied playtime, pressing its tongue along my nerves.

I sheath one dagger, then the other, gestures exaggerated so he can follow every motion. Empty hands, open and away from my body. I take one step forward, then stop when his shoulders jump like I’ve fired a crossbow.

“Terrible place for a midnight stroll,” I say, voice pitching toward light. “The view is appalling, and the company’s worse.”

His gaze flickers to my face, then down to my boots, then back to the monster. His throat bobs. When he finally speaks, the word scrapes out high and raw, as if it’s been dragged over stone on the way up.

“I—” He swallows, tries again. “It grabbed my cousin. On the street. I followed.”

Of course he did. People do foolish things when someone they love screams. Children do them faster.

“Is your cousin here?” I ask.

He shakes his head once. Quick, sharp. His fingers dig into his own arms until the knuckles pale under the grime. No need to ask more. The answer is written in the way his gaze skitters away from the heap of flesh at my feet and then back as if he can’t help looking, trying to recognize a shape that isn’t there anymore.

My tongue finds the small cut I bit into it during the fight, and I taste my own blood, copper-bright against the lingering god-iron in the air. Hunger prowls in acknowledgment, but it’s a distant, manageable thing now the danger’s passed. I swallow it down.

“I’m sorry,” I say simply. No bow, no flourish.

The words hang there, heavier than a joke would have been.

His gaze snaps back to me, surprise cutting through fear for a heartbeat. He’s close enough now that I can see the tear-tracks on his cheeks, carving clean lines through the soot and tunnel-dust. One has picked up a rusty smear where it crossed the scrape on his shin, tinting it pink.

“You…” His eyes narrow, studying. “You killed it.”

“Yes.” I tip my head toward the carcass. “One unauthorized god-spawn, neutralized. I’ll be sure to file the requisite forms.”

“You’re Vigil,” he says, like it’s both accusation and relief. His gaze tracks along my arms as he speaks, squinting at the glimpses of carved sigils showing where my bracers have slid back. “One of the… Relics.”

“Cyran,” I supply. “I find things that eat people and make them stop.”

He thinks about that, chewing at the inside of his cheek. His eyes keep bouncing between me and the dead monster, as if trying to slot us into separate categories and failing. The boy and the beast and the thing that killed it, all crammed into the same trembling moment.

“It was going to get me,” he whispers.

“It didn’t.” I let a small, sharp smile show. “Awful at follow-through, that one.”

Silence coils in again. The low thrum of the pools fills it, each pulse nudging at the edges of my awareness. The blood feels closer now, as if my own killing has tuned me to its frequency. If I stand very still, I can almost imagine words forming in the beat.

I don’t stand still.

“There’s a maintenance stair that way,” I say, nodding toward a narrow flight of stone steps winding up the wall nearby. “It comes out by the lower tooth-market. Do you know it?”

He nods, a little too fast, like he’s agreeing with a test he’s not sure he studied for.

“Good. Take it. Go home. If any officer tries to fine you for being down here, tell them Relic Cyran will happily educate them on proper priorities.”

The corner of his mouth twitches, a ghost of something that might one day remember how to be a smile. “Is that allowed?”

“No,” I say. “But it’ll be memorable.”

I step aside, making a clear path between him and the stairs. He hesitates another moment, pinned by the weight of everything down here—the corpse, the pools, me. Then he uncoils from the little trench of shadow he’s made for himself, one careful step at a time.

As he comes fully into the bloodlight, the hollows under his cheekbones look carved. Someone has not been feeding this child enough. His shoulders are too sharp for his age, collarbones like handles jutting under the stretched neck of his shirt. There’s ink smeared along one forearm in clumsy loops, a child’s attempt at a sigil that never quite took—playacting contracts in chalk and gutter water.

As he passes within arm’s reach, the iron tang of old blood and grime and unwashed fear clings to him. His bare foot slips; he yelps, catching himself on the pump housing.

My hand moves without thinking. Fingers close around his upper arm, steadying. His skin is hot against my glove, the rapid thud of his pulse a frantic drum under too-thin flesh.

He freezes, eyes snapping up to mine. Up close like this, I can see the fine tremor in his lashes, the way his pupils blow wide, swallowing the brown until they’re almost all darkness. He smells of alleyways and cheap incense and the faint, stale sweetness of some stolen street-stall pastry on his breath.

“Easy,” I say quietly. “I’ve got you.”

For a breath, we stay like that—me anchoring him, him searching my face for… something. Promise, threat, explanation. Whatever it is, he doesn’t find enough of it to be comforting. I keep my expression level, the performance stripped back to something bare and simple. No teeth. No jokes.

He eases his arm out of my grip. I let go immediately.

“Thank you,” he says, voice barely more than air.

I incline my head. “You’re welcome.”

He retreats toward the stairs, never quite turning his back on me until the angle of the wall forces it. At the first step, he glances once at the monster’s body, once at me. Fear wins. He bolts up the staircase, small feet slapping stone, footsteps echoing until they fade into the city’s deeper creaks.

When the last patter dies, the cistern feels bigger. Emptier.

Just me, the corpse, and the god-blood.

The pools’ slow pulse presses harder now the noise is gone. My Marks prickle faintly under my skin, the carved sigils along my forearms responding to the kill, to the blood, to the whole closed system that turns death into numbers on someone else’s ledger. The whisper that haunts this place brushes my thoughts again, almost-words forming below hearing.
​

I ignore it in the only way I can: by working.

The Tribunal will want proof. I kneel beside the monster, boots squelching in congealing ichor, and set one knife to its ruined hide. It’s tougher now that life has stopped, flesh fighting the blade with stubborn resistance. I pry plates apart, dig beneath them, searching for the branded knot of flesh that bears the Merit sigil.

There. A ring of tight, scarred tissue tucked under an overlapping ridge of bone, lines of an ugly circle burned deep into it. I cut it free with quick, efficient strokes and hold it up. The sigil glows faintly, answering the lines carved into my own arms. Another tidy entry on my invisible account.

“Congratulations,” I tell the dead thing softly. “You just helped redeem the monster that killed you. Circle of life.”

The joke falls flat in the damp air. I shove the strip of flesh into a pouch at my belt. Its heat seeps through the leather, a low, unpleasant throb.

The pools answer with a pulse of their own, stronger, as if in appreciation. The whisper under the beat spikes—my name, almost, shapes pressing at the inside of my skull. Heat licks the back of my neck.

Not tonight.

I straighten, sheathe my knife, and turn my back on the blood. The hum of it clings to my spine as I cross to the stair, boots leaving sticky tracks on stone. Every step up is away from that pull and toward another kind of leash—reports, audits, ledgers. A different pulsing heart in another part of the corpse, this one made of ink and signatures instead of congealed divinity.
​

By the time I push the hatch open, the marrow-mist is rolling thick through the Ribcage again, cool and greasy against my face. The bone-arches loom overhead, ribs of a god long past saving.

Somewhere above, the Tribunal’s towers watch. Somewhere below, the Titan’s heart keeps beating, patient as a debt.

I adjust my bracer where the leather’s twisted, flick a last smear of ichor from my sleeve, and step out into the street, letting the mist close around me. Another hunt done, another Mark earned, and a little boy who will remember that when the monster died, something worse stood in its place and bowed.
