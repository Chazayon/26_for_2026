# Chapter 24 — *The Arrest in the Spine*
### Lysa | POV

I have written seventy-four pages defending a monster, but the Tribunal only needs one page to destroy a Scribe.

The administrative bell tolls for sunset, a deep, resonant, hollow sound that echoes down the grand corridors of the Spine District like a physical weight. It means the Registry Office closes in exactly five minutes. If I don't file the formal appeal against Cyran's "Donor Event" before that heavy oak door shuts, Lord Thessen’s executive order holds by default, and Cyran goes back to Varos at dawn.

I blow on the wet ink of the final page, my breath scattering a few loose grains of blotting sand across the worn wood of my desk. My fingers are cramped into agonizing hooks, stained black up to the knuckles. I haven't slept in over thirty hours. The smear campaign broadsheets—burning with vicious, twisted lies about my "bewitched compromises"—are piled on the corner of my desk like toxic waste I haven't had time to clean up. 

I ignore them. The law is the only tool I have left that hasn't been turned into a weapon against me. I have to believe the filing process still works. I have to believe there is enough structural integrity left in the Tribunal to withstand a Contract Lord’s corruption.

I reach for the cracked clay cup on my desk and swallow a mouthful of cold, bitter tea. The astringent taste barely registers. I am running entirely on sheer, unfiltered panic. 

I gather the seventy-four pages. The parchment is heavy, the weight of a man's life held together with a simple brass binding clip. I stuff the thick stack into my reinforced leather Scribe’s satchel, buckle the flap, and throw the strap over my shoulder. 

I grab my gray woolen outer robe from the hook behind the door. The tear over the heart—the spot I ruined stepping in front of Cyran this morning—is glaringly obvious, but I don't care. I throw it on, smoothing out the lapels with shaking hands.

Four minutes.

I grip the heavy iron handle of my office door, pull it open, and step out into the hallway. 

And stop dead.

He doesn't look angry. He doesn't look vindictive. Inquisitor Dren looks like a man doing routine municipal pest control.

Dren stands directly in the center of the corridor, his massive frame blocking the only path to the elevator shafts. He wears the polished black-and-silver heavy armor of the Tribunal elite. His face is heavily scarred, his eyes as flat and gray as a slate chalkboard. 

He is not alone. Four heavily armed Tribunal Enforcers flank him, halberds resting at severe, uniform angles across their breastplates.

"Inquisitor," I say. My voice sounds obnoxiously calm, entirely detached from the sudden, terrifying free-fall of my stomach. "You are blocking the corridor. I am en route to the Registry to file an emergency appeal before sunset."

"Your filing privileges have been revoked, Scribe Lysa," Dren says. His voice is a bored, bureaucratic monotone. 

"On whose authority?" I demand, my hand tightening on the strap of my satchel. "The Scribe's Charter clearly states—"

"I am not interested in the Charter," Dren interrupts. He reaches into a perfectly uncreased leather pouch at his belt and withdraws a single sheet of stark white parchment, sealed with the heavy, dark red wax of a Contract Lord. "I am executing a warrant. Signed and authorized by Lord Thessen."

The hallway, usually bustling with scribes, is entirely empty except for us. I notice, with a rising sense of horror, that the doors to the neighboring offices are all closed. 

"Read the charges," I say, lifting my chin. I refuse to shake in front of him. 

Dren unfolds the parchment leisurely. 

"Lysa of the Spine," he reads, his voice echoing off the curved bone walls. "You are hereby charged with four counts of high systemic corruption. One: Wilful forgery and manipulation of official audit documents. Two: Heresy against the Prime Contract. Three: Illicit fraternization with a restricted Vigil asset. Four: Actively compromising said asset, endangering the security of the Ribcage settlement."

"Thessen is lying," I snap. "I have the proof in my satchel right now. Varos is building a Heart-Node. If you let Thessen execute this warrant, you are handing the city over to a rogue priest."

Dren folds the parchment carefully and tucks it back into his pouch. "The Tribunal is not concerned with your romance novel theories, Scribe. By the authority of the Contract Lords, you are stripped of your rank, your privileges, and your freedom."

"I demand my right to legal counsel under Section Two of the—"

Dren steps forward. His movement is brutally fast, entirely at odds with his bureaucratic demeanor. 

He invades my personal space, his armored chest practically touching mine. His heavy, gauntleted hand shoots out and grabs the silver Tribunal badge pinned to the lapel of my robe. 

He doesn't unpin it. He rips it off.

The thick gray wool tears with a sickening, audible rip. The sheer violence of the gesture is shocking. It is a symbolic defrocking, executed with the casual brutality of a slaughterhouse worker.

"Your rights ended when you knelt in front of that monster this morning," Dren says quietly, tossing my silver badge onto the floor. It clatters loudly against the bone-white tiles. 

He grabs the leather strap of my satchel and yanks it violently over my head. The brass buckle bites painfully into my neck as the burden—seventy-four pages of Cyran's salvation—is torn from me. 

"Secure the prisoner," Dren barks.

Two Enforcers step out from behind him. They grab my arms, their gauntlets digging mercilessly into my biceps. I am yanked forward. I don't struggle—there is no point, it would only give them an excuse to break my arms—but I keep my spine completely rigid. I force my legs to lock, making them drag my weight slightly to move me.

They twist my arms painfully behind my back. The cold, heavy iron of Tribunal manacles is slapped onto my wrists. They haven't been sanded or oiled. The rough, jagged edges immediately bite into the delicate skin over my pulse points. The lock clicks with a sound exactly like the sunset bell.

"March," Dren orders.

They force me forward. Dren takes the lead, my two guards flanking me, the other two bringing up the rear. We walk down the main corridor toward the central shafts. 

Now, the doors to the other offices begin to crack open.

I see the junior scribes peering out of the narrow gaps. I see the horror on their faces. They are looking at me—a woman they shared tea with yesterday—as if I am a ghost. I see Scribe Meris standing in her doorway. Her hands are clamped over her mouth. She is weeping silently.

I lock eyes with her as I am marched past. I don't plead for help. I don't shake my head. I hold my chin high, squaring my shoulders against the guards' grip. I want her to know that I am not broken, even as they break me.

We reach the central elevator shafts—massive, hollowed-out spinal cords that drop straight down into the rotting lower vertebrae of the dead god. The heavy iron cage of the lift is waiting. 

They shove me inside. Dren steps in last, slamming the grid shut. He pulls the heavy release lever.

The descent is terrifyingly fast. The air temperature plunges, the sterile, chalky smell of the upper offices immediately giving way to the cloying, suffocating stench of mildew, old blood, and damp stone. We are dropping out of the light of the city, straight into the dark underneath it. 

The elevator grinds to a halt with a teeth-rattling judder. 

The doors open to the Spine Dungeons.

It is a brutalist nightmare. The walls here aren't polished white bone; they are jagged, porous, and dripping with condensation that glows with a sickly, bruised green bioluminescence. The air is so thick it feels like breathing water. 

They march me out of the lift and down a long, narrow corridor lined with solid iron doors. Most of them are silent, but from a few, low, feral growls echo into the damp space. These aren't cells for political prisoners. These are holding pens for the monsters the Vigil catches before they are executed. 

We stop before a cell near the end of the block. The guard unlocks the heavy deadbolt.

"Inside," Dren says. 

When I don't move fast enough, the guard shoves me violently between the shoulder blades. I stagger forward, my manacled hands unable to balance me. I trip over the lip of the stone floor and hit the grimy, wet stone hard on my shoulder and hip. 

I roll over, gasping as the rough iron of the cuffs tears the skin on my wrists. 

The heavy, iron-reinforced door slams shut. The lock echoes like a gunshot.

I push myself up into a sitting position on the freezing floor. The cell is no larger than a closet. There is no bed, no bucket, no window. Just the damp stone and the small, barred grate at eye level in the center of the heavy door.

Dren’s face appears in the grate, backlit by the sickly green light of the corridor. 

"Thessen sends his regards," the Inquisitor says softly. "The monster goes back to the temple tomorrow. If he serves Varos well, maybe the High Priest will convince the Tribunal to execute you quickly instead of letting you rot down here."

"I hope you’re standing close to him when Cyran finds out," I say, my voice vibrating with a pure, cold fury that surprises even me.

Dren doesn't reply. He stares at me for a long, flat second, then turns and walks away. His heavy boots echo down the corridor until the sound fades, swallowed by the drip of water and the distant, miserable noises of the deep dungeons. 

I am alone in the dark. 

I pull my knees up to my chest, my ruined wrists throbbing, the cold creeping into my bones. The Scribe Lysa is legally dead. The system I devoted my life to was just a beautifully disguised slaughterhouse, and it finally realized I wasn't wearing the butcher's apron anymore. 

I close my eyes, and for the first time since Cyran walked into my office a month ago, I have absolutely no idea what to do next.
