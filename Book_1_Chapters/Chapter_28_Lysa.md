# Chapter 28 — *The Note on the Pillow*
### Lysa | POV

The bed is cold, and the silence in the room feels like a physical assault.

I know he is gone before I even open my eyes. There is a specific gravity to Cyran’s presence—a dense, vibrating tension that fills whatever space he occupies. When he is near, the air feels charged, heavy with the quiet threat of violence and the sudden, startling warmth of his attention. 

The air in my quarters this morning is just air. Thin, sterile, and entirely empty. 

I open my eyes. 

The pale, gray light of early morning filters through the narrow window, casting long shadows across the wooden floorboards. I roll over slowly, my joints stiff and aching. The serum Dr. Marrow forced on me in the dungeon has finally worn off, leaving behind a profound, hollow exhaustion that makes my skull throb and my limbs feel like they are filled with wet sand.

The space beside me on the mattress is perfectly empty. The sheets aren't even wrinkled. 

But there is a small, folded square of parchment resting precisely on the dead center of the opposite pillow.

My breath catches in my throat. I don't reach for it immediately. I stare at it, the stark white paper acting like a magnet for my mounting dread. 

*Cyran traded his life for yours.* Marrow’s words, spoken in the frigid green dark of the cell, echo in my ears. 

Last night, when Cyran appeared at my door, I had desperately, foolishly allowed myself to believe the lie. I had let his touch silence the doctor’s warning. I had felt the overwhelming, blinding wave of adoration through the empathic link when he kissed me, and I had used it as proof that we had somehow survived the Tribunal's trap intact. 

I reach out. My hand is shaking so badly my knuckles brush the linen case before my fingers find the parchment. 

I unfold it. 

The writing is executed in an impeccably neat, terrifyingly steady script. The ink isn't ink. It is a dried, rust-red smear. He wrote it in his own blood.

*I won't be the thing that destroys you. Forgive me.*

Twenty-four words. It seems entirely inadequate for ending a world.

I drop the paper. It flutters to the quilt, landing facedown. 

I scramble out of the bed, my bare feet hitting the freezing floorboards. I don't call his name. I know he won't answer, but I check the small parlor anyway. I check the narrow hallway outside my door. Empty. The heavy silence of the Spine District morning mocks my panic.

I catch sight of my reflection in the small, cracked mirror above the washstand.

My hair is a tangled, chaotic mess. There are dark smudges under my eyes. But it is my wrists that hold my attention. The violent, mottled purple bruises from the iron manacles are still there. I remember Cyran kneeling before me last night, a basin of water in his hands, carefully, reverently washing the dirt and blood away without ever pressing hard enough to hurt the bruised flesh.

He wasn't washing me. He was performing a ritual. He was saying goodbye. 

The realization hits me like a physical blow. When he touched me, when he fed on my neck and gave me that beautiful, terrifying sense of safety through the link—he had known he was going to walk out that door. He had built a wall between us while he was inside me, hiding his terror so I would sleep peacefully while he marched back to his abuser.

A sound tears its way out of my throat. It isn't a sob. It is a ragged, serrated gasp of pure fury.

I turn away from the mirror. I move mechanically, operating on pure, destructive momentum. I ignore the ruined gray Tribunal robes pooled on the floor where I dropped them last night. Scribe Lysa is legally dead, and I am not going to wear her shroud. 

I pull open my cedar chest and dig past the neat stacks of official linen. I pull out my oldest civilian clothes—dark canvas trousers, a heavy black wool sweater, a thick leather belt. I dress in sharp, jerky movements, strapping my boots on tight.

I don't pause to run a comb through my hair. I grab my leather satchel—empty now of the seventy-four pages I had written to defend him—and walk out the door. 

I leave the Spine District without looking back. I don't glance at the towering white arches of the Tribunal Halls or the sweeping staircases of the legal courts. They are just monuments to a corrupt machine that used the law to enslave the man I love.

I walk aimlessly down into the Ribcage. The morning is heavily overcast, the sky a bruised, bruised purple. The bone-dust thrown up by the early merchant carts hangs thick in the air like fog, coating my tongue with chalk. I don't know where I am going. I just know I cannot stop moving, because if I stop, the sheer magnitude of what Cyran has done will crush me.

I end up in the Vein Markets.

It is the neutral ground of the city—a chaotic, sprawling labyrinth of tents, stalls, and illicit trade where the Tribunal's jurisdiction frays into mere suggestion. 

I stop in front of a low, squat building made of scorched dark wood and heavy iron reinforcement. A rusted sign hangs over the cracked door: *The Hollow King's Tavern.*

I push the door open. 

The tavern is mostly empty at seven in the morning. The air smells aggressively of cheap engine-grade liquor, stale smoke, and old violence. A few patrons are slumped in the dark booths, sleeping off the night before.

Behind the heavy, varnish-peeling wooden bar, Cairn is wiping down the counter with a dirty rag. 

He stops scrubbing when he hears the door close. He looks up. 

Cairn’s dark eyes track my entry, taking in the civilian clothes, the bruised wrists I didn't bother to cover, and the absolutely dead expression on my face. He doesn't say a word. He doesn't offer a greeting. He simply reaches under the counter, pulls out a short, heavy glass, and a bottle of unlabeled amber liquid that looks corrosive enough to strip paint. 

He pours two fingers of the whiskey and slides it across the sticky bar as I sit heavily on a rickety stool.

I pick up the glass and down the liquor in one smooth, burning swallow. I don't cough. The fire in my throat is a welcome distraction from the ice in my chest. 

I slam the glass down.

"He went back," I say. My voice is dangerously flat. It doesn't shake. 

Cairn picks up his rag and begins wiping the counter again, his broad shoulders impassive. "The broadsheets yesterday were screaming that Varos had leverage on him. I figured you were the leverage."

"He traded himself," I say, my fingers curling into fists on the bar. The rage I felt in my room is crystallizing now, hardening into something sharp and lethal. "Thessen arrested me. Varos offered the pardon in exchange for Cyran returning to the temple to act as a ritual anchor. He took the deal."

I reach out and grab a thick cardboard coaster resting near my empty glass. I begin tearing it. I don't even realize I'm doing it. 

"He thinks his pain is the only currency he has," I snap, my voice finally rising, fracturing entirely. I rip a chunk off the coaster, dropping it onto the bar. "He thinks he's a monster who ruins everything he touches, so he decided the only way to love me was to martyr himself by returning to the man who spent centuries torturing him. He made the choice for me, Cairn. He didn't even give me the chance to fight."

Cairn stops wiping the bar. He sighs, a heavy, tired sound perfectly suited to a man who has lived entirely too long surrounded by idiots. He picks up the unlabeled bottle and pours another two fingers into my glass.

He leans his massive weight onto his forearms, bringing his face closer to mine. 

"Cyran is an idiot," Cairn says, his voice a low, gravelly rumble that drops the usual grumpy apathy. "He’s an idiot who thinks dying for you is easier than living for you. He’d rather take a knife directly to the chest than watch you catch a splinter. It’s selfish, and it’s pathetic, and it is exactly how survival instincts work when they've been terminally compromised."

I stare at the torn shreds of the coaster. "What am I supposed to do with a sacrifice I didn't want?" 

Cairn looks at me steadily. The dark circles under his eyes seem particularly pronounced in the dim tavern light. 

"Scribe," Cairn says quietly. "When someone walks away to save you, the question isn't whether they mean it. The question is whether you are going to let them."

The words settle over me like another shot of the engine-grade whiskey. 

The profound, suffocating grief inside me abruptly solidifies. It stops being a weight dragging me down, and it becomes the fuel for a furnace.

Cyran believed his sacrifice was the end of the story. He believed the Tribunal and the High Priests were monoliths of power that could not be beaten, only appeased. He believed the rules mattered.

But Dr. Marrow had proven last night that the rules didn't matter. Marrow had walked into the highest security dungeon in the city and pulled me out with a skeleton key and a forged signature. 

I cannot use the law to save Cyran. The law is what enslaved him in the first place.

I have to break the rules.

Movement in the periphery of my vision catches my eye.

Walking out of the back gambling parlor of the tavern is Seraph. The extravagant, glittering performer looks utterly strung out. His velvet coat is rumpled, the gold embroidery stained with cheap wine. But what holds my attention, and violently stops my heart, is the thick, rune-carved iron collar locked securely around his neck. 

I look at Seraph's collar, and I see Cyran walking into the Blood Cisterns. I see the heavy iron of the Prime Contract snapping around his throat. I see the light in his silver eyes dying forever as Varos pulls the psychological chains tight. 

I stand up. The stool scrapes harshly against the floorboards. 

I reach into my pocket, pull out a silver coin, and drop it onto the bar next to the torn shreds of the coaster. 

My posture has completely changed. I am no longer slumped. My spine is rigid, my shoulders squared. I am the woman who stepped between a Contract Lord and his victim, but I am entirely done playing by the Tribunal's polite fictions. 

"Where are you going?" Cairn asks, picking up the coin. 

"I'm going to the Archives," I say. My voice is ice-cold. "I am going to find Senior Scribe Solen. And then I am going to tear the Heart-Node out of the Cisterns with my bare hands."

I turn and walk out the door. The grief is gone, entirely replaced by the absolute, militant certainty of war.
