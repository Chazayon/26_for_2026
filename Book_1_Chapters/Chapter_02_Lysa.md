# Chapter 2 — *The Audit of the Blood Relic*
### Lysa | POV

I have audited three hundred monsters, and every single one of them claimed to be innocent.

The Spine District smells of ink and judgment, and today, judgment is loud.

I'm standing in the observation gallery above the main hall when it happens—the moment the Magistrate raises his rod. Below me, the ghoul-kin drops to its knees before the whole assembled floor of scribes and clerks and minor officials who have nothing better to do than watch. The hall is vast, ribbed with pale vertebra-stone that curves upward into cathedral arches, and the sound of the creature's wail fills every hollow of it. It bounces off the bone and comes back wrong, smaller somehow, more frightened. I keep my face neutral. I adjust my spectacles.

The sigil burns green.

I write in my ledger: *Procedure 77-B administered. Subject distress: High. Duration: twelve seconds. Mark successfully revoked.*

My quill scratches the parchment and I focus on the scratch, on the precise drag of the nib, on the fact that this is my job—recording what happens, not what I feel about it. Below, the ghoul-kin is hunched over something that used to be its arm, cradling the space where the merit-mark lived. The skin there is blackened, crater-smooth. It isn't bleeding. Merit Marks don't bleed when they go. They just—unmake. That's somehow worse.

I touch the inside of my left wrist through my sleeve. The Tribunal mark sits there, smaller than theirs, inked rather than carved. Different. I tell myself it's different.

I write: *No procedural irregularities noted.*

Three rows of benches below the gallery, the assembled observers shift and mutter. A pair of clerks in junior grey robes are whispering behind their hands. Someone actually laughs—a short, startled thing, quickly swallowed. I look down at them and they don't see me looking and that is, I think, precisely the problem with this place.

The shoulder-tap comes just as the Magistrate begins his closing statement.

"Investigator Voss wants you."

I close my ledger with a clean snap.

***

The walk through the halls of the Spine District is never short, and today it feels longer. I fall into step behind the clerk—a boy barely old enough to be carrying messages—and I note, in the half-attending way that's become habit, the state of things. A cracked tile near the east corridor junction, the third this month. Bone-walls developing stress fractures in the upper archive wing, grey-green with damp. The infrastructure of the Tribunal is failing in increments that the annual maintenance reports describe, cheerfully, as *minor superficial wear.* I know better. I've read those reports three times.

I've written notes in the margins. No one has asked me about the notes.

The clerks' room is full when I pass—the scratch of forty quills, the soft pneumatic *whoosh-and-thunk* of the message tubes moving overhead like mechanical veins. The smell of iron-gall ink and cold tallow candles has been the backdrop of my working life for six years. I barely notice it anymore. What I notice instead is the half-eaten apple someone has balanced on top of the Relic-case file cart—red and browning at the bite, cheerfully oblivious to the weight of what it's resting on—and I'm angry about that in a way I cannot entirely explain. I straighten a stack of leaning case files as I pass. Small corrections. The only kind available to me.

Voss's office is on the upper floor of the seventh vertebra-tower, which means it has a view. That's intentional. Everything about High Inquisitor Voss is intentional.

The boy knocks, opens the door without being invited to, and leaves me standing on the threshold while Voss finishes something at his mahogany desk. The desk is polished to a shine that feels aggressive in this building—everything else is dusty and practical, and then there's *this*, sitting in a pool of grey light filtered through tall bone-windows. On the credenza behind him, an oil lamp and two volumes of the Contract Code and a pot of expensive tea steeping in a cosy. The tea smells like bergamot. It smells like money.

He doesn't look up.

I wait. I clean my spectacles with the cloth I keep in my front pocket. I wait some more.

"Investigator," he says, finally, and slides a file across the desk.

It lands near the edge. Red leather binding. Heavy. I step forward and pick it up and my hands register the weight before my mind does—this is a full-cycle audit file, not a summary, not a preliminary. Someone has been building this for a while.

The name stamped on the spine reads: *CYRAN. DESIGNATION: BLOOD RELIC. VIGIL ASSIGNMENT: RIBCAGE DISTRICT.*

"Sit," Voss says, still not looking at me.

I sit. I put the file on my knees. I do not open it yet.

"There have been complaints," he says. He folds his hands on the desk—small, careful hands, ink-free, which tells me everything I need to know about what kind of work Voss actually does. "Unofficially, at this stage. Dead civilians, three confirmed, two unconfirmed. Missing time during patrol rotations. Reports of *unbecoming conduct* from contract holders in his quarter."

"Unbecoming conduct," I repeat. "That could mean any of fourteen things under the current code."

"Yes," he says. "That is why we need an auditor."

I open the file.

The first two pages are standard intake—classification, original contract date, issuing authority. Thessen's seal is at the bottom of the issuance page, wax the colour of old blood, the sigil pressed so deep it's nearly worn through the parchment. I look at it for a moment longer than I need to. Then I turn to page three, and I find the first redaction—a thick bar of black ink running through a full paragraph under the heading *CREATION CIRCUMSTANCES: SEE APPENDED RITES DOCUMENTATION.* There is no appended documentation.

Page five has another one. Page eight has three.

Between the redactions, the language is extraordinary in its emptiness. *Temple rites, performed per standard protocol. Subject compliance: satisfactory. Disposition during assessment: amenable.* Amenable. I glance up at Voss, who is watching me now with the particular attentiveness of a man who wants to see what I'll do with information before he decides whether to give me more of it.

I keep my face neutral. I turn to the Merit Mark summary.

"Five thousand marks," I say. "That's significant."

"He's been active for—"

"Decades. Yes, I see that." The date of first service is nearly thirty years ago. The date of expected personhood threshold has been recalculated four times, each time pushing the goalposts further out. I read that entry twice to make sure I'm reading it correctly. I am. "He's been recalculated."

"Threshold adjustments are within the Tribunal's discretionary authority," Voss says.

I write: *Threshold recalculated x4. Review basis: unstated.* My quill presses harder than it needs to, and I ease off before I tear the paper. "You want me to audit the city's poster boy."

"We want you to conduct a thorough, impartial assessment of a Vigil protector whose conduct has come into question. Yes." He lifts his tea and takes a precise, controlled sip and sets it back. "With dispatch. The donor community is watching this closely. We want it resolved quickly and quietly before—"

"Before donor relations sour," I finish.

He looks at me with the flat patience of a man who expected this and has already decided it doesn't matter. "Before the situation escalates."

There's a difference. I don't say that out loud. I write it in smaller letters at the bottom of my page instead, pressed so soft no one but me would read it. I flip to the section marked *COMPLAINTS: ORIGINATING PARTIES* and find, under the first grievance filed, another seal. Thessen's. Again. A different seal this time, not the creation-authority, not institutional—this is personal. A Contract Lord filing a personal complaint against his own asset.

Something cold and alert wakes up behind my sternum. I've seen this pattern before, buried in old case files from three cycles back, cases that quietly closed without resolution. I recognized the shape of it the way you recognize a word in a language you've only half-learned—not perfectly, not fluently, but well enough to know what it means.

Someone wants something from this audit. And it isn't the truth.

"I'll be thorough," I say.

"Be quick," Voss corrects, with the gentleness of someone who does not believe those two things are in conflict, which tells me exactly how thoroughly he's actually read this file.

I stand. I button my satchel. I take the file.

***

The corridor outside his office is empty in the way that institutional corridors are empty mid-morning, full of ambient noise but devoid of people actually *going* anywhere—just the background hum of a bureaucracy digesting itself. I walk to the junction and stop and lean against the bone-wall and open the file to the Merit Mark section again, because I want to read the recalculation notes without Voss watching my face.

My head still aches. The ozone-smell from the hall's punishment sigils has settled behind my eyes the way it always does, a metallic bite at the back of my sinuses that no amount of tea will shift. I press my thumb and forefinger to the bridge of my nose, under my glasses, and breathe.

Down the main stairs, two floor-wardens are guiding the ghoul-kin toward the administrative exit. It's not struggling anymore. It doesn't have the energy for it. Its arm hangs at an angle that isn't quite right, and one of the wardens is holding it by the elbow—not unkindly, just efficiently, the way you hold something fragile that you'd rather not break further before you've finished with it.

I watch until they're gone.

Then I look down at the name on the red leather file.

*Cyran. Blood Relic.*

The title sounds like something from a cautionary tale—the kind told in districts that don't have reliable warden coverage. A name built to frighten people into compliance. A name built to make whatever's underneath it invisible.

*Don't be a monster,* I think, looking at it. *For your sake. Not mine.*

Because if he is—if this is straightforward, if the deaths are real and the gaps in patrol are what they look like on paper—I will write it down exactly as it is, and the Tribunal will do what the Tribunal does, and that will be that. Clean. Procedurally sound. I will sleep fine.

It's the alternative that I'm already bracing for. The alternative where the file is redacted for a reason, and the reason is Thessen, and Thessen is pulling Voss's strings the way he pulled the strings of the three case-managers before me who apparently couldn't find anything worth writing down. The alternative where "quick and quiet" means *convenient verdict.*

That's the alternative where my job becomes something I don't want to think about too closely at nine in the morning on a grey autumn day in a building that's slowly cracking under its own weight.

I tuck the file under my arm and start walking toward my desk.

***

Kess is waiting for me. She isn't technically waiting—she has a reference ledger open on the corner of my desk, which is her preferred method of loitering, and she looks up with the particular bright-eyed alertness of someone who has already heard more than is professionally appropriate.

"Is it the Blood Relic case?" she asks, before I've set down my satchel.

I put the file on my desk and open my satchel with my back to her. "I need the C-series contract index for the ribcage district. The current quarter's."

"I heard Voss called you up." She edges closer. "They say he's killed thirty god-spawn this quarter. *Thirty.* How does a Relic even count that high? They barely—"

"The C-series index, Kess."

"Right, right." She doesn't move. "Did you know someone already requested his full creation file? Hasn't been opened since Thessen first commissioned him, apparently. Decades ago. And someone *pulled it* last week." She says this with the specific hushed energy of a person who considers this information thrilling rather than alarming, which is the primary way I can tell we approach this work differently. "The Vigil brass are already watching to see who gets assigned. Everyone says if he goes feral, it'll be—I mean, at least he's *pretty* to look at, right? If you're going to be assigned a—"

"That will be all, Kess."

My voice comes out quieter than I intend, which she mistakes for dismissal and I mean as warning. She blinks. "I was only—"

"I know." I turn around and look at her, and I make my voice the careful professional one that doesn't indicate anger because I am not angry at her, precisely, only at the fact that this is how we talk about them, as a matter of course, in the Tribunal of all places. *Pretty to look at. If he goes feral.* As though those are the only two things worth noting about a person who has been alive for thirty years and done every dangerous and degrading thing this institution asked of him without—apparently—anyone ever asking whether he wanted to. "Get the index. Please."

She goes.

I sit down at my desk. I light my oil lamp—the flame dims immediately in the draft from the corridor, steadies, dims again—and I open the file to the first un-redacted page and smooth it flat with my palm. The wax of my official seal is still warm in my stamp-case from this morning's intake work, and I find that oddly grounding. The smell of it. Something real.

*Subject has demonstrated consistent merit accumulation over audit period,* the file reads. *Subject responsiveness to instruction: satisfactory. Capacity for independent function: limited. Recommended disposition pending threshold review.*

I read that last line three times.

*Capacity for independent function: limited.* Someone wrote that. Someone who either never met him or met him and decided it was useful to write that. I don't know which is worse.

I pick up my quill and at the top of my fresh audit sheet I write, in my best, most formal hand: *Audit of Blood Relic Cyran. Investigator: L. Vaskin. Preliminary note: Existing file contains significant redactions. Complaint origination: Contract Lord Thessen. Possible institutional conflict of interest. Full independent review required.*

I underline *independent.*

Then I write: *Subject believed to be currently active, Ribcage District. First observation scheduled tomorrow morning.*

I blot the page. I sit back.

The tribunal clock in the main tower begins to strike the hour, ten measured tones that roll through the bones of the building and settle in my chest like stones. Outside Voss's window, somewhere above the grey smear of the Ribcage District's skyline, Cyran is out there doing whatever it is the file describes as *patrol activity* in language so sanitized I can't actually tell if he's protecting people or hunting them. The file doesn't know. The file was written not to know.

That's what I need to fix.

I close my quill case with a small, decisive snap. I gather my papers into the satchel with the precise, unhurried movements of someone who has decided something, and I stand, and I smooth the front of my indigo robes, and I look at the file one last time—red leather, red ink, a life reduced to pages that someone has very carefully made unreadable.

*Cyran,* I think. *Blood Relic. Vigil asset. Poster boy.*

*Tell me which one of those is true.*

Tomorrow morning, I'll find out.
