# Chapter 17 — *The Breath Between*
### Cyran | POV

I know exactly how to seduce a mark, but standing in the middle of her cramped, ink‑stained room, I have absolutely no idea how to touch a woman I love.  

The word she just gave me—yes—hangs between us like a blade catching marrow‑light. Terrifying. Brilliant. Capable of cutting either of us open if I mishandle it.  

The rain drums against the bone‑glass window, a relentless, hollow sound that makes this small room feel even more like a ship in a storm. The hearth has slumped to embers. The table is a low hill of open ledgers and spent quills. We are on the far side of midnight, somewhere in that thin, strange hour where the city feels half‑dead and half‑dangerous.  

I stand with my hand still resting on the door latch, as if my body hasn’t received the message that it is, in fact, allowed to stay.

“Cyran,” Lysa says softly.

My name in her mouth is an anchor and a question and a dare.

“Yes,” I manage.

She watches me for a beat, dark eyes sharp behind fogged spectacles, reading the minute twitches of my face the way she reads fine print. I wonder what she sees—predator, coward, ornament pressed up against a choice he doesn’t know how to hold.

Then, with that infuriating, miraculous practicality that is so specifically her, she moves.

“First,” she says, “we do this.”

She crosses the room in three precise steps and slides the iron bolt on the door home with a solid, final clack.  

The sound goes through me like a nerve.

Locked in.

Except it doesn’t feel like a lock the way temple doors did, weighted with iron runes and command phrases. It sounds like a boundary. Like a shield. Like one quiet, defiant little line between us and the Tribunal.

Lysa touches the bolt a second longer than necessary, fingertips resting on cold metal, as if she’s blessing it.

“There,” she says. “Now they can knock all they like.”

“Reassuring,” I murmur. My voice is too rough. “Do they often?”

Her mouth crooks, a fleeting, sideways thing.

“You’ve heard Olan,” she says.

As if summoned by his own reputation, a sharp rap rattles the other side of the door.

She closes her eyes briefly.

Of course.

“Voss!” a nasal voice bleats through the wood. “Some of us have to transcribe magistrate drivel at dawn. Keep it down or take your arguments back to the Sanctum.”  

Heat crawls up the back of my neck.

I bite the inside of my cheek to stop a hysterical laugh.

Lysa’s expression goes very flat in that way I’ve learned means she is about to commit bureaucratic homicide.

She cracks the door an inch, keeping her body angled to block the view of the room.

“My apologies, Olan,” she says, voice all weary professionalism. “I was practicing a closing argument. I am, in fact, going to bed now.”  

“Well, do it quieter,” he grumbles. “Ink’s expensive. I can’t afford to waste any blotting your theatrics.”

The shuffle of retreating footsteps. A door slam. Silence.

Lysa bolts the door again, firmer this time.

She turns back to me.

“See?” she says dryly. “They’re always listening. Better to give them something boring to hear.”

“You lied for me,” I say.

It’s a stupid thing to focus on, given the ledgers stacked on her table that amount to professional arson. But the casualness of it knots my chest.

“I protected my privacy,” she corrects. “It just happens to include you at the moment.”

The at the moment shouldn’t make something bright and aching unfurl in me.

It does.

She moves to the table, picks up the clay cup we used earlier to drink water between sips of bitter sage tea. It’s chipped at the rim, worn smooth where countless fingers have gripped it. Utterly mundane. No sigils, no gold leaf. Just a thing that holds what it’s meant to.  

She pours fresh water from the jug, then holds it out.

“Drink,” she says. “You’re shaking.”

I look down.

She’s right.

My hand is a tremor where it hangs at my side.

Battlefield nerves, temple flashbacks, adrenaline crash from defying two of the most powerful men in the city—it all stacks, a tower of reasons that do nothing to steady me.

I take the cup.

Our fingers brush.

The contact is brief, incidental, and it still sends a line of heat up my arm.

I drink.

The water is cool and clean, cutting the lingering taste of sage and ink from my mouth. No copper. No spice. No hidden sedatives. Just water.

I hand the cup back.

She sets it down with quiet care.

The silence that follows isn’t awkward.

It’s heavy. Charged.

The room is very small. The air is warm from the hearth, cool from the window, thick from everything we have said and done in the last two days.

She takes off her spectacles and sets them on the table beside the ledgers.

Without the glint of glass, her eyes are softer and somehow more dangerous. Less framed, more… present.

“I don’t know how to do this,” I say.

It bursts out of me before I can rehearse it, scrub it of its edges, turn it into something pretty.

Good.

She deserves the ugly version.

Lysa studies me for a long second.

“I don’t either,” she says. “But I’d rather learn with you than with anyone else.”

The word learn coming from her mouth is always potent. Turned on me, here, now, it lands like a vow.

My throat is dry again already.

“Lysa,” I say.

Her name feels like a question, an apology, a prayer.

She steps closer.

We meet in the cramped, cluttered center of the room, between table and bed, fire and door. There is no space here for distance. No room for theatrics. If I gesture too wide, I’ll knock a book off a shelf.

Maybe that’s the point.

She tilts her head back to look up at me.

The lamplight makes small amber flecks in her dark irises, catches on the smudge of ink along her jawline she must have missed when she scrubbed her hands clean.

It would be the easiest thing in the world to close the gap the way I have a thousand times: a hand at her hip, a smile, a line so smooth it feels like silk.

I keep my hands at my sides.

My chest is tight with the effort.

“May I?” I ask.

The question is so small, so quiet, I’m not entirely sure it reaches her.

But her breath catches.

Her pupils flare.

“Yes,” she says.

Not a breathy, lacquered yes the way donors used to give me, all performance and pretense. This is flat. Certain. Solid as a floor.

Yes.

Invitation, not capitulation.

Something in my knees goes weak.

Slowly—so slowly I irritate myself—I lift a hand.

I let her see it approach.

Let her see the tremor in my fingers.

Let her see that I am not leaning on old scripts, that there is no invisible hand on the back of my neck pushing me forward.

My knuckles graze her jaw first, rough leather catching on the fine, damp hairs there. I trace the line of her cheekbone with my thumb, light as if she’s made of spun glass and Tribunal secrets.

She leans into it.

A tiny, involuntary press.

Gods.

My other hand comes up of its own accord, cupping her face between my palms. Her skin is warm against my cold fingers, almost feverish. Her mouth is parted on a small, uncertain inhale.

I bend.

The first brush of her lips is almost nothing.

A contact of temperature and texture, the barest press. Dry, cool, still.

Then she exhales against my mouth, a soft rush of breath that tastes of sage and exhaustion and something sweeter underneath—a note that might be want, might be trust.

It undoes me.

I press in, deeper, careful but not tentative anymore.

Her lips are softer than I’d let myself imagine. Not the painted, perfumed mouths I’ve kissed in temple galleries and Vigil salons. Human. Warm. A little chapped from the marrow‑wind. When she responds—when she actually moves against me, tilts her head, meets me instead of merely allowing—the shock of it is so profound I shudder.  

Her hands come up, not to clutch or to drag, but to bracket my ribs through the linen of my shirt. The heat of her palms seeps through the thin fabric, branding.

My body remembers a thousand other kisses.

Command phrases in my ear. Donors humming approval. Varos’s hand on my jaw, tilting my head just so for the best view.

I let those memories unfurl and drift away like smoke over the city, useless and thin.

This is not that.

This is Lysa, in a too‑small room, ink on her fingers, paper dust under her nails, pouring everything she is into the simple act of kissing me back.

Want, not need.

Choice, not debt.

I make a sound into her mouth.

It’s embarrassingly raw. Nothing like the practiced, elegant noises I have perfected over centuries to sound appropriately undone at the precise right moment.

She doesn’t flinch.

Her fingers flex against my ribs. She rises onto her toes, chasing my mouth as if she’s afraid I’ll disappear if she doesn’t.

My back hits the edge of the table.

A quill rolls, clatters to the floor. A ledger flops half‑closed.

We break apart on a shared, breathless laugh.

“Well,” I say hoarsely. “There goes the evidence.”

She’s flushed, cheeks pink, eyes bright.

“I have copies,” she murmurs.

Of course she does.

I rest my forehead lightly against hers.

The urge to keep going, to press her back against the wall or drag her down onto the bed and lose myself in the familiar choreography, rushes up like a wave.

I don’t ride it.

I let it crash and recede.

“Tell me if you want me to stop,” I say.

Her breath ghosts over my lips.

“I will,” she says. “You, too.”

“Me?” I echo, startled.

“Yes.” Her hands slide up, over my shoulders, to rest at the base of my neck, thumbs brushing the edge of my jaw. “If you dissociate. If you feel yourself slipping. Say so. Or squeeze my hand. Or—something. We stop. Immediately.”  

A frisson of fear snakes down my spine.

“And if I don’t notice?” I ask. My voice is very small suddenly. “If I go away the way I used to and don’t come back in time?”

“I will,” she says again, more firmly. “I’ll notice. I’ll call you back. And if you don’t come back, we stop.”  

They never stopped, in the temple.

Not for flinching. Not for screaming. Not for silence.

The idea that I could have that power now—that I could end this at any moment and she would accept it without argument—is dizzying.

Dangerous, some feral part of me whispers. Addiction wrapped in mercy.

I kiss her again instead of answering, because my voice is too unreliable and my body has already cast its vote.

We stumble toward the bed.

Stumble is the correct word.

There is nothing smooth about this.

The narrow thing is shoved under the window, its wool blanket rough and thin, its frame creaking in protest even before we reach it.  

I sit on the edge first, drawing her between my knees.

The height difference puts us at a better angle; I don’t have to fold myself in half to kiss her now. Her hands find my shoulders again, my hair, the back of my neck.

When her fingers slip under the edge of my collar, brushing the skin just above an old, faded sigil, my entire body jolts.

Not with arousal.

With fear.

It’s so fast and violent I jerk away, breath punching out of me.

“Sorry,” I spit, hands flying up between us in a placating gesture I hate. “I—I’m sorry, I just—”

“Hey,” she says, immediately releasing me, palms open and visible. “Hey. Look at me.”

I do.

Her pupils are wide. Her own chest is heaving. But her hands are steady as she holds them up.

“We stop if you want,” she says. “Right now. No questions asked. We can go back to arguing about Clause Seven, if that feels safer. Or I can sleep on the floor and you take the bed. Or we can sit by the fire and stare at the wall until dawn. Your choice.”  

No one has ever offered me that many variations of no.

It is, perversely, the thing that tips me into yes.

“I don’t want to stop,” I say.

It comes out hoarse, threaded with something like panic.

I drag in a breath, force the words to steady.

“I just—” I gesture helplessly at myself. “When people undress me, it’s usually the prelude to… performance. Ownership. I—my body goes somewhere automatic. I don’t want that with you.”  

Understanding slices across her features like light.

“Then don’t let them undress you,” she says simply. “You undress yourself. I’ll do the same. No one takes anything off the other person without asking. Agreed?”

The rules are… absurdly mundane.

Revolutionary.

“Agreed,” I say.

We separate a little, enough space to move without colliding.

My hands feel clumsy on my own buttons.

I have peeled out of more absurd costumes in my life than most people see in ten lifetimes. I have bared myself in front of donors, priests, nobles, godsdamned kings. I have been stripped by other hands so often my body still tenses when someone reaches for a buckle.

This is the first time in memory I am doing it purely for myself.

Leather squeaks as I shrug out of my coat. It hits the floor with a damp thump. My shirt follows, linen scraping over old scars. Pale chest, pale arms, sigils like faint, silvery vines over bone.  

I almost cross my arms over my torso out of reflex.

I don’t.

I let her look.

Her gaze isn’t clinical, the way temple healers’ were when they checked the sigils for degradation. It isn’t evaluative, like donors deciding how best to arrange me.

It’s… curious. Hungry, yes—but not devouring. Her eyes trace the faint lines carved into my skin, the ragged edges of old lash marks, the faint puckered circles where blades went in and came out again.  

“May I touch?” she asks, voice gone low.

“Yes,” I say, throat tight.

Her fingertips brush an old rite scar at my shoulder, feather‑light. Gooseflesh prickles in their wake. She follows the line down, over my biceps, my forearm, marveling with her hands like she’s reciting a different kind of testimony.

Then she steps back and, with very little ceremony, pulls her own simple linen undertunic over her head.

For a second, my brain stops.

She is not temple‑polished or donor‑presented. She does not remove it like a performance; she does it like she’s taking off a robe at the end of a long day—practical, unselfconscious.

The sight of her—bare, human, all soft lines and sharp angles and ink stains—hits me harder than any altarpiece.

I reach for her without thinking, then catch myself.

“May I?” I ask again, hands suspended in the space between us.

Her mouth curves.

“Yes,” she says. “Please.”

Please.

Another word no one has ever offered me in this context.

I step close, palms finding her waist, the curve of her ribs, higher to the line of her collarbones. Her skin is hot to my cold touch; she shivers, but not in rejection. Her pulse flutters wildly under my fingertips.

“You’re warm,” I murmur, stupidly.

She huffs out a breath that could be a laugh.

“You’re freezing,” she counters.

“Occupational hazard,” I say.

I lean in to press my mouth to the hollow of her throat.

The taste there is salt and sage and the faint, heady tang of blood thrumming just beneath delicate skin.

The Thirst snarls, interest prowling under my ribs.

I bare my teeth against it, very literally, then pull back enough that my fangs are nowhere near that fluttering pulse.

Not tonight.

Not like that.

I focus on different hungers.

The way she gasps when my lips find the sharp point of her shoulder. The way her hands knot in my hair, not to guide or command, but to hold on. The way our bodies slot together, my too‑cold chest against her heat, hips aligning without any of the practiced artifice I’ve spent centuries curating.  

My knees hit the mattress.

Reflex takes me.

I drop, sliding to them in front of her, head bowed.

Submissive. Offered. Exactly where I have been put a hundred times.

I realise what I’ve done a half‑second too late.

Shame rushes up, hot and strangling.

Lysa’s hands clamp hard on my shoulders.

“Up,” she says, voice firm.

The word slices clean through the muscle memory.

I blink up at her.

She tugs gently but insistently until I sit on the bed beside her instead of at her feet.

“You are not serving me,” she says. “Look at me, Cyran.”  

I do.

Her face is open, fierce. There’s no coyness there, no script, no part of her that thinks this is about worship.

“We are doing this together,” she says. “Or not at all.”

Together.

The bed creaks as we shift, lying down in the narrow space. There is no room for distance; our legs tangle automatically, one of my arms finding its way under her head, the other draped over her waist. Her thigh hooks over mine. Her breath warms my neck.

Everything is contact.

Every inch of me is aware of every inch of her.

It’s almost too much.

My senses are sharpened by unlife—sound, smell, touch all dialed up past human thresholds. Every rustle of the blanket, every hitch in her breath, every tiny change in her skin temperature lands like a small explosion.

She must feel my shudder.

“Too much?” she whispers.

“No,” I say, and it’s honest and it’s a lie and it’s all I have. “Just… new.”

“Stay with me,” she says.

Her hand finds my face in the low light, thumb stroking the arch of my cheekbone, drawing me back down into her kiss.

And I do.

When the old habits tug—the urge to go away, to watch from behind my own eyes while my body performs—I fight. I cling to every point of contact: her fingers in my hair, the scrape of her nails along my back, the way her lips open under mine on a soft, broken sound that has nothing to do with an audience.  

“Tell me what you want,” she breathes against my mouth.

I’ve been asked that question before.

Never with the expectation that my answer mattered.

“Everything,” I say, before I can stop myself. “And nothing that hurts you.”

Her laugh is breathless, a small, incredulous huff.

“We can work with that,” she says.

The rest blurs into sensation.

Her body under my hands, all warm curves and unexpected strength. The slide of skin, the rasp of stubble against the soft inside of her thigh when I forget to worry about where my face goes and just follow the sound of her breathing. The way she says my name, once, twice, like a verdict and a prayer and a promise.

There is a point—one long, suspended heartbeat—where the reality of being this close, this joined, almost breaks me.

I still.

Every muscle in me goes rigid. The room narrows to the point of her hands on my shoulders, her eyes locked on mine.

“Cyran,” she whispers. “Here. With me.”  

Her palm presses flat over my chest, right where my heart would be if it still obeyed mortal rules.

I drag in a breath.

The smell of sage and smoke and rain‑damp linen floods me. The faint creak of the bed. The relentless patter of marrow‑mist on the pane. Her gaze, dark and steady, bringing me back like a command phrase rewritten in a softer language.

I come back.

“Yes,” I say, half to her, half to myself. “I’m here.”

We move.

Slow. Deliberate. No performance. No script.

I don’t bother to sound pretty.

I make broken, ugly noises into her shoulder. I bite down—not hard, not enough to break skin—just to feel something other than the rush in my veins. She arches against me, whispers my true name into my ear in a tone that makes every old sigil along my skin flare with something that is not obedience.

When it comes—when everything crests and snaps and spills over—I don’t see temple ceilings or donors’ faces.

I see her.

Ink on her fingers digging into my back. Lips parted on a gasp. Eyes wide, not in fear, but in fierce, wild trust.

It feels like being shattered and remade.

For once, when the rush recedes and the world slowly settles back into place, I don’t feel hollow.

I feel… full.

Heavy with my own bones. Anchored to my own skin.

We are a tangle of limbs and sweat and breath in the cramped bed, the rough wool blanket itching against bare shoulders, the air cooling quickly on damp skin.  

Outside, the rain hasn’t let up.

Inside, for the first time since I can remember, the aftermath of sex doesn’t taste like shame.

Lysa lies half‑on top of me, cheek pressed to my chest, one hand splayed over my ribs as if making sure I’m still there. Her hair is a dark, damp mess against my skin. My arm curls around her automatically, palm resting between her shoulder blades.

I can feel each of her breaths, ragged at first, then slowly evening out.

“You all right?” she murmurs.

It takes me a moment to realise she’s asking me.

“Yes,” I say.

The word feels enormous.

I lick my lips.

“I don’t feel—” I frown at the ceiling, searching for the right wrong word. “Used. Or… filthy. Or like I need to peel my skin off and start again.”

Her hand tightens fractionally.

“Good,” she says into my chest. “Because if you did, I’d consider that a failure on my part, and I hate failing.”

I laugh, low and incredulous.

“You’re impossible,” I say.

“Efficient,” she corrects, voice already edging toward sleep.

The fire collapses with a soft sigh, embers settling into a low, red glow.

The marrow‑rain keeps its steady percussion.

I stare at the cracked bone of the ceiling, at the faint lines where the god’s ribs meet the Tribunal’s plaster, and realise something that should be obvious and feels utterly revolutionary.

I am not dreading dawn.

Danger is coming. Thessen has my designation circled in his ledgers. Voss has my case underlined in his. The Donor Trial still looms, teeth bared.  

But right now, in this too‑small room with its ink and ledgers and narrow bed, there is something the temple never managed to strip from me.

The ability to choose.

To stay.

To love.

Lysa’s breath warms my chest.

Her heartbeat, fast and mortal and fragile, drums against my ribs like a legal argument I’ve decided to believe in.

I close my eyes.

For the first time in two hundred years, I let myself fall asleep next to someone and do not plan my exit.

Whatever waits in the morning, it can wait.

Tonight, my body is my own.

Tonight, I give it freely.
