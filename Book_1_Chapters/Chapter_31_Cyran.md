# Chapter 31 — *The Wake-Up Call*
### Cyran | POV

The chanting is trying to unmake me, syllable by syllable.

There’s a rhythm to it—rising, falling, curling around itself like a knife slipping between ribs. Old words. Older than the Temple, older than the Vigil, older than the city that rots on this dead thing’s bones. They were carved into my spine long before I knew what they meant.

Tonight they mean *erase*.

Cold iron bites into my wrists and ankles, sunk deep enough into the stone that when I flex, all that moves is skin sliding uselessly against metal. The altar underneath me is slick with condensation and old blood; the Heart‑Chamber is always humid, but the heat tonight is obscene, oppressive, like being pressed against fevered flesh.

Above me, the Heart‑Node hangs from calcified arteries like an obscene chandelier—crystalflesh and bone, pulsing slow and heavy, each beat a red‑black flare that stains the air. Veins of light crawl through it, carrying the glow down through bone tubes into the surrounding walls, then out into the city.

Every time it contracts, the chains around my limbs tighten a fraction.

Every time it expands, the chanting swells.

I hold onto my name like a man hanging off a cliff by his fingernails.

Cyran.

Not Relic.

Not Asset.

Not *Child of the Covenant* or any of the other pretty titles they fed me between knives.

Cyran.

The sound of it in Lysas mouth anchors me for exactly one heartbeat.

Then Varos speaks, and the rope slips.

“Begin,” he intones.

His voice slides over my skin like cold oil.

I force my head to turn, just enough to see him.

He stands at the foot of the altar, close enough that if I could move, if I could slip these restraints, I could have my hands around his throat in a single lunge. His robes are the same immaculate, bone‑white silk he wore in the Tribunal, the same calm mask of patient disappointment on his lined face.

His eyes are wrong.

They catch the red‑black light and throw it back like something under the surface of deep water.

“Obedience,” he says.

The word is not aimed at the gathered priests, or at the Heart‑Node.

It is aimed at me.

It lands.

Pressure spikes behind my eyes.

My tongue tries to form the familiar response, the one beaten into me a hundred times in a hundred candlelit rooms.

“Yes, Father.”

A lifetime of survival in two words.

I grit my teeth until my jaw aches.

“Go to hell,” I say.

My voice comes out raw.

Shaky.

Still mine.

His mouth thins.

“Always so theatrical,” he says softly. “Even now.”

He lifts his hands.

The other priests—pale, hooded, lining the circumference of the chamber like teeth—join their voices to his. The words they chant are in the old tongue, the one I stopped translating for myself years ago for the sake of my own sanity.

Tonight, the meaning slides in whether I want it or not.

Offer.

Bind.

Sanctify.

Strip.

Each syllable is a hook.

They catch on me—on scars, on memories, on the old brand burned over where a heartbeat used to be. The Redeemer’s Mark on my collarbone flares hot in answer, silver bleeding into red. Invisible threads snake out from it, up into the air, toward the descending Heart‑Node.

It pulses in time with the chant.

With my not‑heart.

With the dead god’s fossilized beat.

Pain would almost be a kindness.

Pain is honest.

This isn’t pain.

It’s emptiness.

Like someone is reaching inside my skull with careful fingers and lifting things out one by one.

Important things.

Useless things.

It doesn’t seem to care which.

The smell of the Ribcage in rain.

The feel of Lysa’s hair slipping through my fingers when I buried my hands in it at the graveyard.

The sound of my own voice, younger and rougher, screaming under a different ritual knife.

All of it blurs at the edges.

Who am I?

The thought comes, thin.

Echoing.

Like it’s shouted down a long, hollow hallway.

Who am I?

I am—

Relic, says a chorus of old voices in the back of my head.

Pretty thing.

Holy vessel.

Divine channel.

*Property*.

I taste bile.

No.

I try to clench my hands.

The chains bite.

Except—it’s not just chains.

Somewhere between one heartbeat and the next, my muscles stopped listening to me.

Magic threads me like a marionette, holds me in place. I can feel it traveling along my limbs, numbing as it goes, like too‑cold water filling veins.

Somewhere under my left sleeve, cold metal presses against my forearm.

The fork.

A ridiculous little stolen thing, tucked away in a moment of petty defiance back in my old room. At the time, it felt like the smallest of rebellions.

Now it’s all I can think about.

If I can just move.

If I can just twist my wrist enough to catch the tines on the chain.

If.

The pressure in my head spikes again.

Varos’s voice rises above the chant.

“Accept the grace of the Prime Contract,” he intones. “Surrender will. Surrender want. Become function. Become form. Become what you were made to be.”

The words hit me like blows.

They are not command phrases, not the precise liturgical triggers the Broker used to use when he wanted me on my knees and singing.

They’re broader.

Deeper.

They go past scar tissue I’ve spent years building and aim straight for the soft, pulped places underneath.

Memories slip.

The bar in the Vein Markets.

Cairn wiping a glass with one hand and watching me with that suspicious, narrow‑eyed gaze as I pretended not to flinch when someone brushed against my back.

Redemption patrols.

Pila’s small, sticky hand gripping mine in the Bone Market.

Lysa’s quill scratching across parchment while she pretended not to notice I was staring at the way her mouth pursed when she was thinking.

They all smear together.

Too much.

Too loud.

“I am—” I manage.

The word claws its way up my throat.

“I am—” 

Relic, the magic whispers.

Relic, the old scripts insist.

Relic, the Prime Contract hums from the walls.

“Cyran,” another voice says.

Not in the chamber.

In my bones.

Quiet.

Steady.

*You were a person. They made you a thing. That’s not your fault.*

Lysa’s voice.

Memory, or hallucination, it doesn’t matter.

I latch onto it like a drowning man.

I squeeze my eyes shut.

Bad idea.

The darkness behind my lids is worse.

In it, the Heart‑Node is not just a structure.

It’s an eye.

An enormous, pulsing pupil of congealed blood and contract sigils, staring straight down at me. Every clause ever carved into my skin flares in sympathetic recognition, a thousand little leashes all yanking at once.

Just let go, the magic breathes.

It doesn’t do it in words.

It does it in absence.

In the sweet promise of nothing.

No more choices.

No more fear.

Just warm, eternal silence.

You don’t have to fight if you’re not there to lose.

“You are *nothing* without us,” Varos says, his voice close now, like he’s leaned in over my face. “You know that. You have always known that. We took a dead, pretty corpse off an altar and made you into something holy. Without our structure, you are just… hunger and blood. You should be grateful.”

Grateful.

I almost laugh.

It comes out a ragged wheeze.

He’s not wrong about the hunger.

It snarls now, feral and terrified under the weight of the magic, wanting to run, to strike, to tear. But there’s nowhere to go.

“Obedience,” Varos says again.

The word is a knife.

It slices through what’s left of my resistance with horrifying ease.

My jaw loosens.

My tongue wants to shape the answer.

“Yes, Fa—”

Lysa again, cutting across the script.

*Stop waiting for me to punish you for wanting something. I’m not them.*

Want something.

My fingers twitch.

The movement is microscopic.

Agony.

Glorious.

There is still a *me* in here to hurt.

That thought shouldn’t be comforting.

It is.

The chanting swells.

The Heart‑Node descends, its underside splitting open to reveal a tangle of fleshy cords and glittering bone hooks, all reaching, writhing, eager to connect. Threads of light spill down with it, brushing over my skin like cold spiderwebs.

When they touch old sigils, they spark.

My marks burn.

My Redemption tally along my collar and shoulders explodes into silver fire, each line a fuse. The temple brand at my throat—overwritten, faded, but never fully gone—flares so hot I smell my own skin cooking under it.

My back arches.

I scream.

I think.

It doesn’t sound like my voice.

Who am I?

I—

“I am Relic,” something in me whispers.

The word slips out slick.

Thoughtless.

I choke on it.

No.

“I am—”

The syllables dissolve.

Lysa’s face hangs in the dark behind my eyes.

Her mouth set in that stubborn, infuriating line.

*Choose what you want. I’ll stand with you either way.*

What I want.

Ridiculous question.

No one has ever cared what I want.

I wanted sleep, and they gave me ritual.

I wanted quiet, and they gave me applause.

I wanted death, and they dragged me back screaming.

And now—

Now I want—

A shitty little room over a bar that smells like spilled Grace and dubious decisions.

The weight of her body pinning me to a too‑narrow mattress.

Her hand in my hair.

Her voice in my ear, soft and wrecked and honest.

*I love you because you’re you.*

Heat floods my chest.

It fights the cold of the ritual like a drop of wine in an ocean, pathetically small, instantly diluted.

I cling to it anyway.

Magic claws at my thoughts, trying to pry them loose.

Fingers.

Fork.

Move.

I throw everything I have left at my left hand.

It’s like pushing through wet stone.

Muscles spasm.

The chain bites deeper into my wrist, grinding bone.

My fingertips scrape skin.

Cold metal kisses the side of my forearm.

There.

There.

“Just let go,” the nothing hums.

Varos’s voice weaves through it.

“So much pain, child,” he murmurs, almost pitying. “Always struggling. You could stop. Stop pretending you’re anything but what we made you. Stop clawing at a freedom that hurts you more than us. Be what you are. A conduit. An instrument. A beautiful, hollow thing.”

Beautiful.

Hollow.

For centuries, that was all I knew how to be.

The altar shudders under me.

The Heart‑Node is close enough now that I can feel its heat radiating down, a sick, humid breath that smells of copper and incense and the faint, sour tang of old fear.

Threads of light thicken, become ropes.

They writhe, seeking purchase.

One brushes my chest, just over where Lysa carved my name into my own ribs in that graveyard.

Cyran.

The sigil she left there flares.

For a second, the invading magic recoils, hissing.

Then it surges back, furious.

I am so tired.

The thought slides in, boneless.

Tired of screaming.

Tired of fighting to exist in rooms that resent my presence.

Tired of pulling teeth from systems that grow them back faster than I can break them.

Tired of choosing to live when every day closer to freedom is also a day closer to someone noticing and yanking the leash.

Maybe the nothing wouldn’t be so bad.

Not peace, exactly.

Not even rest.

Just… less.

No more wanting.

No more fear.

If I let go, the chanting will fill the space where I was.

The Relic will smile.

The donors will clap.

The Prime Contract will feed.

Lysa will—

Lysa will…

What?

Go back to her office.

Write my death up as an unfortunate inevitability.

File it under *acceptable losses*.

Move on to the next case.

She told me once she wouldn’t be another name on the list of people who took me like a favor.

She didn’t say she wouldn’t be another name on the list of people who *lost* me.

I squeeze my eyes tighter.

My hand slips on the fork.

The tines bite skin.

Blood slicks the metal.

It’s not enough.

“I’m sorry,” I whisper, and I don’t know who I’m apologizing to. Myself, for giving up. Her, for leaving her with the wreckage. The child I was, for proving him right. The dead god, for not being the obedient little sacrifice it wanted.

The altar vibrates.

My body bows.

The Hammond organ of the Heart‑Node’s beat crescendos.

The world narrows to red‑black light and the cold, hungry nothing and the empty echo of my own name.

Cyran.

Relic.

Nothing.

I let go.

I close my eyes, open my mouth to let the next chant‑word fill me and erase the last of whatever stubborn shard of self is hanging on.

And the universe explodes.

Not metaphor.

A sound like the world cracking in half punches through the chamber.

A concussive *BOOM* that rattles the bone arches, rips chunks of calcified flesh from the ceiling, showers us in brittle shards. The Heart‑Node lurches, its descent stuttering. For a second, the chant falters.

Dust rains down in pale sheets.

My ears ring.

The pressure in my head stutters.

Pain rushes in to fill the gap where the emptiness was.

I suck in a ragged breath I don’t technically need.

“What—?” Varos snaps, the first real crack in his composure.

The priests around the chamber stutter over their syllables, some falling silent, others trying desperately to keep the rhythm.

Another sound slices through the mess.

A voice.

Not liturgical.

Not divine.

Human.

Furious.

“VAROS!”

The single word slams into me harder than the explosion.

It ricochets around the chamber, bounces off bone and blood and ritual, finds its way straight into the hollowed‑out core of me that was bracing for erasure.

My eyes snap open.

For a second, everything is blurred—red light, black shadows, gray dust.

Then my vision clears enough to find the source.

I can’t see her yet.

She’s beyond the edge of the altar, in the shadows near the entrance, backlit by whatever hell she just unleashed on the outer doors.

But I know that voice.

I know it in my bones, in the scars of my contract, in the places the Temple never meant me to have.

Lysa.

“Get away from him!” she screams.

The command is not ritual.

It has no sigils behind it.

No prepared sacrifices.

No divine authority.

And it hits harder than any order the priests ever shoved down my throat.

The paralysis shivers.

The magic, already shocked by the interruption, misfires.

For a heartbeat, the invisible hands pinning my limbs falter.

My fingers jerk.

The fork slides.

Cold metal drops into my palm.

Hope is a knife.

Sharp, sudden, painful.

It cuts through the haze with agonizing clarity.

She’s here.

She *came*.

The part of me that had been so certain—so grimly, logically certain—that leaving her behind was the only way to keep her safe looks at this, at the impossible fact of her voice ringing in the Heart‑Chamber, and shrivels in on itself.

She read my note.

She ignored it.

Bless her stubborn, infuriating, dangerously ethical heart.

Varos whirls toward the sound, his control finally cracking wide.

“Seal the doors!” he snaps. “Do not let—”

Too late.

I can hear it in the way his voice pitches, in the panicked shuffling of feet near the entrance, in the wet, meaty thud of a priest hitting the floor and not getting up.

There is another sound threaded through the chaos now.

Quieter.

Closer.

My name.

“Cyran!”

It’s not a scream this time.

It’s… sharp.

Focused.

Like she’s aiming it, a lifeline thrown across a chasm.

I seize it.

“I’m here,” I rasp.

The words scrape my throat raw.

Blood trickles from my nose, warm and metallic, slicking back into my hairline as it runs. My eyes burn; when I blink, red smears the edges of everything. The chains bite deeper as my muscles try again to move, pushing against the lingering paralysis.

A hand clamps down on my shoulder.

Varos leans over me, his face a mask of outrage and something uglier.

“You *told* me you understood, child,” he hisses. “You walked back into this willingly. You *chose* this.”

“Oh, well,” I say, teeth bared. “You know me, Your Excellency. Always making terrible choices.”

His grip tightens.

“Resume the chant,” he snarls over his shoulder. “Now.”

The priests rally.

Voices rise.

The Heart‑Node, which had jerked to a halt mid‑descent, shudders and starts to lower again, slower this time, as if suspicious.

Magic surges back over me in a frothing wave.

The emptiness reaches for the space I almost vacated.

Only now, something else is there.

Lysa’s voice, fraying at the edges with fury.

Lysa’s footsteps, pounding against the bone floor.

Lysa’s absolute refusal to let this place keep what it stole.

My mouth floods with blood as my bitten cheek splits under the strain of my grin.

Varos sees it.

His eyes narrow.

“You think she can save you?” he asks, low. “You think that little scribe with her ink and her rules can stand between you and a god?”

I look up at him.

At the Heart‑Node looming overhead.

At the web of contracts and blood and exploitation pulsing in its veins.

At the chains on my wrists.

At my own pathetic, blood‑slick hand closing around a stolen fork.

And then I think of Lysa in the Tribunal Hall, putting her badge on the table with a sound like judgment. Of her walking away from everything she thought she was for the sake of something she decided she’d rather be.

I think of her earlier words, spoken in a quiet office that might as well have been a battlefield.

*No one owns you anymore. Not if we can help it.*

If anyone can stand between me and a god, it’s her.

Of course it’s her.

I drag in a breath.

It tastes like copper and smoke and heart‑blood.

“Too late,” I whisper.

Varos’s grip tightens enough to bruise.

“What did you say?” he snaps.

I let my head loll toward the direction her voice came from.

Dust swims in the air, turning the red light murky.

Somewhere in that storm, a silhouette moves.

Small, upright, relentless.

“She’s here,” I say.

It comes out half‑laugh, half‑prayer.

He follows my gaze.

For the first time since I’ve known him, something that looks very much like real fear flickers across his face.

He masks it quickly.

But not quickly enough.

“Oh, Varos,” I murmur, baring my bloody teeth in something that might generously be called a smile, “you have no idea how inconvenient she can be.”

He opens his mouth to respond.

He doesn’t get the chance.

Because from the entrance, cutting through chant and heartbeat and collapsing stone like an oath, comes Lysa’s voice again.

This time it’s not my name.

It’s his.

“High Priest Varos,” she calls, every syllable precise, ringing with authority no god ever granted her.

“I object.”
