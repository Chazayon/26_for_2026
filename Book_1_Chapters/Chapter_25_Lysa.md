# Chapter 25 — *The Auditors Objection*
### Lysa | POV

Thessen looks at me like I’m a bug.

I am a bug that just ate his crops.

The Heart‑Chamber is dead silent for one impossible heartbeat. Cyran’s dagger hangs frozen an inch from Varos’s chest, obsidian point dimpling white cloth. The red light from the Heart paints everything in pulses—Varos’s shocked face, Cyran’s shaking arm, the donors’ wide eyes, the priests’ parted mouths—like the whole room is a throat trying to swallow and failing.

My own voice still rings in my skull.

Cyran.

He heard me.

I know he did.

I feel it down our bond like a live wire.

I’m standing in the main entrance, halfway up the long aisle, filthy and wet and bleeding into my sleeve. The stink of sewer still clings to me under the thick incense—rot and metal and whatever unholy ordure the temple dumps into its hidden ducts. My hair hangs in damp curls around my face. My boots leave faint, grimy marks on the polished bone floor.

Every head turns.

Of course they do.

The donors on the balconies lean over the rails, craning for a better view of the interruption they didn’t pay for. Acolytes shift, robes whispering. Guards nearest the dais tense, hands going to weapons. The entire city’s elite and half its clergy stare at one bedraggled Tribunal scribe who just shouted over their god.

Good.

Let them look at me instead of the blade.

I lift my left hand.

The metal of my Tribunal badge glints in the pulsing light where it sits strapped to my wrist, slick with sewer water and blood. I rip the strap loose with my teeth and raise the badge high, arm shaking with more than exhaustion.

My throat feels scraped raw.

I project anyway.

“Objection!” I shout.

My voice cracks.

I don’t care.

It still fills the hall.

It rides the Heart’s amplification grooves, bounces off the curved ribs of the dead god like a verdict.

The word slams into the silence like a dropped gavel.

Thessen recovers first.

His mask slips.

Just a little—enough that I see the snarl under the courtroom calm.

“Seize her!” he barks.

The order cracks across the nave.

Temple Guards stationed along the walls jerk into motion, white armor flashing as they converge on the center aisle from either side. I hear the scrape of spears, the clatter of boots, the collective intake of breath from a hundred donors.

I don’t look at them.

They’re not my priority.

I keep my eyes on Cyran.

“Cyran!” I call, putting every shred of authority I’ve ever used in a Tribunal square into his name. “Look at me!”

For a terrible second, nothing happens.

His arm stays raised, muscles bunched, dagger trembling over Varos’s heart. Sweat beads on his forehead, glistening under the red light. His eyes are locked somewhere past Varos’s shoulder, unfocused.

The bond between us is chaos.

Pain, fear, a suffocating pressure that feels like being buried alive under orders.

Then, like a twitch in a dying body, his head jerks.

Just a fraction.

But his gaze slips off Varos.

Finds me.

Even at this distance, I see the panic.

His pupils blown wide, silver ringed in red. His lips parted around a breath that doesn’t quite make it out.

Thessen sees it too.

“Finish it, Relic!” he snaps, stepping forward, arm outstretched. “Obedience is Virtue!”

The words hit the air with a weight I can feel in my teeth.

Obedience is the only Virtue.

The Key phrase.

My heart drops and spikes in the same instant.

The dagger steadies.

The tremor in Cyran’s arm smooths out as if someone laid a hand over his tendons. His jaw locks. The pulse at his throat flutters once, twice, then evens.

No.

No.

I take a step forward.

Then another.

The Guards are closing in from the sides; I can see them at the edge of my vision, flanking the aisle like the teeth of a trap. Red light glints off spearheads, off helms, off the slick of sweat on their faces. Some of them recognize me—I see it in the squint of a narrowed eye, a tightening of a jaw.

“Auditor Lysa?” one of them mutters, almost incredulous.

“Shut up and grab her,” his companion hisses. “Thessen said—”

I don’t stop.

If they get to me, it’s over.

If Cyran’s blade moves even an inch, it’s over.

“Obedience is a choice!” I shout, my voice cutting across Thessen’s like a second edge. “Choose, Cyran!”

The words snap out before I can second‑guess them, as instinctive as ink on parchment.

Somewhere under the fear, a colder part of me catalogues the absurdity.

I am arguing semantics with blood‑magic in the middle of a live ritual.

I am exactly myself.

I push the next words out in the cadence of a Key phrase, letting my voice fall into the same pattern Thessen used in the prep room files, the same rhythm carved into Cyran’s nerves.

“I command you,” I say, each syllable a rung on a ladder, “to choose.”

The air hums.

For a moment, it feels like the whole Heart‑Chamber is holding its breath—god’s corpse and donors and acolytes and guards, all waiting to see whose script the universe follows.

Cyran screams.

It rips out of him raw and ugly, a sound I have only heard hints of in nightmares and half‑remembered flashbacks. Not a dignified cry, not a battle roar. A wail from somewhere deep, where the child he was still huddles under layers of performance and scars.

His arm spasms.

The dagger jerk‑drops half an inch, then yanks back up, caught between the downward pull of Thessen’s command and whatever answer he’s trying to give me.

The blood‑bond between us explodes.

I feel it—his nerves on fire, his muscles tearing, the Key script screaming *Obey* in every line of his being, and underneath it, frantic, stubborn refusal. The collision makes my own vision spotty. My knees threaten to give.

Thessen’s face contorts.

“Strike!” he roars, flinging his hand toward Varos like a conductor snapping a baton.

“Drop it!” I yell, without thought.

My shout rips skin in my throat.

The word lands like a thrown stone.

For a brief, crystalline second, the entire world hangs on the edge of a decision taking place in a single, over‑scarred body.

Cyran looks at Varos.

The High Priest staggers back a step, outrage and terror warring on his lined face. He raises one hand as if to ward off the blade, the other clutching at the air, grasping for a command that will save him.

“Treason,” he gasps. “You—”

Cyran isn’t looking at him anymore.

He twists, tendons standing out in his neck, and looks over his shoulder at Thessen.

Their eyes meet.

Something passes there I can’t hear—years of Quiet Orders and Tribunal debriefs, all the times Thessen has sat behind a desk and called him It in a polite voice, all the little humiliations dressed up as procedure.

Then Cyran looks at me again.

Across the chamber, through Guards and smoke and sanctified architecture, his gaze locks on mine. The bond between us snaps tight and clear as a drawn bowstring.

I can see the question in his eyes.

Can I?

If he obeys me, if he takes my words into his bones the way they forced Varos’s into him, am I any better than they are?

I stop moving.

The Guards are almost on me; I feel the air shift as one closes from the left, the faint prickle of an enchantment net strung between spearheads. I ignore it.

I force myself to stand very, very still.

My badge is still raised.

My hand is still shaking.

“No one owns you,” I say.

My voice drops, not loud now, but precise. It slides under the ritual, the old Auditor tone I’ve used in public squares and private offices when a man with too much power tried to explain why a child’s death wasn’t his fault.

“Not them,” I say. “Not me. Not the Heart. Not the Contract. No one. You choose, Cyran.”

I do not tell him what to do.

I do not say *obey me*.

I give him the thing no one else has ever given him in this chamber.

A moment.

And a choice.

His pupils flicker.

Something like understanding flashes across his face—sharp, terrified, almost disbelieving. As if the possibility hurts.

“No,” he gasps.

The word is barely audible over the collective hiss the crowd draws in.

But I hear it.

I feel it.

A refusal pushed through a throat trained to say *Yes, Master* until it bled.

His fingers open.

The dagger falls.

For all that it’s only a second of motion, it hits my senses in slow detail: the spin of the obsidian as it drops, the smear of Varos’s white robe on the edge, the way the red Heart‑light catches the carved sigils along the blade for the first and last time.

Then it hits the altar’s stone with a clang that cracks the ritual wide open.

Sound returns to the hall in a rush.

Donors scream.

Some in outrage, some in fear, some purely because their tidy script has been ruined and they don’t know what to do with themselves. Acolytes stumble back from the altar, candles flaring and then snuffing in currents of panicked air. Varos stares at the knife at his feet, then at Cyran, as if inability to finish the stroke is a more profound betrayal than the attempt.

Thessen roars.

The sound is ugly.

“Kill them both!” he bellows, voice amplified by the Heart as if it recognizes a familiar timbre of sacrifice.

The Guards surge.

The one to my left lunges, spear net crackling blue‑white, aiming to pin me against the bone wall. I wrench my gaze from Cyran long enough to duck under the sweep, feel the static heat of the enchantment kiss the back of my neck. Another Guard reaches for my wrist; I snap my badge down like a shield, the metal rim connecting with his knuckles.

He yelps.

Drops his grip.

“Tribunal business!” I snarl, shoving past.

It’s absurd in context.

But the training is bone‑deep.

I have spent my adult life walking into rooms where no one wanted me and making myself impossible to ignore. This is no different, just… louder.

Cyran’s knees buckle.

He crumples to the floor as if someone cut his strings, hands slamming down to catch himself on the altar’s edge. His chest heaves; he drags in great gulps of air as if he’s been drowning for minutes and only now broken the surface.

Free.

The bond between us floods with something bright and wild, laced with agony and relief.

“Monster!” a donor shrieks from a balcony, voice cracking. “It’s all a farce—”

“Blasphemy!” another howls.

“Get her away from the altar!” one of the senior priests cries, face mottled with rage.

Varos stumbles backward, one hand clutching at the heavy pendant at his throat, the other pointing a shaking finger at Thessen.

“You did this,” he spits. “You brought—”

Thessen wheels on him, and for a moment I almost think he’s going to finish the original plan anyway.

Then his gaze cuts back to me, arctic with hate.

“Auditor Lysa,” he snarls. “You are under arrest for treason, ritual interference, and aiding a hostile Relic. Do you have any idea—”

“Yes,” I say, and start running.

There is no finesse left.

No strategy beyond speed.

I bolt down the aisle, ducking between grasping hands and swinging weapons. A Guard lunges to intercept me; I drop into a slide on the polished bone, my wet clothes making it easier, my cut arm sending a flare of pain up to my shoulder as it scrapes the floor.

I slam into Cyran’s side at knee height.

The impact jars my own bones.

I brace a hand on the altar and one on his shoulder to steady myself as I scramble upright.

Up close, he looks worse.

Sweat slicks his hair to his temples. His pupils flicker, unfocusing and refocusing like someone keeps turning the world’s brightness up and down. The veins along his throat and wrists stand out in a livid mesh, as if the Key carved them there.

“Hey,” I say, breathless. “Can you move?”

It is a ridiculous question.

His body has been a puppet for the last hour; every move cost him pain.

But I need him to hear the subtext:

Do you want to?

His head tilts up toward me.

Even on his knees, he’s almost level with my face.

A grin crawls slowly over his lips—not the sharp, theatrical one he wears like a mask, but something weaker and far, far more honest.

“For scenes of violence?” he rasps. “Always.”

Relief punches through my ribs so hard it almost drops me.

“Good,” I say. “Because we just ruined their show, and they’re about to cut to a different act.”

He plants one hand on the altar, the other on my thigh, and pushes himself to his feet with a groan that sounds like it’s being dragged out of him with hooks. For a second, he sways toward me; I slide an arm around his back on instinct, under the edge of his white robe, feeling the heat of his skin.

He tenses.

Then, just as quickly, relaxes into the contact.

Not like a man collapsing back into harness.

Like someone choosing to lean.

The first wave of Guards reaches the dais.

They hesitate, just a heartbeat, at the threshold of the sanctified space, as if old training wars with Thessen’s new orders.

Thessen solves their dilemma by drawing his own weapon.

He reaches under his ceremonial robes and pulls free a sword I’ve only ever seen reference to in sealed Tribunal files—a blade of translucent bone, shot through with veins of frozen red. It hums with contained power, the edges blurred with residual god‑light.

Of course he has an unregistered Artifact.

Of course he’s been saving it for a moment like this.

The Heart’s pulse stutters when the sword clears its sheath, as if recognizing a piece of itself.

“By authority of the Prime Contract,” Thessen intones, every inch the pious Contract Lord he’s always wanted to be, “I condemn you both.”

His gaze cuts between me and Cyran.

He raises the blade.

Behind him, Varos scrambles farther away, putting the altar between himself and both of us.

“Treason!” Varos wheezes again, voice cracking. “This is—this is—”

“Redundant,” I snap, not taking my eyes off Thessen.

My free hand finds the hilt of the stolen stun‑mace at my belt.

The charge is low; I can feel the faint buzz of it through my fingers, an underfed wasp nest. It’s not going to be much help against a god‑sword and a temple full of Guards.

I tighten my grip anyway.

“Stay behind me,” I start to say to Cyran.

He laughs once, harsh and genuine.

“Absolutely not, love,” he says, his voice already rasping toward something more familiar. He straightens, pulling away from my support to stand at my side, white robe falling just so despite sewer and sweat and almost‑murder. His eyes glitter with something that might be pain, might be exhilaration.

He bares his teeth at Thessen.

“If anyone’s going to die first on that altar, it’s him.”

The crowd roars.

Fear. Fury. Anticipation. All of it blurs into a single, brutal sound that rattles the ribs of the dead god.

The Heart beats once.

Twice.

Faster.

Around us, the temple’s careful choreography has dissolved into chaos—donors pushing back toward the exits, priests shouting contradictory prayers and orders, Guards trying to decide whether they serve Contract Lord, High Priest, or Heart.

But in the eye of that storm, at the foot of the altar, the lines are clear.

Thessen, with his illegal sword and lawful hatred.

Varos, clutching his half‑spent authority like a relic.

Cyran, still in white, still shaking, but standing of his own will.

And me.

An Auditor with no writ, no backup, no right to be here by any sane measure.

My pulse steadies.

My mind sharpens in the way it always does when a hearing goes from theoretical to lethal.

This is what all the ink and all the nights and all the tiny, stubborn no’s have been for.

I lift my badge again, not as a shield this time, but as a declaration.

“I audit this ceremony,” I say, my voice finding that dangerous, quiet register that has made men twice my size sit down without realizing why. “Every command. Every clause. Every body on this altar. And I find your use of this Relic”—I nod once toward Cyran without looking away from Thessen—“in violation of Founder Precedent, Consent Doctrine, and basic sanctity. Effective immediately, this ritual is suspended.”

It’s absurd.

It’s theatre.

It’s law applied to god‑magic like ink to blood.

But words matter.

Especially here.

Thessen’s lip curls.

“You think your little loopholes and scratched stone mean anything down here?” he sneers. “You think the Heart listens to your paperwork?”

He lifts the sword higher.

The red light along its edge flares.

The Heart’s pulse syncs to the movement.

“I don’t care if the Heart listens,” I say. “He does.”

I nod toward Cyran again.

I can feel him at my side, bristling, alive, ready.

Thessen’s gaze flicks to him.

Mistake.

Cyran smiles.

It’s not pretty.

It’s not performative.

It’s full of teeth.

“Bad news, Contract Lord,” he purrs, voice low but carrying, playing now for the crowd as well as the man. “You’re about to find out what happens when the pretty weapon on your leash learns to bite back.”

The Guards between us and Thessen shift uneasily.

Some of them have Temple brands older than my career.

Some of them still have the last Tribunal decree fresh in their heads.

All of them know, on some level, that what just happened with that knife should not have been possible.

Choice is contagious once you see it.

Thessen bares his own teeth.

“Then let’s test his bite,” he says, and lunges.

The god‑sword comes down in an arc of red light.

I move to meet it, stun‑mace up, body already bracing for impact I know it cannot fully take.

Beside me, Cyran moves too.

Together, we step into the fight we’ve just made inevitable.
