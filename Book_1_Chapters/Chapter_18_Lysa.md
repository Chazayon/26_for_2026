# Chapter 18 — *The Library of Skin*
### Lysa | POV

The Skull District stares at you, literally. The windows are carved like sockets. 

Empty eye-holes punched into bone towers, watching as we slip through the mist.

Marrow-fog curls around our boots, cold and greasy, carrying the smell of old incense and older blood. The shrines up here are older than the Tribunal—ribbed chapels and spine-spires grown straight from the dead god’s skull, layered with centuries of offerings and rot. 

At night, it’s quiet.

Not safe.

The safe house feels very far away.

Cyran moves at my side, close enough that his sleeve brushes my wrist when the fog gusts. He’s back in Vigil blacks tonight, but stripped of ornament: no collar, no insignia, just dark, close-fitting cloth and a dagger at his thigh. His hair is pulled back in a rough knot, as if he got impatient halfway through and gave up. 

On anyone else, it would look sloppy.

On him, it looks like he’s haunted a little less and hunting a little more.

“We’re late,” he murmurs.

“We’re on time,” I answer. “The Archive bells chimed the eleventh mark three minutes ago. The night-chant will just be ending.” 

“Nothing like liturgical boredom to lull the guards,” he says.

His tone is light enough, but his hands are very, very still.

When he’s at ease, he talks with them—flourishes, little theatrically precise ways of touching the world. Tonight, his fingers barely flex at his sides. When he swallows, his throat works too hard.

I could pretend not to notice.

I don’t.

“Remember,” I say quietly. “If you need out at any point, you say so. We leave. Records or no records.”

“We both know I’m constitutionally incapable of backing out halfway through a dramatic entrance,” he says.

“Cyran,” I say, sharper.

He huffs, breath clouding in the chill air.

“I remember the way,” he replies, voice a notch softer. “That will have to be enough.”

The Hollow Archive sits under one of the oldest temples in the Skull—a squat, heavy building hugged into the curve of the god’s cranium, its façade nothing but blank bone and a single, stern archway. No windows. No bells. No friendly lanterns. 

If you’re invited in, you go through the front door.

We’re not invited.

“Which ear?” I ask.

He tilts his head, gauging.

We’re standing in the shadow of the temple now, tucked between it and the next shrine along—smaller, dedicated to some minor saint of contracts. The wall at our backs is cold bone, faintly damp. Overhead, the curve of the god’s skull looms, the sky beyond it a black dome pricked with dull city-stars.

He points up, to a small, oval opening near where a human ear would be.

“There,” he says. “Ventilation. Smells atrocious inside if they don’t keep the air moving.” 

“Of course the Archive’s ear is a grate,” I mutter.

“At least it’s not the nose,” he offers.

“Small mercies.”

We climb.

The wall is all smooth bone at first, but the builders carved shallow niches into it centuries ago for devotional candles. Their soot has stained the depressions black. They make adequate footholds. 

Cyran goes first.

He doesn’t look back to see if I’m following. He knows I am. I could resent the assumption. I find it… oddly comforting.

We move in practiced silence: reach, haul, brace. My fingers slide on damp bone; his find the old chips and ridges from previous climbers. The air grows thinner, colder, the mist thinning as we leave it behind.

At the ear-grate, he braces one arm, levers the other under the edge of the metal. It’s old—corroded around the bolts. He grunts softly, muscle bunching in his shoulders, and something gives with a shuddering squeal of protest.

He pauses, every line of him suddenly arrested.

We hold still.

No alarms. No shouted challenge from below. The faint murmur of night-chant from inside continues unbroken—a low, rhythmic susurration that vibrates in my teeth. 

Cyran exhales.

“Still hate that sound,” he whispers.

He eases the grate sideways, just enough for us to squeeze through.

“After you, Investigator,” he says, with a little mock-formality that almost hides the tremor.

I slip into the dark.

The grate mouth is tight.

Metal scrapes my shoulders; bone scrapes my boots. The air inside the passage is stale and smells of old paper, lamp-oil, and something under it like dried meat. I hate it immediately. 

Behind me, Cyran follows, moving with the economy of someone who has done this before and knows exactly where not to bump his head.

The ventilation shaft slopes down, then levels off. It’s just high enough for us to crouch-walk if we don’t mind gathering dust on our backs. My hands brush along the walls as we go, feeling for cracks, for changes in temperature. The sound of chant grows louder, then abruptly cuts off.

Silence rolls in behind it, heavy as marrow.

We reach another grate.

Light glows faintly beyond it—not the warm yellow of oil lamps, but a sickly, greenish-white, like marrow-glow filtered through bad glass. 

Cyran’s hand comes over my shoulder, knuckles a whisper from my cheek, and touches the grate.

“Wait,” he breathes.

He closes his eyes.

I feel more than see the way he pulls in one long, steadying breath, holds it, then exhales words that are older than the Tribunal and older than my mother’s lawbooks.

“Sanctum silentium,” he murmurs. 

The words are feather-light.

The air doesn’t crackle. No visible sigils flare to life. But something in the pressure around us shifts: a softening, a retreat. The hum I hadn’t been aware of under my skin—some ambient awareness in the walls—dims.

“Now,” he says, and pushes the grate.

It swings inward without a sound.

We drop into the Archive.

The floor is bone-tile, worn slick and pale by centuries of feet—and something else. It’s too quiet for dust; someone maintains this place. The room stretches out around us in all directions, shelves marching off into the gloom, vanishing into shadow above. 

They’re too tall.

That’s my first thought.

The shelves are built with no regard for mortal reach, stretching floor to ceiling in uneven lattices of dark wood and carved bone. Each is packed with volumes, spines ragged, some bound in leather, some in something I do not want to name yet.

The air is cold.

Not the honest cold of outside, but the stale chill of a cellar left too long. It smells of preservatives and dust and the faint, metallic tang of ink made with blood. 

Cyran lands beside me in a silent crouch.

For a moment, he doesn’t stand.

His eyes travel up the nearest stack, tracing familiar sigils carved into the uprights—wards of silence, of forgetting, of obedience. His hand goes, unconsciously, to his own throat.

“Cyran,” I say.

He drops his hand.

His jaw flexes.

“All right?” I ask.

“No,” he says quietly. “Keep moving.”

He straightens, shoulders back, and the ghost of the old temple posture slides over him: back straight, chin lifted just so. Not a supplicant. Not quite an equal. Something in between, all edges sanded off for display.

I hate it.

“We need the Vessel Registry,” I say. “Where?”

He forces his gaze away from the shelves, down to me.

“Deeper in,” he says. “Subjects were… specialized. They kept us separate from the usual relics and rites. This way.”

We move.

The stacks form narrow corridors, some barely wide enough for two people to pass. Words in old tongues mark the ends of rows: Liturgia. Lexicon. Correspondentiae. Vasa. 

“Vessels,” I translate under my breath.

“Clever girl,” he mutters.

“This one?” I ask, nodding at the row marked VASA ANNO 300–400.

“Too early,” he says. “I’m not that old.”

“Year?” I press.

“Four hundred,” he says, lips curling over it like the number is a rotten seed.

We find it.

VASA ANNO 400–500.

The smell is stronger here.

Not just dust and preservative, but something richer, more organic. I let my fingers skim a spine as we pass, purely by reflex, and snatch my hand back.

Not leather.

Skin.

The texture is wrong—too thin, too soft, a faint pattern of pores under the varnish. 

I swallow bile.

“Books should smell like paper and glue,” I mutter, “not dried meat and regret.”

Cyran makes a tiny, breathless sound that might be a laugh if you put it under a microscope.

“Welcome,” he says, “to the Library of Skin.”

I want to burn it down already.

Later.

We follow the spines down to the end of the row, candles guttering in little sconces at ankle-height providing just enough light to read the stamped titles. VAS 4A. VAS 4B.

“Here,” Cyran says abruptly.

His hand hovers over a thicker volume, its spine cracked, the stamped letters nearly worn away: REGISTRA VASORUM: ANNO 400. 

Year 400. That’s me.

His fingers twitch back.

He doesn’t touch it.

“I can’t…” he says, voice fraying on the edges. “You do it.”

My chest tightens.

“You don’t have to—”

“I know,” he snaps, too sharp. Then immediately, softer, “I know. I… need to know. But I can’t put my hands on it.”

All right.

I step into his space.

Make him move back half a pace so I can stand between him and the shelf. It’s a small, physical buffer, but sometimes the body needs whatever lies and illusions we can give it.

I lay my hand on the spine.

It’s slick.

Preservatives have soaked into it over the years, making the surface shiny, polished by too many fingers. Under the varnish, the skin has a subtle pattern of stretched lines, pulled tight over the board.

“Street-urchins didn’t donate their hides,” I say, low.

“No,” he breathes. “We didn’t.”

I pull the book out.

It’s heavier than it looks, dense with pages.

We move a few steps down, to a small reading alcove carved into the stack—nothing more than a bare bone-ledge and a narrow standing shelf. A single candle burns there, unsupervised. 

I set the book down.

Open it.

The first page is just columns and columns of neat, crabbed script: SUBJECT, ORIGIN, MODIFICATIONS, OUTCOME.

The ink has faded to brown in places. Some entries are crossed out with ruthless slashes. Some have little notes in the margin—corrections, improvements.

Subject 1: Failed.

Subject 2: Failed.

Subject 3: Structural instability. Body rejected grafts.

Subject 4: Insufficient resilience to hunger induction.

Subject 7: Explosive reaction. Containment breach. Litigated as “divine displeasure.” 

Beside me, Cyran’s breathing has gone shallow.

He’s not looking at the page.

He’s staring at the wall, expression carefully blank.

I turn pages.

Subject 8: Appears promising, but cosmetic glamour incomplete. Donors displeased. Returned to street.

Subject 9—

My hand tightens on the edge of the paper.

Subject 9: Acquisition—male, approximate age 17, origin unknown (Skull District gutters). Condition: malnourished, high adaptability, minimal familial ties. Selected for Vessel Protocol 4. 

My throat closes.

They didn’t even give him a name.

Modifications: Enhanced reflex arc response. Vampiric blood infusion (successful). Hunger induction rituals (Stage 3). Cosmetic glamour—alveolar and dermal (donor feedback: aesthetically pleasing). Obedience lattice bonded to spinal column and cranial sutures. 

Outcome: SUCCESS.

“Lysa,” he says.

His voice is very small.

“It’s a recipe,” I say.

The words are flat, and make me want to be sick.

“Of course it is,” he answers. “That’s all we ever were, here. Ingredients.”

I bite the inside of my cheek.

There’s more.

I force myself to keep reading, eyes scanning faster now. Additional notes: Blood resource replenishes efficiently. Recovery periods shorter than projected. Subject displays higher-than-average linguistic and performative skills. Recommended for high-profile ritual assignments.

At the bottom of the page, the ink changes.

Red.

The letters are larger, more ornate, surrounded by a thin halo of sigils that make my eyes hurt if I stare too long.

COMMAND PHRASE:—

“Stop,” Cyran says.

I do.

Silence settles, thick as the dust.

He swallows.

“Read it,” he says. “I need to know.”

“No one should have this kind of power over you,” I answer.

“They already do,” he says. “Thessen bought it like a party favor. Better that at least one person who doesn’t want to use it knows what it is. Please, Lysa.” 

I exhale slowly, through my teeth.

My heartbeat is too loud in my ears.

I look back down.

COMMAND PHRASE: Obedience is the only Virtue. 

The syllables sit on the page, innocent and horrifying.

I feel them, more than hear them, reverberate somewhere in the air between us. The sigils around the words pulse faintly in my peripheral vision, a whisper of old magic.

I shut my mouth.

I do not say them aloud.

My tongue presses to my teeth, held there by sheer will.

“Lysa,” Cyran says.

I look up.

His eyes are wide, pupils blown, his body very still. Not predator-still. Not ready-to-pounce. A different kind of stillness—frozen, braced for impact.

“Do you—” His voice cracks. He clears his throat. “Do you feel anything? Any… pull?”

I touch my wrist unconsciously, fingers brushing the Tribunal mark tattooed there. 

“No,” I say.

“You’re sure?” He takes a half-step closer, as if he can sniff out a lie on my breath. “No sudden, overwhelming desire to shelve me back where you found me?”

“That desire predates this visit,” I say.

It’s a terrible joke.

He huffs out something like a laugh anyway.

Then his mouth firms.

“Say it,” he says.

“No,” I answer.

“Test it,” he insists. “If it works, we can—”

“No,” I repeat.

My voice drops in volume and rises in precision, the way it does when an argument in Tribunal chambers goes from frivolous to dangerous.

He goes quiet.

“You just read a set of instructions that, if spoken aloud, might strip my will,” he says, softer. “And you’re not even a little tempted to see if it works?”

I meet his gaze.

“Thessen is tempted enough for all of us,” I say. “The temple was tempted. Every priest who signed off on this—” I tap the page hard enough that the paper crinkles “—was drunk on that temptation. I’m not interested in joining their number.”

“Knowledge is power,” he says faintly.

“Yes,” I say. “And sometimes power is a test you choose to fail.”

I close the book.

The snap of paper and flesh-bound cover meeting feels like a gavel.

The sigils around the words flare once, then settle.

“I have it,” I tell him. “Up here.” I tap my temple. “I will remember it. If Thessen uses it, I’ll recognize it. I will never speak it to you. Not as test. Not as threat. Not as anything.”

“And if Thessen speaks it first?” he asks.

“Then I will do whatever it takes,” I say, “to make sure he never speaks anything again.”

Something dark and sharp in me likes that idea far too much.

I don’t examine it.

“We destroy this,” I say, putting my hand back on the cover. “Now.”

“Won’t that alert someone?” he asks.

“Yes,” I say. “Good.”

He arches a brow.

“Strategic arson,” he says. “How adorably out of character, Auditor Voss.”

“I call it evidence management,” I reply.

From my belt pouch, I pull a small glass vial.

Tribunal issue, technically. Acid for dissolving sigils or stubborn physical seals when paperwork isn’t enough. I sign it out on the form as tool for field correction of unauthorized bone-glyphs. This qualifies. 

“Last chance to back out,” I tell him.

He stares at the book.

At the place where his whole existence has been reduced to lines of ink and one neat red sentence that could gut him.

“Do it,” he says.

I unstopper the vial.

The smell is sharp, eye-watering—copper and citrus and ozone. I flip the book back open to Subject 9, resisting the urge to skip the earlier subjects an apology. 

I hold the vial over the red ink.

Pour.

The liquid hisses as it hits the page.  

For an instant, nothing happens.

Then everything does.

The letters bubble.

They don’t dissolve so much as… struggle. The red ink writhes in place, lines twisting, trying to hold their shape. The sigils around them flare, throwing off tiny sparks. Smoke rises, thick and grey and smelling of scorched hair and old, cooked meat. 

Then the book screams.

It’s not a literal voice. No lips, no lungs.

It’s the wards.

A psychic howl rips through the Archive, hitting my ears and bones at the same time. Every carved sigil in the walls flares in outraged sympathy. The shelves around us shudder as if something large has just slammed into them.

Light gutters.

Somewhere deeper in the stacks, something heavy moves.

“Well,” Cyran says, a little too loud over the echoing wail. “That answers the question of whether anyone’s home.”

The ink collapses.

The phrase Obedience is the only Virtue melts into an unreadable blot, sigils sloughing away like shed skin. The paper beneath it blackens, curls. Flame licks up from the acid itself, small but vicious. 

I snap the book shut.

The scream cuts off.

Silence rushes in, full of the hiss of cooling wards.

Then the alarms start.

Not bells.

Worse.

Every candle along the row flares green. The carved bone uprights of the shelves light up, sigils racing along them in a lattice of cold fire. The hum in the air spikes from background annoyance to full-blown roar. 

“Construct guardians,” Cyran says, already moving. “We need to—”

He’s cut off by the sound of bone scraping stone.

At the far end of the row, where the VAS spines vanish into shadow, something steps out.

It looks like someone took a human skeleton, enlarged it by half again, and rebuilt it with more attention to intimidation than anatomy. Extra ribs flare along its sides like armor. Its fingers are too long, tipped with hooked talons. Its skull has been carved with sigils and given a pair of shallow, glowing pits where eyes should be. 

“Sanctum silentium?” I hiss.

“Only works on the passive wards,” he answers. “These move.”

“Of course they do.”

The construct turns its head toward us.

The pits in its skull flicker.

“Run,” Cyran says, very calmly.

We run.

The shelves turn into a blur of spines and parchment as we sprint down the row, boots slapping bone-tile. Behind us, the construct moves—not fast, but with terrible inevitability, each step a measured, scraping thud. 

At the end of the row, another one shambles into view, this one smaller, more animalistic—its spine arched, moving on all fours with disturbing grace. Its skull-mouth opens in a soundless snarl.

“They’re flanking,” I say.

“Of course they are,” he replies. “Intelligent design.”

Left.

We dart down a side aisle, ducking under an arch where two shelves lean too close. Something long and jointed—a rib? a spine?—swings down from above as a deterrent. Cyran shoves me ahead of him, taking the brunt of the hit on his shoulder.

He grunts.

“You all right?” I shout over the rising hum.

“Just bruised my already bruised pride,” he snaps back.

Behind us, the constructs adjust.

I risk a glance over my shoulder.

The first guardian has reached the mouth of the aisle. It doesn’t run. It doesn’t need to. It extends an arm, palm splayed, and sigils flare between its fingers. 

“Down!” I bark.

We drop as a wave of force rips down the aisle above our heads, scattering loose parchment, knocking a few smaller volumes from the shelves. One bounces off my back. It lands open in front of my face.

An illustration stares up at me—a meticulous diagram of a skull being etched with sigils, labels pointing to each cut. My stomach turns.

“Move!” Cyran hauls me up by the back of my shirt.

We stagger to our feet and plunge forward.

Choice of exits dwindles: deeper into the Archive, where more guardians wait, or try to get back up.

“Window,” Cyran pants.

“There are no windows,” I say.

“Not at ground level,” he replies. “Come on.”

We veer right, through a gap between shelves that’s barely more than a crack. Wood scrapes my shoulder. My hair snags on a jut of bone; I yank it free, leaving a few strands behind as tribute.

The air changes.

Less cold. Less preserved. A hint of outside—a trickle of fresher air, damp with Skull-district mist. 

Ahead, the shelves break.

We spill out into a narrower chamber that runs along the outer wall of the Archive. Here, instead of shelves, the bone is bare, carved with tiny, precise holes high up—vents, this time not for air, but for light. 

Faint grey seeps in through them, the city’s night pressed close on the other side.

“Those are not windows,” I say.

“They will be,” Cyran says.

He darts to the nearest, examines the stone under it.

The construct-guardians thud closer.

Two now, from different corridors. Their footfalls echo, overlapped. The air itself feels thicker, like it’s turning against us.

Cyran plants his feet.

“Cover your ears,” he says.

I do.

He inhales, then lets out a wordless, piercing whistle note—so high it sets my teeth on edge even through my palms. It focuses in the narrow chamber, bouncing, amplifying. 

Hairline cracks spiderweb over the vent above him.

He hits it again.

This time, the stone gives way.

The vent shatters in a spray of fragments, bone-dust raining down. Cold night air knifes in, along with a spill of fog. The hole is just big enough for one reckless Vigil Relic and one increasingly complicit Tribunal Investigator to wriggle through if they don’t mind losing some skin.

“That’s our exit,” he says, already jumping to catch the edge.

One of the constructs lurches around the last shelf into the chamber.

Closer now, I can see the faint gleam of marrow-glue at its joints, the way its sigils pulse faster when it sees us. Its jaw unhinges, dropping too wide, and a low, subsonic growl rolls out that I feel in my chest more than hear. 

“Go,” Cyran snaps.

I don’t argue.

I run, plant a foot on the bone wall, and jump, grabbing the jagged edge of the shattered vent. Cold cuts into my fingers. Something sharp slices my palm. I haul myself up.

Below, Cyran steps between me and the construct without hesitation.

He squares his shoulders.

“Sanctum silentium,” he says again.

It doesn’t work this time.

The construct’s eyes flare brighter, a mockery of obedience. It raises one hand, sigils coalescing in its palm.

“Cyran!” I shout.

“Climb, Lysa!” he throws back.

It’s an old temple call-and-response. I hate how well it fits.

I scramble, wriggling through the opening. Bone scrapes my ribs. Fog kisses my face. Then I’m out, sprawling on the rough curve of the god’s skull, slick with night-mist. 

I twist, reach back through the hole, hand groping.

“Take it,” I snarl. “Now.”

His hand slaps into mine.

I brace, dig my boots into uneven bone, and pull with everything I have.

For a heartbeat, nothing moves.

The air behind him crackles—sigils discharging. He makes a sound that might be a curse or a half-strangled prayer. Then his shoulder clears the vent, then his hips, his boots scrabbling for purchase as he kicks free.

The force of him slamming into me knocks the breath out of my lungs.

We collapse in a graceless heap on the outer curve of the skull, rolling until my shoulder hits a shallow ridge hard enough to make stars explode behind my eyes.

Behind us, inside, something hits the vent from the other side.

Bone fragments spray out through the hole, stinging my back.

We don’t wait.

We run.

We slide along the curve of the skull, boots slipping, hands grabbing at carved ridges and devotional niches. The Archive wall drops away below, a sheer fall to the street far down. Wind tears at us, cold and thin.

“Left,” Cyran gasps. “The candle niches—use them—”

We zigzag, half-climbing, half-falling, until the slope eases near another temple’s shoulder. From there, it’s a matter of scrambling down, skinning more knuckles, dropping the last few feet into an alley that smells blessedly like normal city rot instead of embalming fluid. 

We hit the ground hard.

Stay down.

For a long moment, all I can hear is my own ragged breathing and the furious pounding of my heart. The Archive looms overhead, its smooth face as blank and silent as if nothing at all just tried to murder us from the inside.

A faint, greenish glow pulses once behind bone, then fades.

“They know someone was there,” I say.

“Good,” Cyran says, wheezing. “I’d hate to think we went to all that trouble for a discreet break-in.”

I turn my head.

He’s sprawled on his back in the muck of the alley, one arm flung over his eyes. Bone dust clings to his hair. There’s a scorched patch on his sleeve where he must have caught a near-miss from the construct’s sigil-blast. 

His chest rises and falls too fast.

“Cyran,” I say.

He lets his arm drop.

His eyes are… clear.

Shocked, yes. Hollowed out around the edges from what we just read. But clear.

“Well,” he says hoarsely. “Now we know.”

“Command Phrase,” I say quietly. “Obedience is the only Virtue.”

He flinches.

I feel the word settle in my mind like a brand.

Ugly. Heavy.

A piece of knowledge I will carry like a blade, angled away from him, toward anyone else who tries to use it.

“Thessen already has a copy,” he says, after a moment. “You know that, right?”

I nod once.

“At the Gala,” I say. “He bragged about ‘Temple-favored protocols.’ You don’t say that if you’re just speculating.” 

“His scribes would have come here,” Cyran says. “Paid whatever it cost, signed whatever blasphemies, for a duplicate of that page. That’s what he was selling last night—rights and keys and little strings to pull on men like me.”

“We destroyed the original,” I say. “That matters.”

“It’s symbolic,” he counters.

“Symbols move courts,” I say. “And people. And gods, if any of them are still listening.”

He snorts, then winces, a hand going to his ribs.

“You all right?” I ask.

“I’ll live,” he says.

He looks at me.

Really looks.

“You could have used it,” he says. “In there. Even just… to see. Make me sit. Make me stand. Make me shut up for five blessed minutes.”

“That’s not redemption,” I say, voice gone very calm. “That’s theft. The temple did enough of that. I’m not adding my name to the ledger.” 

His eyes soften.

“Lysa,” he says.

“Yes.”

“Thank you,” he says.

For once, I don’t deflect.

“You’re welcome,” I answer.

We lie there another breath.

Above us, the Hollow Archive crouches, full of screaming books and bone guardians and the ghosts of every Subject who didn’t get a neat red SUCCESS stamped next to their number.

In my mind, the words on that page are etched as clearly as ink.

Obedience is the only Virtue.

I add a line under them, in my own mental hand.

No.

I get to decide what virtues I serve.

I push myself up, bones protesting.

“Come on,” I say. “We should move before someone looks up and puts two and two together from the broken vent and the missing blasphemers.”

“Ah, yes,” Cyran says, levering himself to his feet with a theatrical little groan. “The thrilling return to skulking. My favourite part of any pilgrimage.”

He sways once.

I step closer without thinking, my hand hovering near his elbow, not quite touching.

“Tonight,” I say, “you were a person who broke into the place that wrote you down as a thing and ripped out the page. That matters.”

He smiles.

Small. Tired. Real.

“Partners in crime,” he says.

“Independent investigators,” I correct.

His laugh follows us into the mist as we disappear back into the ribs of the city, carrying a secret neither Archive nor temple nor Thessen can fully own anymore.
