# Chapter 20 — *The Cage Drops*
### Lysa | POV

The marrow-mist has soaked through every layer by the time we reach the gate.   

It clings to my skin and leeches heat from my bones, turns my fingers into stiff, ink-stained sticks inside wet gloves. My hood has long since given up; rain needles the back of my neck, creeping down my spine in cold, insistent threads.   

Beside me, Cyran walks in a silence that isn’t his usual theatrical sulk. His stride is too tight. Too contained. Like he’s holding himself in with sheer force of will.   

I can feel him vibrating next to me.

The sigil in the alley is a ghost between us.  Every time the memory of it surfaces in my mind’s eye—lumen-dust tracing harvest lines, the jagged triangle of a Contract cipher under temple script—I have to clamp down on the urge to spin on my heel and march back to the Sanctum to shove it under Voss’s nose.   

But I see what it did to Cyran.  The way his back hit the opposite wall because there was literally nowhere else to go. The wild, animal terror in his eyes when he said Varos’s name out loud.   

I can’t fix that with a statute.

Not tonight.

The iron gate to the scribe dormitories looms out of the gray like a rib broken clean through the city’s skin.  Thick bars, slick with rain, cut the courtyard from the Spine’s main artery. Behind them, the stairwell up to my quarters waits, a vertical tunnel of damp stone and weak lumen-lamps.   

Warden Griggs steps out of his little booth as we approach, lantern raised.   

He looks like the weather carved him. Heavy patched oilskin coat, hood sodden, gray whiskers clumped with moisture. His joints creak audibly when he straightens.   

“Evening, Auditor,” he grumbles. “You’re out late.” His gaze flicks past my shoulder to the tall, armored shadow trailing half a pace behind. His mouth tightens. “No guests past curfew. You know the rules.”   

Let alone… Vigil.

He doesn’t say it, but the pause is there, heavy as a dropped stone.   

Cyran keeps his head down, collar high, letting the rain hide half his face.  The Warden’s lantern still catches the gleam of his bracer, the telltale cut of Vigil leather. The man’s eyes narrow.   

“He’s an official escort,” I say smoothly, stepping slightly into the lantern’s line so it hits my badge instead of Cyran’s scars. “On Tribunal business.”   

The lie slides off my tongue with a fluency that would have made the Lysa of three weeks ago flinch.

Griggs snorts. “Tribunal business comes with paper.” He taps the side of his head with a gnarled finger. “I may be old, but I’m not stupid.”   

“Of course.” I slip my hand into my robe, fingers closing around the bit of brass and parchment that marks my authority. When I draw it out, there’s a thin silver coin tucked neatly under it. I hold both out, badge uppermost. “Here’s the paper.”   

His eyes flick to the glint of metal.

It vanishes as neatly as any street magician’s trick.

“Official escort, then,” he mutters. “Buy yourself something warm,” I add.

He grunts, already turning to unlock the gate.

The heavy latch groans, an old, familiar sound that usually signals relief. Safety. Four flights of stairs and a locked door between me and the city’s teeth.

Tonight it sounds like a question.

“You’re soaked through,” Griggs adds as he swings the gate open. “Try not to drip all over the stairs. I’m not scraping scribe off the steps if you slip.”   

“I’ll do my best,” I say.

He steps back, letting us pass.

The marrow-mist follows us into the stairwell, turning the stone steps slick. My hand slides along the cold iron rail as we climb, boots leaving wet prints behind. The building hums faintly with the life inside it—murmurs through thin walls, the distant scratch of pens, the occasional cough.   

Cyran’s breathing is too fast.

I hear it over the patter of rain. Short, controlled inhales, as if he’s rationing the air. The climb costs him nothing physically; his body doesn’t tire the way mine does. This is something else.

“Almost there,” I say over my shoulder.

Three flights.  Landing.  Turn.   

My key is a small, cold weight in my palm by the time we reach my door.

The wood is damp around the frame. A thin draft slides under the crack, bringing with it the familiar scent of old paper and cold ashes.

Home.

Such as it is.

The lock gives with a reluctant click.

I push the door open, step inside, and stand just inside the threshold.

The room looks exactly as we left it this morning.  The bed is a tangle of rumpled white sheets still marked by the shadows of our bodies.  The small table is crowded with ledgers and cups, one with a faint ring of coffee at the bottom.  The hearth slumps under a thin crust of ash over half‑dead embers. The air is cold, smelling of rain and sex and ink.   

Behind me, Cyran stops dead.

I can feel his panic building in the air like static.

The door hangs open, spine of the building gaping behind us. The iron bolt waits, handle dull with years of use.

Then I slam the door and slide the bolt home with a solid, echoing clack.   

That sound.   

Last night, it meant sanctuary. The world locked out, just us, time suspended.   

Tonight, with Varos’s sigil still burning in my mind, it feels like drawing a line in bone. This far. No further.

He mutters under his breath.

Bits of language slip out—High‑Divine, temple cant, Vigil code—tangled and jagged, fragments of mantras and curses. “Harvest binding—” “He’s here—” “Should have burned me with the altar—”

It ricochets off the walls and tries to lodge in my ribs.

They glow stubbornly under the ash.

There. Something I can fix.

Cyran paces.

Back and forth.  Back and forth.   

He is unraveling.

I watch him for one full circuit, then another, cataloguing.

Shallow breaths.  Tremor in his hands.  The way his gaze keeps snagging on the bed and jerking away.  The way his shoulders are climbing toward his ears, muscle by muscle.

His whole body flinches.

Not me.

Not this room.

Handlers.  Donors.  Varos.

“Lysa,” he says, like a man clinging to a single, solid word in a wash of noise.

“You don’t drink because he wants you weak,” I say. “You drink because you need to be strong enough to fight him tomorrow. And you don’t drink from him. You drink from me. On your terms. On mine.”

The smell of cider curls up from the pot, sweet and warm. It’s not what he needs. Not tonight.

I take my hand off his chest long enough to pour a mug of the spiced liquid. I press it into his fingers.

They’re trembling.

He grips the mug like it might float away.

“Try,” I say.

He lifts it, takes a sip.

His nose wrinkles.

He sets it down almost immediately on the edge of the hearth.

“It’s good,” he lies.

“It’s insufficient,” I say.

The cider’s heat has flushed a bit of color into his cheeks, but it’s surface. Cosmetic. His hands still shake. His eyes still dilate when he looks at my throat.

Fine.

If he won’t ask, I will offer.

I reach up, fingers sliding under the edge of my damp collar.

The fabric clings to my skin, reluctant. I unbutton it, one, two, three, until the skull’s chill air hits the hollow at the base of my throat. Then I tug the collar aside and bare the left side of my neck.   

His gaze snaps there like it’s on a string.

His pupils blow wide.

“Lysa,” he says, horrified.

“Yes.” I tilt my chin, exposing the steady, thrumming pulse at my carotid.  My heart beats a little faster under the cool air and his stare, but it’s not fear.   

Not of him.

I am not afraid of the man who flinched away from my touch this morning in case it tethered him to an obligation.

He takes a step back, hands lifted like I’ve pointed a loaded crossbow at his chest.

“No,” he says. “Not like this. I’m—” He gestures at himself, frustrated. “I am a shattered thing right now, Lysa. There is a non‑trivial chance I’ll forget the line between feeding and—” He cuts himself off, jaw clenching.

“And what?” I ask. “Enjoyment?”   

He swallows hard.

“Violence,” he says. “I don’t trust myself.”   

I look him straight in the eye.

“I do,” I say. “Do you trust me to tell you when to stop?”   

He freezes.

The question hangs there, razor‑delicate.

“No one has ever—” He stops, voice gone thin. “They never—” He shakes his head, tries again. “If you say stop…” He laughs, raw and breathless. “You realize I might not be able to. I might not hear you over the noise.”   

“Then I will make you,” I say.

His eyes widen.

“May I?” I ask.

He stares.

Then, slowly, he nods.

“Yes,” he whispers. “Gods, yes.”   

The world narrows.

Just us.  The hearth’s thin light.  The rain against the bone-glass.  The smell of cider and cold leather and salt.   

I step into him fully now, closing the last inches of air between us. The wet front of his tunic soaks my shirt in an instant, cold seeping in. His body is chilled from the rain, but under that, I can feel the unnatural coolness of his unlife, the electric hum of Thirst in his veins.   

My hands find his waist, fingers splaying over leather belts and buckles. His own hover for a second, uncertain, then settle—one at my hip, the other cradling the back of my head like I’m something breakable.

“Last chance to change your mind,” he murmurs, voice ragged.

“I’ll take it,” I say. “If I need it.”

He exhales.

“Say it,” he asks, words barely audible over the rain. “Please. I need to hear it.”

“I am sure,” I say.

His fangs descend.

I feel it more than see it—a subtle shifting in the angle of his jaw, a tension in the muscles of his face, the faintest rasp of enamel as they slide down.   

He lowers his head, breath fanning warm and uneven over my exposed throat. The prickle of his lips against my skin sends a shiver skittering down my spine. His hand tightens at my waist.

“Lysa,” he says again, one last appeal.

“I’m here,” I answer.

The bite is a sharp pinch, a twin‑pointed pain that steals my breath for a heartbeat.   

Then the venom hits.

Heat blooms out from the punctures like someone poured fire under my skin. It lances up the side of my neck, down into my chest, unfurling through my veins in a hot, heady rush. My knees buckle slightly.   

He catches me.

His arm cinches around my waist, holding me upright as my body adjusts to the sudden shift.

There is a moment—sharp, crystalline—where I am acutely aware of everything.

The pull at my vein, a slow, deliberate tug.  The wet slide of his tongue as he laps at the wound between draws, careful not to spill a drop.  The low, involuntary sound he makes against my throat as my blood hits his system.   

It is intimate in a way sex hasn’t been with anyone else.

There is no space here for performance, no scripts, no roles beyond giver and taker—and even that line isn’t clean. My fingers are fisted in his hair before I realize it, nails scraping his scalp, holding him to me as much as he holds me.   

The room tilts.

The Thirst is an odd mirror.  As his hunger quiets, something in me flares. The shared pulse under my skin and his tongue becomes a loop, a closed circuit.  Heat pools low in my belly, mingling with the heady, floaty lightness starting to creep into my limbs as the blood loss catches up.   

“Enough,” I whisper, when the edges of my vision start to fuzz.

He stops.

At once.

He pulls back as if yanked, fangs sliding free with a wet sound. My knees go fully out. He lifts, turning, and suddenly the room blurs—a few long strides—and then my back hits the mattress.   

The world spins.

His face looms above, panting, lips stained faintly red. His pupils are blown black, distant pinpricks of silver around the edges.   

“Are you—”

“I’m fine,” I cut in, breathless. “Just—” I laugh, dizzy. “That’s… more intense than the manuals say.”

Concern furrows his brow.

“Damn the manuals,” he mutters. “If you feel faint, if you—”

“I feel,” I say, catching his wrist and pressing his hand flat to my racing heart, “alive.”   

He shudders.

The energy between us shifts.

What was necessity a moment ago, grim and sharp and practical, tilts into something else.   

The bite throbs, a deep, insistent ache that hums in time with my pulse. The venom’s heat licks lower, curling around nerves that have nothing to do with survival and everything to do with want.

His hand on my chest feels suddenly insufficient.

I tug.

“Here,” I say.

He hesitates a fraction of a second.

Then the dam breaks.

Leather and linen hit the floor in a tangle.  Cold, rain‑chilled fabric gives way to skin, to old scars under my fingers, to the hard planes of muscle I’ve only traced through clothing until now. He is all contrast—cool body, burning mouth, shaking hands that are nevertheless careful as he peels my wet tunic away.   

The bed is too small.

We don’t care.

He moves over me, bracing his weight on his forearms so he barely touches me at first, as if afraid full contact will shatter something fragile. His hair falls in damp strands around our faces, turning the world into a small, enclosed space of breath and heat and the faint cinnamon tang of my blood on his tongue.   

“Tell me to stop,” he whispers, hovering at the edge of penetration, every muscle in him trembling with restraint.

“If I want you to,” I say.

His gaze locks to mine.

“I don’t want to be him,” he breathes. The words are raw. “I don’t ever want you to look at me and see him.”   

“I won’t,” I say.

It isn’t a reassurance. It’s a fact as solid as the wall at my back.

“You are not Varos,” I add. “He took. You ask.”

His throat works.

He presses in.

The stretch is a shock, even after last night.  Deeper. Heavier. The residual blood‑bond amplifies every sensation until it feels like being filled with molten metal. My back arches. His name tears out of me on a curse.   

For a few glorious, suspended seconds, everything is heat and friction and the intoxicating sense of being utterly, thoroughly here with him—not in a ledger, not in a file, not in a ritual.   

Then something in him snaps.

It is minute.

A hitch in his rhythm.  A stutter in his breath.  His eyes squeeze shut, expression contorting, not in pleasure, but in something close to pain.   

He goes very still.

Then he’s gone.

Not physically.  His body remains, braced above mine, still inside me, muscles quivering.   

But his gaze, when it jerks back to mine, is glassy. Far away.   

“Cyran,” I say.

No answer.

His breaths come too fast, ragged.  He jerks away abruptly, pulling out, scrambling back against the headboard with a violent, uncoordinated scramble that leaves the blanket twisted around his legs. He drags his knees up to his chest, wraps his arms around them, makes himself small.   

“I’m sorry,” he chokes out, hands flying up to cover his face. “I’m sorry, I can’t, it’s—it’s too much, I—” His shoulders shake. “I’m sorry.”

I don’t move for a heartbeat.

The old Lysa—the one trained on etiquette manuals and logic trees—would have reached for modesty first, for distance. Clothes, separation, apologies of her own for putting him in this position.

That Lysa is gone.

I scoot up the bed, blanket be damned, utterly unashamed of my nakedness. My skin is flushed, my pulse pounding, my body unsatisfied and humming with thwarted need.

None of that matters.

I reach for him.

He flinches when my hand touches his shoulder, a violent, instinctive jerk away, pressed into the corner between wall and headboard like he’s expecting a blow.

“Breathe,” I say.

My voice surprises even me.

It isn’t soft. It isn’t the lullaby cadence comforters are supposed to use.

Commanding.

Present.

“Look at me,” I add.

Slowly, fingers trembling, he peels his hands away from his face.

His eyes are wet.

It guts me more than any scream.

He stares.

“No,” I say.

His brows knit.

“No,” I repeat.

His mouth twists.

In ragged, hitching gasps at first.

“Lysa,” he says.

“Yes.”

“We already did,” I point out. “Look at that. World’s still here.” I make a vague gesture toward the window. “Rain’s still falling. Tribunal’s still corrupt. Nothing exploded because we chose your mental health over a neat narrative arc.”

A wet laugh bursts out of him.

He stares at me like I’ve done something impossible.

“If you apologize for being traumatized one more time,” I say, “I’m going to make you recite Founder Clause Eleven-A backwards.”

He chokes.

“Don’t threaten me with a statute,” he says weakly.

“Then stop giving me reasons to,” I reply.

We look at each other.

The air between us has changed.

Still charged. Still thick with everything that almost was.

But now, also, with something heavier. Denser. A new rule carved in quiet ink:

This can stop.

And nothing will be taken away.

I lift a hand, cup his jaw.

A question.

To drink.  To stop.  To begin again.   

To be held.

And I—gods help me—am allowed to hold him.
