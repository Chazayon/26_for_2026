# Chapter 21 — *The Puppet Strings Drafted*
### Cyran | POV

Daylight usually feels like a threat, but today it just feels warm.

I am holding her hand in public, right out in the open where anyone with two eyes and half an agenda could see, and the sky hasn’t fallen yet. 

The primary bone-market square in the Ribcage district is a riot of noise and color, a desperate, defiant celebration of life built directly over the grave of a dead god. Above us, the massive, curving arches of the Titan's ribs filter the harsh noon sunlight into long, pale slashes across the cobblestones. Tents made of dyed canvas—sunflower yellow, bruised purple, deep crimson—crowd against each other like blooming tumors. The air is thick with the smell of roasting marrow, heavily spiced wine, and the sharp, sweaty perfume of ten thousand mortals trying to forget they are mortal for an afternoon.

It is the perfect mask for everything we found yesterday.

"I still think we should have brought a larger coin purse," Lysa says, her voice bright enough to cut through the din of haggling merchants and street lutes. 

She looks different today. The stiff, imposing Tribunal robes are gone, replaced by a soft, loose tunic and dark leggings that allow her to move without the bureaucratic drag of her office. Her hair is down, catching the intermittent sunlight as we walk. She looks younger. She looks exactly like the woman who held me together last night when I was coming apart at the seams.

"My lady," I reply, leaning in close enough that the breath of my words brushes her ear. "If we had brought more coin, Pila would have successfully sold you that 'authentic' god-bone paperweight that looked suspiciously like a cow's femur."

Lysa laughs, a sharp, genuine sound that sends a warm ripple of pure affection straight down the empathic link still connecting us. It isn’t a magical hum today; it’s just the quiet, baseline knowledge that she is happy, and therefore, for the first time in centuries, so am I. 

We navigate the crush of the crowd, our shoulders brushing. I guide her toward a bustling vendor’s stall marked by a sputtering brazier. The vendor, a woman with hands scarred from hot sugar and a smile missing three teeth, practically shoves two clay cups of spiced wine toward us before I’ve even pulled out a silver pin. 

I pay her, taking the cups and handing one to Lysa. The wine is cheap, the spices loud and crude enough to strip paint off a hull, but it burns going down in a way that feels intensely alive. 

We step out of the main thoroughfare and carve out a quiet moment beside a bone-fountain. The water cycling through the porous, calcified structure is relatively clean, bubbling up with a low, rhythmic gargle. I lean against the smooth stone edge, deliberately reaching out to catch Lysa's free hand again. I don't hide the gesture. I weave my fingers through hers, my thumb tracing the faint ink-stains permanently settled into her knuckles.

"Thank you," she says softly, looking down at our joined hands.

"For the terrible wine, or for not buying the cow femur?"

She glances up, her eyes crinkling. "For today. For being here."

A pang of something entirely human tightens my chest. I raise her hand and press a kiss to the back of it, my eyes never leaving hers. "I am exactly where I want to be."

"Scribe Lysa! Relic Cyran!"

The shout breaks the moment. A few stalls down, Merchant Pila is waving enthusiastically over a towering stack of woven charms and dubious baubles. Her bright orange headscarf acts like a beacon in the crowd. 

Lysa sighs, but she’s smiling. We walk over, allowing the current of the crowd to pull us. 

"I have exactly what you need," Pila declares before we even reach the counter. She thrusts a small, intricate knot of red silk and pale bone toward Lysa. "Freshly woven this morning. For protection, and..." Pila drops her voice to a theatrical whisper and winks broadly at me, "...fertility. The energies are very... aligned today, yes?"

Lysa blushes a brilliant, furious red, coughing on her mouthful of spiced wine. "Pila, I assure you, we are just—"

"I'll take it," I interrupt smoothly, dropping a coin onto the counter. I pocket the charm with a practiced, devastating smile that earns another delighted wink from Pila. "One can never have too much protection. Or alignment."

Lysa elbows me hard in the ribs as we walk away, but she doesn't order me to throw it out. 

Ahead of us, a circle has opened in the crowd for a group of street performers. They are juggling globes of low-grade alchemical fire, the flames trailing neon green and violent purple through the dusty air. I stop, letting Lysa step slightly in front of me to get a better look.

I watch her watch the fire. The joy on her face is so open, so unshielded. It is a terrible, beautiful vulnerability. I want to build a wall around her and burn the rest of the city down so she never has to stop smiling.

And then, it happens.

It isn't a sound. It isn't a thought.

It is a sharp, brutal tug deep in the meat of my chest, just behind my sternum.

Like a cold iron hook snagging directly on my heart.

The breath is dragged from my lungs. My vision flashes stark white, then pitches into blinding black. The festival noise cuts out, replaced by a low, suffocating hum that resonates in the marrow of my bones.

*Serve.*

The word doesn't come from my ears. It echoes inside my skull, wrapped in the heavy, cloying scent of temple incense. 

Varos.

My hand spasms involuntarily, crushing the clay cup of wine in my grip. Shards of pottery slice into my palm; hot, spiced wine spills over my knuckles, mingling instantly with my own blood. I forcefully lock my knees to keep from dropping to the cobblestones. My heart hammers a frantic, erratic rhythm against the invisible hook pulling at it.

Lysa notices the sudden stiffening of my posture. She turns, her smile faltering when she sees my ruined cup and bleeding hand. 

"Cyran? Are you alright? You're bleeding."

I force my lungs to work. I swallow the iron-taste of panic rising in my throat. I push the "ornament" smile onto my face—a perfect, flawless mask of unbothered charm that I haven't worn with her since the alley. 

"I'm fine," I say smoothly. "Just clumsy. The clay was brittle." I flick the shards onto the ground, ignoring the sharp sting as the cuts on my palm begin to knit themselves back together with preternatural speed. 

She isn't entirely convinced, her gaze darting from my hand to my eyes, but the empathic link is closed. I mentally slammed the heavy door on it the second the hook caught my chest. I cannot let her feel this. I cannot let her know that Varos is literally pulling my strings from across the city. 

"Are you hungry?" she asks, a slight furrow in her brow.

"Famished," I deflect, leaning closer and letting my voice drop into a lower, suggestive register. "But I suspect eating a merchant would ruin the atmosphere."

She rolls her eyes, the tension breaking slightly. "Come on. I saw Cairn and Seraph near the tavern tents."

She takes my uninjured hand again. Her touch is warm, grounding, but underneath my ribs, that cold hook remains, dragging against my heart with every step I take. 

We spot them across the square at an outdoor table under a battered canvas awning. Cairn is aggressively wiping down the splintered wood, glaring at anyone who looks like they might order a drink. Seraph is sitting opposite him, dressed in his usual extravagant, glittering stage fabrics—sapphire velvet and gold embroidery that look utterly ridiculous in the Ribcage daylight. 

As we approach, Seraph spots us. He waves flamboyantly, his heavily lined eyes sparkling.

"Well, well. If it isn't the law and the outlaw," Seraph purrs as we reach the table. He leans back, looking me up and down with an unvarnished, appreciative gaze. Usually, it’s a game. Usually, I play back, matching his flirtation parry for parry.

Today, my tongue is heavy. I don't feel like a person; I feel like a dog waiting for the leash to snap tight again. 

I just nod, a tight, curt gesture.

Seraph’s smile slips precisely a quarter-inch. His hyper-observant eyes narrow, dissecting the rigidity in my shoulders.

Cairn doesn’t look up from his rag. He reaches under the makeshift bar and unceremoniously shoves two heavy tin plates onto the table. They are piled high with charred, oily roasted tubers. 

"Eat it or don't," Cairn grates out. "I'm not running a charity. If you sit, you consume."

"Charming as always, Cairn," Lysa says, pulling out a rickety stool and sitting down. 

I remain standing for a moment longer, staring at the grease pooling at the edge of the tin plate. My stomach churns. I am a predator built to consume blood and violence; the idea of eating roasted roots while a high priest plucks at the magical tether in my soul is physically revolting.

But Lysa is watching me.

I sit. I pick up a wooden fork. I spear a piece of the tuber and bring it to my mouth. It tastes like dry dust and despair. 

I force it down.

"So," Seraph says lightly, resting his chin on his hands. "You two look remarkably unbroken for people who were neck-deep in the Archives yesterday. Did you find what you were looking for?"

"We found enough," Lysa answers vaguely, politically. She is enjoying the food, entirely oblivious to the fact that I am actively fighting a magical coronary across the table.

Then, the second tug hits. 

It is violent. A sharp, physical yank that steals my breath entirely. My vision shears sideways. The wooden fork snaps between my fingers with a loud crack.

The fragment falls to the table. 

Silence descends on our little corner. 

Cairn finally looks up, his dark eyes instantly assessing the threat level. Lysa's hand shoots out, landing warmly on my knee.

"Cyran?" Her voice is sharp with genuine alarm now. 

But it is Seraph who understands.

Seraph leans forward, his glittering, theatrical persona dropping away entirely. His eyes lock onto the collar of my shirt, specifically the pale scars of the temple sigils that are hidden from view. He knows what a magical binding feels like. He wears a physical collar; mine is just carved into my immortal soul. 

"Old chains pulling tight, darling?" Seraph asks, his voice barely a whisper, empty of all flirtation. It is a question from one slave to another.

A new, colder terror spikes through me. Not just the pain of the magic, but the absolute, crushing realization that Varos is waking up the Prime Contract. He is testing the tethers. Distance means nothing. The walls of the temple mean nothing. 

I am not free. I never was. I just had a really long leash.

Lysa’s warm hand on my knee feels like a brand. I look at her—at her fierce, lovely face, at the concern etching lines around her eyes. 

I am a danger to her. As long as Varos holds my leash, I am a weapon pointed directly at the woman I love. The self-loathing I thought I had banished last night roars back, a tidal wave of toxic shame. I ruin everyone I touch. I dragged her into the mud, and now I am going to be the thing that destroys her.

I stand up abruptly, the stool scraping harshly against the cobblestones. 

"I need to check in," I lie, the words tasting like copper. "With a Vigil patrol. I saw one pass the perimeter. I should... ensure our route back is clear."

"Cyran, no, wait—" Lysa starts to rise.

"Stay," I say, too sharply. I soften it instantly with a devastating, empty smile. "Enjoy the wine, my lady. I will return before you finish the plate."

I don't wait for her reply. I turn and walk away, forcing my stride to remain even, relaxed, predatory. 

I make it behind the heavy canvas of a nearby spice tent before my knees finally give out. I hit the alley wall hard, my shoulder taking the brunt of the impact. I curl inward, violently rubbing the center of my chest, digging my knuckles into my own sternum as if I can massage the cursed hook away. 

*Serve. Come Home.*

The temple magic hums, sick and sweet in my blood. 

I gasp for air, fighting the dissociation, fighting the overwhelming urge to just surrender to the command and walk back into the cage. 

I force my eyes open, staring through the narrow gap between two tents, looking back toward the tavern table. I need to see her. I need her to anchor me.

I spot Lysa. She is looking in the direction I left, her brow furrowed in worry. 

And then, my gaze shifts.

Standing in the deep shadow of the bone-fountain we had just vacated fifty feet away, entirely motionless amidst the swirling festival crowd, is a figure in the heavy, black-and-silver armor of the Tribunal elite. 

Inquisitor Dren.

He isn't looking at me. He isn't looking at the crowd.

He is staring directly at Lysa.

The cold dread in my veins instantly crystallizes into militant, terrifying clarity. 

This isn't a coincidence. Varos isn't just testing my leash. He is coordinating with Thessen. They are pulling me away to isolate her. The Tribunal is hunting her, and the High Priest is pinning me down so I can't stop them. 

The tug hits my chest a third time, agonizingly hard. 

I slump against the wall, the festival noise roaring in my ears like the ocean as the jaws of the trap snap shut around us.
