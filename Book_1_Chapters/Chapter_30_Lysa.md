# Chapter 30 — *The Archivist's Warning*
### Lysa | POV

The Halo Ward Archives smell like dust, paranoia, and secrets that want to stay buried.

It is a massive, circular library built directly into the hollowed-out skull cavity of a lesser god. Towering shelves of brittle bone and dark wood curve up into the darkness, spiraling out of sight like the ribs of a leviathan. Shafts of pale, late-morning light filter through the high, narrow ocular windows, illuminating millions of dust motes dancing in the dry, stuffy air. 

If I am going to break the law, I might as well consult the man who memorized it.

I navigate the labyrinthine stacks, moving past exactly eighty-four rows of municipal ledgers and property disputes, heading directly for the Restricted Arcanum section in the back of the skull. My boots are loud on the polished floor, but I am moving too fast to care about librarians. 

I round the final towering shelf and stop. 

Archivist Solen is sitting cross-legged on the floor in the center of the restricted aisle, surrounded by a precarious, chaotic half-circle of ancient ledgers. He looks like a man actively weathering a hurricane of parchment. 

He is wearing oversized, dusty archivist robes, the cuffs permanently stained black with ink. He is young—perhaps my age—but he has the frantic, twitchy energy of a man who knows entirely too much about how the world ends and hasn't slept since he figured it out. He is absentmindedly chewing on the feathered end of a dry quill while squinting at a crumbling scroll spread across his knees.

"Archivist Solen," I say sharply. 

Solen jumps, nearly upsetting a terrifyingly tall stack of leather-bound folios. He scrambles backward, pushing his wire-rimmed glasses up his nose. 

"The Arcanum is closed," Solen stammers rapidly, waving a hand at the empty air between us. "It's restricted. Tribunal authorization only. I am currently engaged in delicate translations of pre-Founder civic structural runes, and I cannot be interrupted unless the Ribcage is actively collapsing, and even then, I would prefer a written warning."

"My name is Lysa. I am—I was—an auditor for the Spine District," I say, stepping fully into the aisle. "I need everything you have on High Priest Varos, the Prime Contract, and the Heart-Node ritual. Now."

Solen flinches violently at the mention of the Heart-Node. He scrambles to his feet, clutching the scroll to his chest. 

"Absolutely not," Solen says, his voice dropping to an anxious whisper as he looks around the empty stacks. "That is Class-A restricted theology. Audit or not, you need a Contract Lord's signature just to look at the index."

I slam my hand down on the nearest wooden reading desk. The sharp crack echoes loudly up into the skull cavity. 

"Contract Lord Thessen backed the deal that handed a Vigil warden over to Varos this morning," I say, my voice vibrating with absolute, militant fury. "The Tribunal is compromised. Thessen doesn't care if Varos burns the city down as long as he gets to hold the matches. I need to know how to stop the binding."

Solen stops twitching. He looks at me properly for the first time. His eyes drop to my civilian clothes, lingering on the heavy canvas jacket covering the spot where my silver Tribunal badge used to be pinned over my heart. 

He sees the rogue in me. He sees the sheer, unadulterated desperation radiating off me.

Solen sighs, a long, weary sound that makes him sound a hundred years old. He tosses the chewed quill onto the desk. 

"The Tribunal has always been compromised," Solen mutters darkly. He turns to the shelf behind him, standing on his toes to reach the uppermost tier. "But Varos isn't building a bonfire. He's building a bomb."

He pulls down three ancient, crumbling treatises—their bindings made of cracked, yellowed bone—and drops them onto the desk. A cloud of ancient dust puffs into the air. 

"The Heart-Node isn't just a ritual," Solen says, speaking in rapid-fire bursts as he flips the heavy pages, his eyes darting across the archaic text. "It’s a hardwire into the Prime Contract. The dead god isn't dead, Scribe. Not entirely. The Contract acts as its nervous system, maintaining the magical equilibrium of the city. Varos wants to control that nervous system."

"How?" I demand, leaning over the book. 

"He needs an anchor," Solen replies, tracing a jagged line of text. "A soul capable of acting as a conduit between mortal will and divine magic. If Cyran is fully bound to the Node, he won’t just be Varos’s slave. He will become a battery. Every ounce of suffering, every magical fluctuation in the city, will pass through him. It will burn his mind to ash within a year, but while he lasts, Varos will wield the power of a minor deity."

My stomach drops. Cyran hasn't just marched back into his old cage; he has marched into a cosmic meatgrinder. 

"There has to be a way to stop it," I say. "Can I break the physical wards on the Cisterns?"

"No," Solen says flatly, grabbing a piece of scrap paper and a stick of charcoal. He begins sketching rapidly. "The Blood Cisterns are old magic. If you try to smash the altar with a hammer, the wards will liquefy your organs. Violence won't work."

"Then I need a legal weapon," I insist. 

As Solen sketches out the jagged layout of the Cisterns, his oversized sleeve slips down slightly. For a split second, I see something glowing faintly on the skin of his forearm. It is a strange, triangular glyph—sharp, angular, and pulsing with a strange, cool blue light that doesn't look like any temple magic I've ever seen.

Solen notices my gaze and aggressively yanks his sleeve back down, his face flushing. He immediately focuses back on the charcoal map. 

I file the strange glowing mark away. The Archivist clearly has his own terrifying secrets, but right now, I only care about the one that can save Cyran. 

"This is the Heart-Chamber," Solen says, pointing the charcoal at a large circle in the center of the jagged lines. "The ritual must take place at the exact apex of the lunar alignment. High noon. That’s in exactly forty-seven minutes."

"I can make it," I say. "But how do I stop the binding once I'm in?"

Solen pushes his glasses up again. He heaves the largest of the three bone-bound ledgers to the center of the desk. He opens it to the final few pages. The text is written in the sharp, archaic script of the original founders of the Ribcage. 

"The Prime Contract is a literal mechanism," Solen explains, his finger resting on a dense paragraph. "It obeys the rules of its own creation. Magic here is just civic law written in blood. Section Four, Article Nine. The Founder's Clause."

He traces the words, translating rapidly from the archaic text. "The Prime Contract cannot bind a soul that was coerced by a mortal proxy. Consent must be absolute. If the proposed anchor refuses consent, verbally and explicitly during the final incantation of the binding... the contract rejects the proxy. It creates a magical feedback loop that shatters the Node."

I stare at the text. "There's a flaw, Solen. Cyran *did* consent. He agreed to the deal to secure my pardon from the dungeons."

Solen smiles. It is a grim, entirely un-librarian-like expression.

"He consented under the threat of your torture," Solen says softly, tapping the ancient page. "I read the audit codes, Lysa. The threat of violence against a bonded companion constitutes *duress* under Section Four of the Old Laws. The deal Varos made is legally invalid."

The sheer brilliance of the archaic loophole hits me like a lightning strike. 

It is the exact legal framework I had been searching for in this very archive weeks ago, before Cyran caught me reading his files. The law that enslaved him is the exact same law I am going to use to break his chains.

Without a word of warning, Archivist Solen grips the edge of the priceless, ancient page detailing the Founder's Clause. 

With a sharp, horrific tearing sound, he rips the page completely out of the binding. 

For a scribe or an archivist, destroying a primary ledger is supreme heresy. It is a crime punishable by exile. Solen doesn't even blink. He folds the brittle, heavy parchment and shoves it directly into my hands. 

"If you reach the altar before the high noon bell," Solen says, his eyes wide and frantic behind his glasses. "You stand in front of the High Priest and you read this aloud. You bring the law down on his head. It invokes an Audit of the Soul."

I clutch the torn page to my chest, tucking it inside my canvas jacket, flush against my ribs. It feels infinitely heavier than the seventy-four-page appeal I carried yesterday. 

"And if it fails?" I ask, my voice incredibly quiet. "If the feedback loop doesn't shatter the Node?"

Solen pushes his glasses up his nose, the nervous twitch returning in full force. "Then the Prime Contract eats you both, Varos becomes a god, and I have to find a new hiding place in a different district."

I look down at the charcoal map on the desk. Solen has drawn a secondary path—a jagged line bypassing the main guarded entrances to the Cisterns. 

"The old capillary tunnels," I note, tracing the route. 

"A tight squeeze, but unwarded," Solen confirms. He reaches under the desk and hauls up a massive, iron-bound volume. It must weigh twenty pounds. He pushes it across the wood toward me. 

"What is this?" I ask.

"A glossary of obsolete taxation laws from the second century," Solen says practically. "For hitting people. Just in case the reading doesn't work right away."

A tight, dark smirk breaks across my face. The crushing despair of the morning is entirely gone. I am locked in. 

"Keep your head down, Solen," I say, stepping back from the desk. 

Solen waves me off, already diving his hands back into the pile of scrolls, his eyes locked onto a text involving triangular geometric warding. "Just don't get eaten by the floor, Scribe."

I don't reply. I grab the charcoal map, secure the torn page over my heart, and burst out of the Restricted Arcanum. 

I break into a dead sprint through the stacks, my boots hammering against the polished bone floor. I burst through the heavy double doors of the Archives and out into the gray, overcast daylight of the city. 

The high noon bell is less than forty minutes away. 

I am going to break the Prime Contract, or I am going to burn with it.
