# Chapter 30 — *The Heart That Eats*
### Cyran | POV

Victory should taste like wine.

This tastes like copper.

The safe house Lysa found us is a converted scribe’s cell carved into one of the lower vertebrae—a narrow room with a warped bone floor, a single shuttered slit of a window, and a hearth that smokes more than it warms. The air smells of damp, old ink, and the faint, permanent tang of bone dust.

It smells like home, for her.

For me, it smells like hiding.

The fire pops.

A coal collapses in on itself, sending up a brief spray of orange sparks that dance in the draft before winking out. Shadows swell and contract on the walls in time with the flame, turning the stacked trunks and bundled scrolls into lurching silhouettes.

I sit on the floor in front of the hearth, back to the rickety chair, elbows on my knees.

The scrap of parchment burns colder in my fingers than any metal.

Thessen’s last little secret.

The ink has dried to a dark brown, almost black. Numbers march across it in neat columns, every digit a tiny act of aggression. At the bottom, half eaten by the tear, curls the sigil.

A circle.

Hooks and angles biting inward.

It feels like it’s looking at me.

Behind the closed door to the adjoining room, Lysa sleeps.

Or is at least lying down and pretending to.

I bullied her into it.

After the Tribunal, after the stairs, after the Vein Markets lunch where I impressed her with my capacity to inhale an entire loaf of bread and an alarming amount of cheap stew, we spent hours in Scribe Meris’s shadowed stall, trading favors we don’t yet know how to pay for slivers of information about the Nameless Creditor.

Names that weren’t names.

Ledgers that were more redacted than readable.

Hints.

Threads.

By the time we staggered here, the circles under her eyes were deep enough to drown in.

“You take the bed,” I told her. “I’ll take the floor.”

She started to argue.

Of course she did.

“You’re still healing,” she said. “The burn—”

“Is better,” I lied.

She gave me a look that said she knew I was lying and also that we didn’t have time to dissect it.

“The floor,” I repeated. “I’ve slept in worse places.”

“In worse company, too, I imagine,” she said, lips twitching.

“Undoubtedly,” I said. “I’m trying to improve my standards.”

That earned me a small huff of amusement.

And, eventually, her reluctant agreement.

The door clicked shut behind her with a softness that felt like trust.

I lasted almost a full five minutes before pulling the parchment out again.

Now, in the flickering half‑dark, the sigil’s lines seem to shift.

Not dramatically.

No theatrical swirl of ink, no glow.

Just… a subtle wrongness at the edge of my sight.

As if the curves aren’t quite where I remember them being a heartbeat ago.

“You are being paranoid,” I inform myself.

The parchment does not respond.

I turn it between my fingers.

The sigil comes to rest upright.

I think.

The hooks bite inward.

The circle closes.

The memory hits like a sudden drop in elevation.

Not a specific moment.

A sensation.

The first time the Prime Contract ever noticed me.

Not through priests’ mouths or donors’ demands, but directly—raw god‑pressure pressing down on my bones, turning my blood to static, whispering in a language older than speech.

*Mine.*

The Heart‑Chamber thrummed then the way the city does in a storm.

Tonight, the hum is smaller.

Concentrated.

Right here.

The hairs on my arms rise.

I wet my lips.

“Hello?” I say.

Because apparently I’ve reached the point in my life where I talk to paper in empty rooms.

The air doesn’t stir.

The hearth keeps hissing, the fire chewing slowly through its meager fuel.

But something… *else*… listens.

I can feel it.

A pressure at the base of my skull, like the beginning of a headache. A heavy weight sliding down the back of my neck, along my spine, settling in the hollow just behind my sternum where my not‑quite‑a‑heart remembers how to beat when it’s convenient.

My breath shortens.

Not entirely by choice.

Old scripts light up in my muscles.

Kneel.

Bare throat.

Present wrists.

I stay where I am.

Elbows on knees.

Parchment between my fingers.

“I am not yours,” I say.

My voice shakes.

I hate that.

The sigil throbs.

Only once.

Like a pulse.

The room dissolves.

Not dramatically.

No swirl of color, no ripping sound.

One blink there is smoke and bone and bad furniture.

The next there is… nothing.

No floor.

No walls.

No up, no down.

Just a vast, depthless black that swallows sound before it’s born.

I know dream logic when I feel it.

Nightmares have rules.

This one doesn’t.

The parchment is still in my hand.

Or I think it is.

Hard to tell when you can’t see your own body.

I look down.

Down doesn’t exist.

But there is the faintest suggestion of shape where my hands should be.

Pale, like bone.

Moving slowly as if underwater.

“Ah,” I say.

Or think I say.

The word hangs in front of me, luminous for a heartbeat before winking out.

“Is this about your customer satisfaction survey? Because I would rate your last ritual a resounding *do not recommend*.”

Silence answers.

Then—

A point of not‑dark opens ahead.

Not light.

An absence of absence.

It widens.

Unfurls.

Becomes an eye.

Not anatomical.

Not iris and pupil.

A concept of sight.

A circle of impossible geometry, lined with tiny, shifting sigils that rearrange themselves faster than I can follow. Looking at it feels like trying to read a contract that’s being redrafted mid‑sentence.

Pressure spikes in my chest.

Like a fist closing around whatever passes for my heart.

I can’t breathe.

Which is ridiculous.

I don’t need to.

But panic is an old reflex.

The… thing… regards me.

Not with curiosity.

With assessment.

Asset evaluation.

A voice—not sound, not word, but meaning shoved directly into the soft meat of my mind—speaks.

ASSET REALLOCATED.

The impact of it makes me flinch.

“Stop doing that,” I manage.

The words feel dragged out of me.

I try to step back.

No ground.

No body.

Only the sensation of retreat.

The eye does not move.

YOU BROKE THE TOOL, the voice presses. THE DEBT REMAINS.

Images slam into me.

Not memories.

Possibilities.

Thessen on his knees in the Heart‑Chamber, chains made of clauses wrapped around his wrists. Varos standing by, hands folded, a supplicant and a handler both. Behind them, a shadow the size of a city, made of slips of paper and sigils and little red marks on necks and wrists.

Contracts.

All of them.

Layered a thousand thick.

Each one a string tied to some poor bastard’s life.

All anchored to a single black point that eats light.

A creditor.

Nameless.

Bottomless.

Thessen falls.

The chains go slack.

The strings snap.

For a moment, it looks like freedom.

Then every broken end whips around.

Finds a new anchor.

Me.

The sense of weight slamming into my chest nearly knocks me to my knees.

If there were knees.

“Return them,” I choke.

Utterly pointless.

But I say it anyway.

“This debt isn’t mine.”

DEBT IS INHERITABLE, the pressure says. TERMS SPECIFIED IN INITIAL AGREEMENT.

“If you’re quoting fine print at me, we are truly doomed,” I rasp. “I didn’t *sign* anything with you.”

YOU SIGNED WITH MY SERVANTS.

“And I killed them,” I snap.

The eye brightens.

Amusement.

Faint.

You killed THE HAND, it says. THE WRITER OF LOCAL LEDGERS. THE HEART THAT EATS STILL BEATS. THE CREDITOR STILL HOLDS THE NOTE.

“Then take it up with them,” I say. “I have retired from being a divine chew toy. You may refer all further inquiries to my legal representation.”

A flicker.

The suggestion of scales.

A quill.

Lysa, standing in the Tribunal Hall, badge bright on her wrist, jaw set.

They have noticed her too.

A colder fear slides under the bravado.

If this… thing… has followed the threads of Thessen’s debts to me, it can follow them to her.

“You leave her out of this,” I snarl.

The void doesn’t echo.

But the sentiment reverberates anyway.

NEW GUARANTOR IDENTIFIED, the voice presses. COLLATERAL NOTIFIED.

Something slams into my chest.

Not metaphor.

Not image.

AGREEMENT ENFORCED, the voice says.

The pain is instant and total.

White‑hot.

It spears through the center of me with such intensity that for a moment I don’t realize I’m screaming. There is no air, nothing to move, but the sound rips out of me regardless—a raw, hoarse thing that tears the not‑space into ragged strips.

I look down.

My body is there now.

Or a version of it.

Pale skin.

Old scars.

And over the left side of my chest, where a heart should be, a brand burns itself into existence.

Lines.

Hooks.

A circle that tightens as I watch, searing deeper.

The sigil from the parchment.

Bigger.

Darker.

Carved into flesh instead of ink.

I claw at it.

My fingers pass through.

The pain doesn’t.

“I am no one’s asset,” I shout.

The words taste like blood.

They feel like Lysa’s hand at my back, pushing me to my feet in the Heart‑Chamber. Like her voice in my ear.

*No one owns you anymore. Not if we can help it.*

The eye narrows.

REFUSAL NOTED, it says.

The pressure spikes again.

BLACK MARK APPLIED.

ACTIVE ACCOUNT.

DEBT IS ETERNAL.

“Then so is my spite,” I grind out.

For a second, just one, I feel something else under the crushing weight.

Not divine.

Not bureaucratic.

Warmth.

A hand, small and human, closing around mine.

Ink‑stained fingers.

Lysa.

Not physically.

Not here.

But the memory of her refusal to let a system define me burns hotter than the brand.

I cling to it.

“Get. Out.” I snarl.

The void shudders.

For a heartbeat, the eye flickers.

Not in fear.

Not yet.

In interest.

Amusement sharpens.

CONTESTED, the voice says.

Then it drops.

The pressure vanishes.

The darkness tears.

I hit the floor.

Real floor.

Bone.

Hard enough that my teeth click together and my vision whites out for a second with the impact. Air slams back into my lungs in a ragged gasp that sounds too loud in the small room.

The fire is low now.

Mostly ember.

Smoke stings my eyes.

The parchment scrap lies beside me, its edges singed, the sigil dark and quiet.

My chest—

I can’t breathe properly.

Pain throbs there, deep and wrong.

I scrabble at my shirt, fingers fumbling at the fastenings.

“Cyran!”

Lysa’s voice knifes through the fog.

The door crashes open.

She’s there an instant later, dropping to her knees beside me, hair mussed from sleep, robe half‑tied, eyes wide and bright in the dim.

Her hands hover over me.

Not touching.

At first.

“What happened?” she demands. “You screamed. The wards—”

“There are wards?” I croak.

“Minimal,” she says. “Enough to yell at me when someone tries to rip a hole in reality in the next room. *What happened?*”

I force my fingers to cooperate.

Buttons give under my hands.

I yank the shirt open.

Her gaze drops.

Her breath catches.

Over my heart, the skin is not just bruised.

Not just reddened.

It’s marked.

A jagged, black sigil, lines still faintly smoking as if the flesh only just finished sealing around them. The circle bites inward. The hooks cling.

Exactly like the one on the parchment.

Only bigger.

Deeper.

Wrong.

“It marked me,” I whisper.

My voice sounds very small.

Very young.

I hate it.

Lysa doesn’t flinch.

She leans in.

Her face is very calm in the low light.

Too calm.

She looks, for a moment, exactly like she does in hearings when confronted with something appalling.

Clinical.

Focused.

She reaches out.

“May I?” she asks.

Consent.

Even now.

I nod.

Words are beyond me.

Her fingertips brush the edge of the mark.

I brace for pain.

There is none.

Not from her touch.

The brand itself is… cold.

Not chill like winter air.

Cold like deep space.

Like the absence of heat.

Her brows draw together.

“It’s—”

“Not natural,” I say.

“Nothing about you is natural,” she says automatically, then shakes her head. “It doesn’t feel like the Prime Contract. Different… texture.”

“New management,” I say.

The joke falls flat.

She looks up at me.

Eyes sharp.

“Who?” she asks.

“The Creditor,” I say.

The word sits badly in my mouth.

“He— it— found me. Through the sigil. Through Thessen’s debts. The Prime Contract was… just one of its pet projects. We killed a middleman. The account remains open.”

Her jaw tightens.

“What did it say?” she asks.

“Asset reallocated,” I say. “Thessen void. You active. Debt eternal. All the greatest hits.”

Her hand curls into a fist against my chest, just above the mark.

Not hard enough to hurt.

Just… anchoring.

“You are not an asset,” she says.

The words are quiet.

Precise.

“You are not a debt. You are not collateral. You are Cyran. You are a person. And whoever or whatever that thing is, it does not get to own you.”

“I admire your optimism,” I say.

Somehow, I can still manage a half‑smile.

She doesn’t return it.

Not yet.

“There’s more,” she says, reading my face like parchment.

“Of course there is,” I say. “He implied you’re on the books too. Collateral notified, I believe was the phrase. Congratulations, love, you’ve been upgraded from independent Auditor to co‑signer on a cosmic catastrophe.”

Her expression doesn’t change.

Not much.

The crease between her brows deepens.

“That tracks,” she says.

I blink.

“Does it,” I say.

“I stole the Blood Ledger,” she reminds me. “I threatened a Contract Lord with precedent old enough to set the Prime Contract’s teeth on edge. I helped dismantle one of its favorite temples. I publicly voided its claim on you. Of course it noticed me.”

“You are taking this remarkably well,” I say.

“What is the alternative?” she asks, lifting one eyebrow. “Panic? Run? Pretend it isn’t happening? That’s your coping style, not mine.”

“Rude,” I say.

“Accurate,” she says.

Her fingers uncurl.

She smooths my shirt back over my chest, covering the mark.

Not hiding it for herself.

Shielding it, somehow.

“My job,” she says slowly, “used to be to identify the origin and legality of contracts. This is a contract. Just… bigger. Older. Nastier. Someone, somewhere, put your name on a line you did not see. Our task is the same as it always was: find the original document and set it on fire.”

I huff out something that might be a laugh.

It hurts.

“Metaphorically,” she amends. “Probably. Depending how flammable gods are feeling.”

“I have tried the literal approach,” I say. “Limited success. Spectacular visuals, though.”

She almost smiles.

Almost.

Then her eyes sharpen again.

“Look at me,” she says.

I do.

Her gaze is steady.

“You fought it,” she says. “I can see it in your eyes. In your body. Old you would have dropped straight to your knees and begged. New you snarled at a cosmic loan shark that you have spite. That’s progress.”

“Such low bars we set for ourselves,” I murmur.

“Survival is a series of low bars,” she says. “We clear enough of them, we get to set higher ones. You did not choose this mark. But you get to choose what you do with it. With *him*. With them.”

“Ah,” I say. “And what do you suggest, Auditor emerita?”

She considers the question for all of half a breath.

“Then we kill him too,” she says.

There is no tremor in it.

No humor.

Just a statement of intent, as casual and absolute as if she’d said *we file an appeal*.

I stare at her.

“Lysa,” I say carefully. “This is not a priest or a Contract Lord or a crooked Inquisitor. This is… I don’t even have a word. An institution made sentient. A debt with a mind. The Nameless Creditor. People whisper about it in footnotes and then refuse to admit they did. You are suggesting we kill a concept.”

“Yes,” she says.

“You are aware that is insane,” I say.

“Possibly,” she says. “So was investigating the Redemption system from the inside. So was falling in love with a Temple Relic. So was walking into the Heart‑Chamber with you when everyone told me it was suicide. I appear to have a type.”

“Terrible decisions in good coats?” I offer.

“Traumatized immortals and systems no one else wants to touch,” she corrects.

“Flattering,” I say.

“True,” she says.

She sits back on her heels.

Her hand stays over my chest.

Over the mark.

“As far as I can tell,” she says, “this thing made a mistake tonight.”

“Oh?” I ask.

“Yes,” she says. “It told us it exists. It showed us its signature. It marked you in a way we can now see and study. It thinks that makes you easier to own. It’s wrong. It makes you easier to track—*backwards*.”

“You think we can follow the thread from me to it,” I say.

“I think that’s what ledgers are for,” she says. “Follow the money. Follow the magic. Find the original note. And then… we see if debts older than gods can be voided.”

The enormity of it hangs between us.

It should crush me.

It doesn’t.

Maybe because I am already lying on the floor.

Maybe because the worst has already happened to me more than once, and I am, against all odds, still here.

Maybe because there is a woman in a wrinkled robe kneeling over me with smoke in her hair and fury in her eyes, offering war as casually as other people offer comfort.

A sound bubbles up in me.

Not quite a laugh.

Not quite a sob.

Somewhere between.

I catch her wrist.

My hand fits around it easily.

Her pulse beats under my fingers.

Rapid.

Steady.

“Partners, then,” I say.

“To the point of cosmic treason,” she says.

“Darling,” I murmur, a smile creeping in despite the burn in my chest, “I knew there was a reason I liked you.”

She rolls her eyes.

“Up,” she says. “You’re on the floor.”

“Very observant,” I say.

“Also, you look terrible,” she adds. “We need you not half‑dead if we’re going to take on an eldritch accounting error.”

She braces herself.

Offers me her hand.

I take it.

She pulls.

I let her.

My muscles protest.

The new mark throbs.

The room tilts at a sick angle, then settles as my feet find the bone floor.

She is close enough that I can see ash smudged along her jaw, the faint mark on her throat where my mouth was last night, the tiny furrow between her brows that never entirely leaves.

I lean in.

Just enough.

Rest my forehead against hers for a second.

Her breath ghosts across my lips.

“We are in so far over our heads the ribs might as well be the floor,” I murmur.

“Yes,” she says.

“And yet,” I add.

“And yet,” she agrees.

Her hand slides down.

Covers the mark again.

Claiming.

Defying.

“We start small,” she says. “Tomorrow. We go back to Meris. We find every reference to this sigil in the archives. We map the pattern. We identify local agents. We make this… Creditor… bleed, contract by contract.”

“You are aware gods don’t technically bleed,” I say.

“Everything bleeds somewhere,” she says. “We just have to find where to cut.”

The brand aches.

But under the ache, under the lingering terror of that void and its eye and its voice, there is something I did not expect to feel ever again.

Excitement.

Horrified, exhausted, utterly unreasonable excitement.

They have marked me.

Claimed me.

Again.

This time, I am not walking into the cage alone.

I look at Lysa.

At her ink‑stained fingers and stubborn mouth and the way she doesn’t even pretend to consider backing down.

A slow, dangerous smile curls my lips.

“Very well, love,” I say.

I cover her hand with mine.

Squeeze.

“Yes,” I murmur. “We kill them all.”

The fire gutters.

Somewhere far below the city, the dead god’s heart stutters in its cage.

Above us, beyond ribs and law and bone, something vast and nameless shifts its attention.

For the first time in a very long time, it has a fight on its hands.
