# Chapter 25 — *The Leverage*
### Cyran | POV

I am going to paint the bone-white walls of the Spine District red.

I am going to start with the guards at the gate, work my way up the central elevator shaft, and I am not going to stop until Inquisitor Dren’s head is separated from his shoulders and Lysa is walking out the front doors with me. 

"Cyran, stop!" Warden Vesper shouts, his voice raw. He has his hands planted squarely in the center of my chestplate, using the entirety of his considerable bulk to try and physically shove me back from the heavy oak door of the Vigil barracks armory. 

I snarl, the sound tearing out of my throat entirely inhuman. I grab Vesper’s wrists, intending to snap them or throw him across the room—I honestly don't care which. The Thirst is raging just beneath my skin, a violent, buzzing static that has erased every rational thought in my head. 

"Get out of my way, Vesper." The words are distorted, vibrating over the elongated edge of my fangs. The glamor is completely stripped from my eyes. The silver in my irises is practically blinding in the dim, practical oil light of the barracks.

I am strapped with enough weaponry to level a mid-sized merchant caravan. Twin bone-hilted daggers at my thighs. A short sword across my back. Throwing knives lining my bracers. They feel terrifyingly light. I need more. 

"If you walk out that door, you kill her!" Vesper roars, not backing down even as applying pressure to his forearms makes the bones in his wrists groan. "Cyran, listen to me! You hit the Tribunal halls, you validate every single charge in that warrant! They will execute her for treason before you are halfway up the shaft!"

I shove him backward. He stumbles, catching himself on a wooden rack of halberds. They rattle discordantly in the tense silence of the room.

"She is in the deep blocks, Vesper," I say, my breath hitching as panic claws at the underside of my ribs. "Do you understand what that means? They don't put scribes in the deep blocks. They put monsters there. If I don't go right now, they will—"

I choke on the words. I can't even say them. The image of Lysa, soft and completely unprotected, locked in the freezing rot of the dungeons, is actively destroying my mind. The news of her arrest had rolled into the barracks ten minutes ago, delivered casually by a junior watchman. It hadn't hit me like a fist. It had hit me like an executioner's blade, neatly severing my heart from my reason. 

"I know where she is," Vesper says, lowering his voice, holding his bruising wrists but keeping his eyes locked on mine. "And getting yourself killed in a blaze of glory doesn't get her out. You need a strategy, not a bloodbath. You need—"

The heavy oak door of the armory swings open.

The room goes dead silent. 

Standing in the doorway is a Temple Courier. He is a young man, dressed in the immaculate, infuriatingly serene white-and-gold silks of the High Priest's personal retinue. He looks entirely out of place amidst the sweat, gun oil, and sharpened steel of the Vigil barracks. 

He is holding a single envelope. Black parchment, sealed with a heavy, iridescent red wax.

The Courier doesn't look at Vesper. He looks directly at me, unaffected by the silver flare of my eyes or the weapons strapped to every inch of my body. 

"Asset," the Courier says smoothly, extending the envelope. 

I snatch it from his hand with enough force to nearly dislocate his shoulder. I rip the heavy wax seal. The parchment is thick and smells sickeningly of frankincense. The handwriting is elegant, flowing, and perfectly legible.

*I have the key to your scribe's cage. Come to the Cisterns.*
*—Varos*

The static in my head abruptly cuts out. 

The manic, violent panic vanishes, replaced by a sudden, absolute, freezing certainty. 

I lower the letter. The swords and daggers strapped to me suddenly feel incredibly heavy, incredibly useless. I look at Vesper. I see the grim realization dawn on his face as he reads my expression.

"Cyran," Vesper warns quietly. "Don't."

I walk past him. I don't run. I don't snarl. I walk out of the barracks and into the night, ignoring his calls behind me, the cold truth settling into the marrow of my bones.

Varos isn't pulling my strings anymore. He doesn't need to. He found the one thread holding me to the world, and he tied it to an anvil. 

***

The Blood Cisterns are a dark, cavernous sub-chamber hidden entirely beneath the Ribcage district. The massive, porous bone ceiling arches a hundred feet overhead, lost in the shadows. The floor is broken by vast, circular pools where the congealed, divine blood of the dead god collects. The pools give off a dull, pulsing red bioluminescence that paints the cavern in the color of a fresh wound. 

The air down here is incredibly thick—humid, suffocating, and reeking of ozone, old copper, and heavy temple incense. It feels exactly like walking into the lung of a massive, dying beast.

I emerge from the steep access tunnel, my boots hitting the slick, wet stone of the central walkway. 

High Priest Varos is waiting for me on the far side of the largest pool. 

He is surrounded by a dozen heavily augmented temple guards. Their armor is ceremonial gold plate, but the men wearing it are massive, their bodies warped by decades of ingesting refined god-blood. They are standing perfectly still, halberds at the ready.

Varos himself is draped in robes of profound crimson velvet. He wears heavy gold rings on every finger. He looks serene. Paternal. He does not look like a man who just engineered the destruction of an innocent woman’s life.

I stop twenty feet away. The heat of the god-blood pool radiates against my left side.

I draw a bone-hilted blade from my thigh. It whispers slickly against the leather scabbard. I don't posture with it. I hold it low, loose, ready to gut him. 

"Release her," I say. My voice is flat, devoid of any ornamentation. 

Varos smiles grandly. He extends both hands, palms up, as if offering an embrace. 

"Welcome home, my child," Varos says. The acoustics of the cavern catch his voice, wrapping it in a terrifying, booming echo. "I have missed your face in the temple."

"Drop the charges," I repeat, taking a single, slow step forward on the slick stone. "Have Thessen sign the release order, or I will cross this walkway and cut your throat before your guards can draw a breath."

Varos doesn't flinch. He clasps his hands in front of him, looking at me with deep, sickening pity. 

"You could," he concedes softly. "You are quite fast, Cyran. But Inquisitor Dren currently holds Scribe Lysa in cell block four of the deep Spine. I am told she has not been given water or a blanket. Furthermore..." Varos pauses, letting the silence hang heavy over the pulse of the blood pools. "Dren has requisitioned a bone-saw and a set of cranial calipers for her specialized interrogation regarding your… corruptive influence. The transfer to the interrogation chamber is scheduled for midnight."

I stop walking. 

The blade shakes in my hand. 

I stare at him, the horrific reality of his words crashing into me. A bone-saw. Cranial calipers. Tools meant to extract truth from feral monsters, applied to the soft, mortal flesh of a scribe. 

I look at the heavy gold rings on his fingers. I look at the dozen augmented guards. I look down at the dagger trembling in my grip. 

I am completely, utterly checkmated. 

I slowly lower the knife. My knees feel incredibly weak, as if the gravity in the caverns has suddenly doubled. The fight drains out of my posture, my shoulders slumping, the feral, protective rage dissolving into pure, unadulterated terror for her.

"What do you want?" The words scrape out of my throat, barely audible over the hum of the ritual pools. 

Varos steps forward. He walks to the very edge of the walkway.

"I want the same thing I have always wanted, Cyran," Varos says gently, reasonably. "I want to restore the balance of our city. I want to wake the Prime Contract fully. But to do that, the Heart-Node ritual requires an anchor. A soul capable of processing the immense, divine power of the Titan without immediately burning to ash. It requires a vessel that has spent centuries being perfectly conditioned to obey, and perfectly conditioned to survive pain."

He looks at me, his eyes shining with terrifying piety.

"It requires you. I want you back, Cyran. Not as a guard. Not as an ornament. As the conduit for our god."

I feel the old temple magic—the conditioning carved into my soul during a hundred years of slavery—hum in the humid air between us. I recognize the shape of the cage waiting for me. If I agree, I am not just returning to abuse. I am surrendering my autonomy forever. I will become a battery for the High Priest's madness. 

"If I come back," I whisper, looking down at the red reflection of the god-blood below the walkway. "If I surrender. You let her go."

"I will personally ensure Lord Thessen drops all charges," Varos promises smoothly. "Her record will be expunged. The broadsheets will be retracted. She will be a free woman by dawn. But you must come to the Heart-Chamber willingly. The Founder's Clause is very clear on that. A forced anchor will shatter the Node."

Willingly.

He had orchestrated her arrest to manufacture my consent. It is the purest, most concentrated evil I have ever encountered, wrapped in the quiet, reasonable tones of a holy man.

"Prove it," I say, lifting my head. "Prove you can free her."

Varos gestures lazily with one hand. A courier steps from the shadows behind the guards, holding a sealed document. Varos takes it, breaking the wax, and holds up the thick parchment for me to see in the dim light. 

It is an official Tribunal pardon. It bears the heavy silver seal of Lord Thessen, fully executed. It lacks only one thing: the counter-signature of a High Priest, validating the "cleansing" of the corrupted asset. 

I stare at the blank line at the bottom of the page. 

In the blank space, I see Lysa's face. I see the amber light of her room. I feel the warmth of her hand against my jaw, telling me I belong to myself. I remember the exact weight of her when she fell asleep against my chest. 

She was the first clean, good thing I had touched in four hundred years. 

I close my eyes. I take a slow, agonizing breath of the humid, blood-scented air. 

"I have one condition," I say. My voice doesn't sound like mine. It sounds dead. Hollowed out. 

"Name it," Varos says genially.

"I see her tonight," I say, opening my eyes. "One last night. I get to tell her goodbye. At dawn, I will report to the Heart-Chamber, and I will submit to the binding."

Varos studies me. He can see that the fight is gone. He is looking at me not as a person, but as a prized hunting hound that has finally accepted the collar again. The profound, sickening satisfaction on his face makes me want to vomit.

"Granted," Varos smiles. "A final indulgence before your holy work begins."

He turns to the courier, withdraws a silver quill from his robes, and signs his name with an elegant flourish across the bottom of the pardon. He rolls the parchment, seals it, and hands it back.

"Take this to Inquisitor Dren at the Spine," Varos commands the courier. "Inform him the prisoner is to be immediately released to her quarters. The interrogation is canceled."

The courier bows deeply, turns, and vanishes into the dark access tunnel, his footsteps echoing rapidly until they fade. 

I watch him go. I track the sound until there is nothing left but the slow, heavy drip of condensation and the wet, pulsing rhythm of the god's heart beneath the stone. 

My soul feels numb. I have just traded it, completely and irrevocably, for her life. The man who had briefly existed in the safety of Lysa's room is dead. All that remains is the asset, and the asset is finally going home.

"Dawn, Cyran," Varos says softly, turning to leave with his guards. "Do not be late."
