# Chapter 34 — *The Verdict*
### Lysa | POV

He looks small without his pulpit.

Just an old man in fancy robes, back to the altar, breath coming too fast.

The High Priest of the Prime Contract, architect of half the city’s suffering, is leaving sweat marks on his own sanctified stone.

Cruelty is banal, Ilan likes to say.

Boring, when you strip away the pomp.

Standing here, in the dripping red light of a god’s broken heart, I finally believe him.

Cyran circles Varos like a predator that’s remembered it has teeth.

The dagger in his hand looks natural there, like an extension of his arm, of the violence they carved into him and then tried to leash. The chain still trailing from his wrist drags across the floor with a harsh, scraping sound that makes the priests flinch.

The Heart‑Node thrashes overhead.

Its beat has gone arrhythmic, frantic. Red‑black light spasms through its veins, spilling down into the chamber in jagged pulses. Every time it contracts, cracks crawl a little farther along the bone arches. Bone dust falls in a soft, constant rain.

We are standing in the ribcage of a dead god, arguing about what counts as consent.

Of course we are.

Varos’s hands are empty.

He lost his dagger when Cyran slammed him into the pillar. His fingers twitch anyway, like he’s still expecting power to answer his every gesture.

Maybe it is.

Maybe it isn’t.

The Old Law scroll is still warm in my hand.

Its ink pulses in time with a rhythm that is not the Heart‑Node’s.

Older.

Steadier.

“Stay back,” Cyran had told me.

He didn’t look at me when he said it.

Didn’t need to.

I can see the outline of his body from behind: the taut lines of his shoulders, the ripple of muscle along his back as he shifts his weight, the tension in his neck as he keeps his eyes on Varos.

Blood mats his hair at the temple where he hit the pillar.

More soaks the edge of the brand at his throat, where the ritual tried to drink him alive.

He is glowing faintly.

Residual magic clings to his skin, to the sigils carved there, to the still‑smoldering marks of his Redemption tally.

He looks like something pulled halfway out of a star.

It would be beautiful, if I weren’t so aware of how much of that light is pain.

“You need me,” Varos says.

His voice shakes.

He is trying to hide it.

He is failing.

“Do you understand what you’ve done?” His gaze flicks up to the Heart‑Node, then back to Cyran. “The ritual won’t stop just because you broke your toys. The Prime Contract is older than you, older than this city, older than your Auditor’s arrogance.”

He spits the last word like it tastes bad.

His eyes land on me, scroll in hand.

I hold his stare.

Still.

Unblinking.

“Without guidance,” he says, dragging his attention back to Cyran, “you’re a beast without a master. You will hurt them, boy. You *always* do. You need someone to hold your leash.”

Cyran goes very quiet.

The air tightens around us, pressure ratcheting up another notch.

Above, the Node convulses again.

The sigils burned into the floor flare.

The remaining priests huddle at the edges of the circle, scared animals in expensive vestments.

I take a step forward.

“Careful,” I say softly, “you’re starting to repeat yourself.”

Varos’s head snaps toward me.

“You understand nothing, girl,” he snarls.

The mask is fully off now.

There’s no holy distance in his face, no patient indulgence.

Just age.

And rage.

“You waltz into my Chamber with your papers and your little clauses and think you can rewrite the will of a god—”

“I understand you used a child to build a weapon,” I say.

Calm.

Precise.

I don’t raise my voice.

His words crash against mine and break.

“He was a corpse on an altar and you signed his name for him,” I continue. “You called it sanctification. It was coercion. You called it necessity. It was convenience. You called it Redemption. It was slavery in better robes.”

A murmur ripples through the guards lining the outer wall.

Some of them avert their eyes.

Others don’t.

Varos sneers.

“It was necessary,” he spits. “For the city. For order. You think this place runs on your pretty ideals? It runs on blood and bargains and someone willing to do what must be done. I made sure the Prime Contract got what it needed so the *rest* of you could play at justice.”

His gaze flicks to the Node, then back.

“You know what happens if we don’t feed it,” he says. “You’ve read the same histories I have. You know how cities die when the law unravels. Chaos. Famine. Monsters in the streets. How many lives do your principles buy, Auditor?”

“Principles don’t kill people,” I say. “People hiding behind them do.”

I take another step forward.

My boots slide a little on the slick stone.

I don’t break eye contact.

“What you built here,” I say, gesturing with the scroll at the altar, the Node, the circle of sigils under our feet, “isn’t order. It’s hoarding. You funneled everything—hope, blood, obedience—upward, into the hands of a god that can’t tell the difference between consent and capitulation. You tell yourself it’s for the city.”

I nod toward Cyran.

“He’s the one who paid the bill.”

Varos’s jaw clenches.

For a heartbeat, there’s something almost like uncertainty in his face.

Then it hardens.

He looks back at Cyran.

“At least I gave him purpose,” Varos says.

Cyran laughs.

It’s a low, vicious sound, all barbs and exhaustion.

“Is that what we’re calling it now?” he drawls. “Purpose? I’ve been called worse things by better people, darling, but that might be a new low.”

His voice is rough, but the familiar theatrical edge is there.

It steadies me more than I want to admit.

Varos takes a step along the curve of the altar, automatically trying to maneuver Cyran back into the old positions—Relic below, Priest above.

Cyran mirrors him.

Keeps himself between Varos and me.

Between Varos and the scroll.

Predator pacing a cage that doesn’t exist anymore.

“You think you’re free,” Varos says. “Because she read a few old lines and made you a technicality. You think that makes you a *man*.”

He spits the word like it’s an insult.

“You are a vessel,” he hisses. “A thing. You were *made*. Every impulse you have, every desire, shaped by this Chamber. You breathe because the Prime Contract allows it. You feed because we taught you how to ask. Without us, you are hunger with a face.”

Cyran’s hand tightens on the dagger.

His knuckles go white.

“That’s not funny,” I say.

They both look at me.

Cyran’s eyes flash—confused, for a second, at the *tone*.

Varos looks outraged that I dared interrupt his monologue.

“You like to turn his trauma into liturgy,” I say to Varos.

“Dress it up in phrases that sound holy enough that no one questions the blood under the ink. You call him Relic and Vessel and Instrument. It’s neat. Clean. Very efficient.”

I take another step.

I’m close enough now that I can see the individual creases around Varos’s mouth.

The way his left eyelid twitches when he’s truly furious.

The faint tremor in his right hand.

“You didn’t give him purpose,” I say quietly. “You stole it. You took a boy who wanted to live or die on his own terms and turned him into a thing that existed only to make your job easier. You didn’t build a system to protect the city. You built one to protect *you*.”

His lips curl.

“It was necessary,” he says again, stubborn. “Someone has to wield the knife.”

“The knife doesn’t get to decide it’s a hero,” I say.

Silence falls, sharp as broken glass.

Cyran is watching me now, not Varos.

There’s something raw in his eyes.

Something close to wonder.

The Heart‑Node above us beats again.

Hard.

The floor under my boots shakes.

A chunk of bone breaks free from one of the arches and crashes down at the edge of the chamber, sending up a spray of shards. One of the priests yelps and ducks.

Time is not on our side.

The ritual is destabilized, but not destroyed.

As long as Varos is upright, it has a voice.

“Enough talk,” Varos mutters.

Perhaps to me.

Perhaps to the god.

He takes another step and misjudges the blood on the stone.

His foot slips.

He pinwheels for balance, catching himself on the edge of the altar.

He looks small.

Not holy.

Not terrifying.

Small.

I almost feel sorry for him.

Almost.

Cyran moves.

Fast.

He closes the last of the distance in a blur of pale skin and chain and blood.

One hand catches Varos’s wrist.

The other drives the dagger up, stopping with the point pressed directly under Varos’s jaw, right in the vulnerable soft place behind the bone.

Varos freezes.

His throat works.

A bead of blood wells at the tip of the blade where it kisses skin.

“Do it,” Varos whispers.

The words are ragged.

Desperate.

Not a plea for mercy.

An invitation.

“Prove me right,” he hisses. “Kill your father.”

The word hangs in the thick air.

Cyran’s whole body goes rigid.

For a heartbeat, he looks younger.

Smaller.

Like the boy that died on this altar and woke up with someone else’s god in his veins.

His hand trembles.

Just a little.

I can see the pulse in his wrist beating, fast and frantic, under bone‑white skin.

The Heart‑Node above us thrashes harder, as if scenting the possibility of blood.

“Cyran,” I say.

His name comes out softer than I intend.

He doesn’t look at me.

Not yet.

His eyes are locked on Varos.

“There’s your Relic,” Varos croaks, even with a blade at his throat.

He sounds triumphant.

“Red in tooth and claw. This is what you are. This is all you will ever be.”

I see it then.

The trap.

He wants this.

He wants to die by Cyran’s hand.

Wants to be proven right at the end—that his creature is nothing but sharpened violence, after all. That all this talk of autonomy and consent and personhood is a pretty story we told ourselves in the margins of his script.

He wants to make Cyran his instrument one last time.

If Cyran kills him now, it will be *truth* for everyone who has ever doubted.

The monster did what monsters do.

The priest died a martyr.

Nothing changes.

My jaw tightens.

“Cyran,” I say again.

He flinches.

Just a little.

His eyes flick toward me.

They’re wild.

Too bright.

There is hunger there—old, honest hunger for revenge, for an end to this man, to this voice, to this weight on his existence.

There is fear under it.

Of what it will mean if he gives in.

“Lysa?” he says.

It’s a question.

Not about what happens next.

About *what he is*.

My fingers tighten around the scroll.

I want to go to him.

Put my hand over his on the knife.

Tell him what to do.

Drag him off Varos bodily if I have to and damn the consequences.

But that would just replace one leash with another.

He gets to choose.

I take a breath.

The air tastes like copper and dust and burning law.

“If you kill him,” I say, voice very steady, “he forces you to act for him one last time.”

Varos laughs.

A short, sharp bark that sprays blood onto the blade.

“Listen to her,” he says.

He is shaking.

Not with fear.

With anticipation.

“She’s afraid of you,” he whispers. “Afraid of what you are without a leash.”

I ignore him.

I keep my eyes on Cyran.

“He wants you to be the killer he made,” I say. “Not because he deserves your justice. Because he needs your violence to justify his own. He needs to die with you still wearing the mask he put on you, or everything he’s done loses meaning.”

I take a slow step closer.

The Heart‑Node’s beat stutters.

The chamber trembles.

Cracks race farther along the arches.

“Him dying here is not justice,” I say.

“It’s an ending.”

My throat is tight.

I push the words through anyway.

“Justice is him living long enough to have his contracts audited. To have survivors testify. To have his pretty system taken apart clause by clause until there’s nothing left but bones and shame.”

Cyran’s grip shifts minutely on the dagger.

“Do you want him dead?” he asks.

His voice sounds hoarse.

You.

Not *should*.

Not *must*.

You.

“Yes,” I say.

There’s no point lying.

The word drops heavy between us.

Blood‑warm.

“Absolutely,” I add, quieter. “I want him gone more than I have ever wanted anything for myself. I want him out of your head. Out of this city. Out of every ledger and prayer and nightmare he’s ever touched.”

Varos stares at me in outrage.

“Then—”

“But,” I say, cutting him off, “I will not ask you to carry that weight for me.”

I straighten my shoulders.

My wrist aches where the Tribunal mark used to mean something.

“If you kill him, you will survive it,” I say to Cyran. “I know that. I will still love you. That will not change. But it will be his choice, echoing in your bones, every time you remember this moment. His hand on yours. His script in your throat.”

His eyes shine.

Not with magic.

With something my training never covered.

“If you spare him,” I say, “it’s *yours*. Your mercy. Your restraint. Your refusal to let him define you one second longer than he already has.”

His jaw works.

I can see the war in his face.

Hunger vs. choice.

Obedience vs. self.

“Do you want him to live?” he asks.

Because of course that’s what he hears.

Of course.

For Varos, mercy was always code for complicity.

For Cyran, consent has always been weaponized.

I take one more step.

Close enough now that I can reach him if I have to.

“I want you to look at him,” I say quietly.

“At this small, bitter man in his stolen sanctity. At what he’s done. And I want you to ask yourself if he is worth the stain on your soul. If he deserves one more drop of your blood, one more heartbeat of your life, one more action shaped around *him*.”

The chamber groans.

A fissure splits the floor at the far end, seeping thick, dark fluid.

We don’t have much longer before the Heart‑Node makes its own decision about us.

“Whatever you choose,” I say, “I will write exactly what happened. I will call it what it is. I will stand beside you in the consequences. But I will not let him make this choice for you. Not again.”

Varos snarls.

“You think sparing me saves him?” he hisses. “You think parading me through your Tribunal will change anything? The system is bigger than both of you. Kill me or don’t, little Auditor. The city will still feed the Contract. It *has* to.”

“Maybe,” I say.

“Maybe not. But you don’t get to be the last word on what he is.”

I look at Cyran.

My chest hurts.

“I see a man,” I say softly. “Not a weapon. Not a saint. Not your property. Whatever you do next—prove me right.”

Silence.

For a moment, the whole world is just the point of that dagger, the tremor in Cyran’s hand, the thin line of red at Varos’s throat.

Then Cyran exhales.

Something in his face loosens.

The feral edge doesn’t vanish.

It shifts.

Becomes something with teeth and boundaries, instead of teeth on a leash.

“She’s right,” he says.

Varos’s eyes bulge.

“No!” he snaps.

“You *can’t*—”

Cyran flips the dagger in his grip.

The motion is absurdly graceful, a little flick that sends the blade spinning halfway around his hand until the hilt rests snug in his palm.

He raises it.

For a heartbeat, Varos’s gaze tracks the movement, convinced he’s about to feel steel in his throat.

Instead, the pommel slams into his temple with a dull, decisive *THUD*.

His eyes roll back.

He crumples.

His fancy robes make it less dignified, pooling around him in a heap of embroidered bone‑white silk and false piety.

The sound of his body hitting the stone echoes in the chamber.

It is not holy.

It is not tragic.

It’s just… a man falling down.

The Heart‑Node screams.

The wordless, furious pressure slams into us like a shockwave.

The room tilts.

A piece of rib‑vault gives way entirely, crashing down near the far wall in a hail of splinters.

Priests scatter.

Guards swear.

Some run for the doors.

Some drop to their knees in instinctive prayer.

No one moves to help Varos.

Cyran stands over him, chest heaving, chain clinking softly as his arm trembles with spent adrenaline.

“I didn’t kill him,” he says.

It’s barely above a whisper.

A statement.

A question.

I cross the remaining distance without thinking.

The scroll is still clutched in my left hand.

With my right, I reach up and wrap my fingers, gently, around his blood‑slick wrist.

The one still half‑raised from the blow.

His skin is hot.

The chain is cold.

“Yes,” I say.

I look from his face to the unconscious man at our feet and back.

“You didn’t.”

He swallows.

His throat works around old brands and new words.

His eyes search mine.

Find something there.

I hope it’s what I feel—pride, fierce and aching.

“I could have,” he says.

“I know,” I say.

“I wanted to.”

“I know,” I repeat.

My voice goes softer.

“And you chose not to. That’s what matters.”

He looks like he might argue.

Or laugh.

Or collapse.

Instead, he does the most dangerous thing I’ve ever seen him do.

He lets me take the knife out of his hand.

There’s a moment of resistance—callus against hilt, old reflexes clinging to the familiar weight of a weapon—then his fingers uncurl.

I toss the dagger aside.

It skids across the stone and disappears into a crack.

“Hey,” I say.

He blinks down at me.

“You did well,” I tell him.

“I am proud of you.”

The words feel heavy.

Important.

Not because I have any right to confer moral grades.

Because he’s spent centuries having every action judged by people who saw him as an asset, not a person.

His face crumples.

Not much.

Just enough to break my composure in a way bone dust and godhearts never could.

“Careful, Auditor,” he rasps, trying for a smile.

His voice shakes.

“You’ll ruin my fearsome reputation.”

“You’re bleeding on my boots,” I say.

It comes out rougher than I intend.

He glances down.

“So I am,” he murmurs.

“Tragic.”

The Heart‑Node answers with another convulsion.

A crack opens in the floor beside Varos’s inert body, oozing thick, dark fluid that smells like congealed sanctity.

The entire chamber is coming apart.

We are not done.

Not yet.

But this part—the choice he just made—is finished.

I step in.

Close the last centimeter between us.

Hook my hand around the back of his neck and pull his forehead down to mine.

He stiffens.

Then, slowly, he relaxes.

His breath brushes my lips.

It tastes like blood and ash and stubborn life.

“You are not what they made you,” I whisper.

His hands hover at my sides, uncertain.

Then settle.

Lightly.

On my hips.

Not to pull.

Not to pin.

Just… there.

“My terrible influence,” he breathes.

It’s almost a laugh.

Almost a sob.

“You keep saying no to all the worst parts of me, love. One day they’ll give up entirely.”

“I’m not saying no to you,” I correct.

I pull back far enough to meet his eyes.

“Only to the scripts that hurt you.”

Heat flickers there.

Want, sharp and familiar, even here in this collapsing sanctum—because of course it’s here, layered over relief and terror and rage.

We’re alive.

We’re touching.

He chose mercy.

The world is ending.

Desire doesn’t care.

Another violent pulse from the Heart‑Node shoves us apart, physically, as the floor cracks wider between us and the altar.

I stagger.

He catches my elbow.

I tighten my grip on his arm.

Bone dust rains down on our shoulders in a pale, constant fall.

Around us, priests and guards scramble for exits, duck under falling debris, cling to whatever doctrine still makes sense as their god’s engine convulses.

Varos lies unconscious at our feet.

A problem for later.

The Prime Contract is still very much awake.

“We need to move,” I say.

“Agreed,” he says.

He glances at Varos.

“Do we… take him?”

“Yes,” I say.

The answer comes faster than I expect.

He huffs.

“You are relentless,” he mutters.

“You love that about me,” I say.

His eyes soften.

“I do, actually,” he says.

It steals my breath for half a heartbeat.

The Heart‑Node shrieks again, dragging us back to the present.

The chamber groans.

A support column cracks with a sound like a bone breaking.

I tuck the Old Law scroll back against my chest.

Tight.

Solid.

Weapon of paper.

Anchor of principle.

Then I look at the man beside me.

Bloody.

Glowing.

Shaking.

Still here.

“Come on,” I say.

I squeeze his hand once before letting go.

“We’ve won the argument. Now we have to stop the god.”
