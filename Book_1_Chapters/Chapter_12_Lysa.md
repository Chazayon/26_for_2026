# Chapter 12 — *The Gala of Teeth*
### Lysa | POV

Cyran enters the room like a detonation—silent, bright, and devastating. 

The crowd doesn’t gasp, not aloud. These people are far too well-trained for that. But there’s a shift, a subtle drawing in of breath, a collective tilt of heads toward the doorway where the Vigil’s favourite weapon has just walked in on a leash made of gold and expectation.

I feel the blast wave anyway.

The chandelier overhead throws a hundred fractured reflections across his skin. It’s made of actual crystal, not bone or glass imitating it, which is how you know Thessen is dangerously rich. The facets catch the light from the marrow-lamps around the ballroom, scattering it in cold sparks over velvet, silk, and the single, bare strip of Cyran’s exposed chest. 

He looks nothing like the man who slumped against an ossuary wall at my feet two nights ago, bleeding out and snarling that he’d manage or die. Tonight he looks like he was carved for this. 

Sheer silk clings to him in careful, revealing lines, the bone-white fabric cut to show the glow of his Marks and the sharp plane of his sternum. Tight black leather maps his legs like someone’s very expensive sin. Gold cuffs circle his wrists and throat. There’s dust on his collarbones that makes his skin shimmer every time he moves. 

He is beautiful.

He is a target.

The ballroom roars quietly around us.

Music drapes through the air—strings, formal and intricate, playing a hymn I recognize from the tribunal halls, but the tempo is wrong, twisted upward into something almost manic. Laughter bubbles at the edges of it, too loud in places. Glasses clink, polite and constant, as servants in bone-white livery weave through the crowd with trays of blood-wine and bite-sized atrocities. 

I hate parties.

I especially hate parties where the hors d’oeuvres are sentient.

A crablike god-spawn claw arranged artfully on a cracker twitches when a passing magistrate plucks it up. The guests around him titter as he bites down and thick, dark liquid spurts. A server passes near me with a platter of something that looks too human around the knuckles to be comfortable. I step aside. 

The floor doesn’t help.

The entire ballroom is suspended over a vertical abyss, the Ribcage district yawning far below. The floor is glass. Perfectly polished, transparent glass. Every step is a reminder that there is nothing but money and hubris between us and a very long fall. 

I don’t look down. Often.

Protocol dictated that the Relic and his Tribunal escort not enter arm-in-arm like some scandalous rumor given flesh. The carriage dropped us at the foot of the Spine, and then we were separated neatly: Cyran swept away by Thessen’s steward with a murmured Lord Thessen wishes to present you personally, of course, and me by a doorman with a ledger and a sneer.

“Name,” he’d said, quill poised, eyes flicking immediately to my indigo robes, to the mark at my wrist.

“Investigator Lysa Vaskin,” I answered. “Tribunal. Here as Auditor Voss’s designated observer.” 

That changed the angle of his sneer. Not less contemptuous. Just… more careful.

“Of course, Investigator,” he’d murmured, the word oiled. “Lord Thessen is honoured by the Tribunals presence.”

He stamped a little sigil on the back of my hand—temporary clearance mark, glowing faintly green—and waved me through.

Now, inside, I do what I came here to do.

I disappear.

Not physically: that would be difficult, given the color of my robes and the fact that I am one of perhaps three people in this room not dressed as if the corpse of the god we live in might judge their fashion choices personally. But professionally. I slip to the outer edges of the room and let the aristocracy’s glare slide over me the way it slides over the servants.

Wallflower strategy. Observation. 

A server approaches with a tray; I take a glass of blood-wine because having something in my hand makes it easier to look like I belong. The goblet is cut crystal, heavy, the stem cool against my fingers. The liquid inside is a deep, true red, thicker than wine should be.

Real blood. Not dyed. 

Of course.

I lift it toward my mouth as if to drink, let the aroma hit my nose—rich iron, spiced with something floral—and then, as the server turns away, I pivot and casually set the glass down behind the trailing leaves of a potted fern whose roots probably haven’t seen honest soil in decades. 

One more small lie in a hall full of much larger ones.

The chandelier throws long, skeletal shadows down through the glass to the depths below. I focus on the walls instead: tapestries woven from dyed silk and strips of cured god-skin, scenes of old temple rites turned into tasteful art. An enormous fountain dominates one end of the room, water the color of diluted blood spilling from the mouth of a stylized Titan head into a bone basin.

And everywhere, people.

Lords in bone-embroidered coats. Magistrates in their most somber black, trying not to look like they’re enjoying this. High priests with sigil-stitched collars, the lines of their authority glowing faintly under the surface of the cloth. Their ladies—and a few of their lords—in dresses so encrusted with calcified god-bone jewels they move like decorated statues. 

I catalogue faces, names I recognize from case files, seals I’ve seen on redacted pages. Voss isn’t here. Of course he isn’t. Men like Voss only touch corruption from behind desks and legal fictions. Thessen is.

He’s at the far side of the room, elevated on a small dais near the fountain, surrounded by his peers. His laugh cuts across the music occasionally, a sharp, barking sound. Cyran stands half a step behind and to his left, like an extremely beautiful dagger in an elaborate sheath.

Thessen rests one proprietary hand on Cyran’s bare shoulder as he talks.

My fingers tighten on the stem of my empty glass.

Work, Lysa.

Intel first. Rage later.

There’s a cluster of men to my right, all in the same shade of expensive dark, their heads bent over something one of them is holding. A scroll, maybe, or a slate. Numbers murmur between them: twelve percent, seven hundred souls, adjusted for posthumous miracles.

I drift closer, angling myself toward a massive tapestry of the Prime Contract writ as art—lines of ink reimagined in silver thread, binding figures kneeling at an altar. I frown at it, as if trying to parse the symbolism, and let my ears do what they do. 

“…yields are up twelve,” one of them says. “Even with the ghoul-kin cull last quarter.”

“Because the ghoul-kin were off-book meat,” another replies. “The real return is in the Redeemed. Those Marks feed the Prime directly.” 

A third chuckles. “Soul futures,” he says. “Best investment this city ever accidentally invented.”

Soul futures.

My hand moves without thinking, reaching for the satchel that isn’t at my side. Habit. I settle for pressing my thumb hard into the fragile flesh of my palm instead, marking the words into my skin since I can’t yet write them on paper.

They’re not just buying devotions. They’re buying shares in damnation.

“Thessen’s portfolio must be very healthy,” the first man says. “With his pet killing half the god-spawn in the Ribcage and earning Marks hand over fist.”

“Rumor says he’s bundling them,” the second murmurs. “Tranching out the Relic’s future kills. Prime-backed contracts. Very exclusive offering.”

My stomach goes cold.

Bundling merits. Selling pieces of future redemptions like they’re slices of a carcass. Every person Cyran saves, every monster he kills, every Mark he earns—turned into profit for men like this. 

Evidence, I remind myself. Facts, not feelings.

I fix the phrase tranching the Relic’s future kills in my mind, along with the faces of the men saying it. Pale hair, hawk nose. Scar on the right cheek. One with a ring shaped like a noose.

Someone drifts into my orbit from the other side.

“Dreadful, isn’t it?” a breathy voice says near my ear. “All this talk of yields at a party.”

I turn.

The woman addressing me is shorter than me by a head, but no one would ever notice that first. They’d see the gown—a cascade of midnight-blue velvet that clings in all the expected places—and then the jewelry.

Her collarbones glitter with tiny god-bone shards set in gold. A necklace of vertebrae, polished to a pearl sheen, wraps twice around her throat. Her earrings are finger bones tipped with rubies. 

Lady Morven, if I’m not mistaken. I’ve seen her name on several donors’ ledgers.

“My lady,” I say, inclining my head. “Lysa Vaskin. Tribunal.”

She lights up.

“Oh, the Auditor,” she breathes. “How delicious. I simply had to ask—why on earth would the Tribunal waste a mind like yours at a little social gathering when there are so many monsters to count?”

Her hand lands briefly on my forearm, cold and weightless, then flutters away. Touch as punctuation, not connection.

“Observation,” I say. “The Tribunal likes to keep an eye on its assets.”

“Mm, yes, yes.” She sips her wine. It leaves a dark ring on her lower lip, which she does not wipe away. “Thessen’s Relic is exquisite, isn’t he? They say he killed three men last month with just his bare hands. So dreadful. So thrilling.” 

My jaw wants to tighten. I make my face do something that approximates polite interest instead.

“You seem well-informed,” I say. “Has the Vigil been issuing public reports?”

“Oh, no,” she laughs. “One hears things. Servants talk. Priests talk more. Of course, everyone knows Thessen keeps the creature on a very short leash these days. Ever since that… incident with the Ambassador’s daughter.”

She lowers her voice on incident, eyes bright with gossip.

“Unpleasantness,” she adds. “But really, what did they expect, letting a pretty little diplomat near a weapon built for more… vigorous company?”

You were a person. They made you a thing. 

I can taste copper at the back of my throat. I realize I’ve bitten the inside of my cheek hard enough to sting.

“That must have been distressing,” I say calmly. “For the Ambassador.”

“For Thessen,” she corrects, and pats my arm again. “You know how he hates to share his toys.”

I could list, from memory, five Tribunal code sections that apply to this conversation: provisions on consent, on Relic handling, on donor conduct. I could quote rate-of-incidence statistics on Relic-triggered violence in exploitative settings vs. standard patrols.

Instead, I take a slow breath and say, “How fascinating, my lady.”

She beams.

“You must simply ask Thessen for a private demonstration,” she says. “Of the Relic’s skills. It’s quite… educational. The way he uses his teeth—”

“I prefer my demonstrations to involve paperwork,” I say, and let my smile sharpen, just enough for her to feel the edge. “Then the Tribunal can learn from them.”

Something flickers in her eyes. Not shame. That would require a conscience. Calculus, maybe—a quick reassessment of whether I am useful for gossip or dangerous as a witness.

“Well,” she says, laughter a shade too bright. “We must all do our part, mustn’t we?”

“Indeed,” I say.

She drifts away in a swirl of velvet and bone-shards, hunting fresher scandal.

I’m left with the echo of her words and the image of Cyran’s mouth on my throat, nothing like a demonstration, everything like survival and choice. 

I look for him.

It isn’t difficult.

The fountain makes a convenient stage, and Thessen understands staging. Cyran stands at its edge, the red-tinged water behind him, framed by stone and light. People orbit him like he’s the only interesting object in the room.

He’s laughing.

Head tipped back, throat exposed, that sharp white line of the collar catching the chandelier’s gleam, he looks, from here, perfectly at ease. One hand curls loosely around the stem of a blood-wine glass; the other gestures in an elegant, dismissive arc as he tells some story that makes the small cluster of men and women around him ripple with delighted horror. 

For one awful heartbeat, doubt punches me in the lungs.

Is he enjoying this?

Maybe I’ve projected too much onto him. Maybe this is his element and I am the one being sentimental, reading trauma into what is, for him, simply an evening of being admired and fed.

Then I see his hand.

The one holding the glass is fine—loose, easy, splayed fingers framing the crystal just so. The other, the one hanging by his side, is pressed hard into his own thigh, just above the knee. His knuckles stand out bone-white against the black leather, tendons rigid.

He’s bracing himself.

Enduring.

Thessen leans in, says something near his ear. Cyran’s smile broadens fractionally, all teeth and no warmth. His eyes stay a shade too bright.

Another man slinks into the circle. Taller than Thessen, thinner, with hair white as scraped bone and a mouth that has never learned what no means. Lord Vane, if I’m remembering the donor lists correctly: minor Contract Lord, major problem.

He moves like he owns everything his gaze lands on.

Including, now, Cyran.

Vane closes the last bit of distance without hesitation, sliding up behind Cyran as if they are lovers. His hands land on Cyran’s waist, fingers spreading over the exposed sliver of skin where silk has been deliberately cut away. 

My vision goes sharp at the edges.

I step forward before I can stop myself.

The glass in my hand protests, the thin stem groaning faintly under the sudden pressure of my grip. I force myself to slow, to breathe, to remember that if I march across the floor and rip Lord Vane’s arm off in front of half the city’s elite, I’ll be the one escorted out.

We can’t help him if we’re both in chains.

Cyran doesn’t flinch.

Of course he doesn’t. That would ruin the illusion.

He lets Vane press closer, lets one of those hands slide a fraction higher, across the lowest strap of the vest. From here, the angle is obscene. Several nearby women fan themselves, pretending it’s the heat in the room.

Then Cyran moves.

It’s graceful. Effortless. A little pivot, a little shift of weight that turns Vane’s grip into momentum and redirects it outward, as if they’re merely adjusting pose for the benefit of their audience. Cyran spills away from him like water, twirling into a half-bow that earns a chorus of breathy laughs. 

Vane’s hands close on air. His smile tightens at the corners, but he accepts the rebuke. For now.

Cyran straightens.

In the split-second before he turns back to his circle, his gaze snags on mine across the glass. 

The distance between us might as well be another city.

For an instant, there’s nothing in the room but the look on his face.

It’s tiny. Anyone not studying him would miss it. The barest falter at the corner of his mouth, the brief, naked flash of something like plea in his eyes.

Help me. Or maybe Don’t look. Or both.

I hold his gaze.

I can’t cross the floor. I can’t walk up and stand between him and the hands and the teeth and the contracts they’ve woven around his body. Not yet.

But I can do this.

I straighten my spine, let my chin lift a fraction, and give him the smallest, sharpest nod. I see you. I’m here. We are doing this on purpose. 

His chest rises on a slow breath, as if he’s matching my inhale.

Then the Ornament slides back over his features like a veil, and he’s all dazzling smile and wicked laughter again, hand trailing along the rim of his glass as he makes some quip that sends his admirers into fresh paroxysms.

Around us, the party swells.

A servant stumbles near my shoulder, nearly jostling my empty glass. I step aside, catch his elbow lightly to steady him. He blanches when he realizes who he’s nearly spilled on—a Tribunal insignia will do that to people—and stammers an apology.

“Careful,” I say, my voice soft. “The floor cracks, we all fall.”

His eyes flick involuntarily to the glass under our feet. For a second, we are united in the dizzy awareness of the abyss yawning below.

Then someone calls for more wine, and he’s gone.

The music shifts, moving into a brighter, showier piece. Couples begin to drift onto the central expanse of glass, skirts swirling, coat-tails flaring. The light from the chandelier fractures over them, over Cyran, over the very expensive nightmare Thessen has built here.

I move.

Slowly. Methodically. I trace the perimeter of the room, cataloguing faces, half-heard deals, whispered jokes about how many souls a certain magistrate has tucked away in private clauses. Everywhere I turn, the Prime Contract surfaces: in metaphors, in business talk, in the lazy entitlement of men who think owning pieces of other people’s afterlives is just good financial planning. 

Soul futures.

Relic tranches.

Shorting redemption.

My audit has just changed shape.

I’m not just auditing a patrolman anymore. I’m auditing a slave trade.

“Everyone!” Thessen’s voice booms suddenly over the low murmur.

He’s on the dais, glass raised, smile wide and wolfish. A subtle gesture at the quartet, and the music dwindles obediently.

“Friends, patrons, esteemed colleagues,” he calls. “If I might have your attention?”

He doesn’t wait for full silence. He never has to. People hush around him like water drawn into a funnel.

“At the stroke of midnight,” he continues, “we will move into the Devotions Hall for a… more focused celebration of our shared prosperity.” He smiles, teeth sharp. “Until then, enjoy the fruits of your faith and the safety our most devoted servants provide.”

His hand slides, casually, to Cyran’s shoulder.

The room’s collective gaze follows it.

The chandelier’s light turns the dust on Cyran’s skin into a halo. He smiles—perfect, empty—and allows himself to be displayed. 

My glass cracks.

It’s a small sound, almost inaudible under the ripple of appreciative laughter, but I feel it in my fingers: a fine, spiderweb fracture racing up the stem from where I’ve been holding it too tightly. A single droplet of blood-wine oozes from the break, sliding over my knuckle like a wound.

I loosen my grip.

The glass holds. Barely.

People around me are already turning, drifting toward the far doors as ushers start to open them. Through the widening archway, I glimpse another chamber beyond—darker, more intimate, lit by rows of candles and lined with private boxes for those who prefer to watch from the shadows.

The auction is beginning. 

I slip my hand inside my sleeve as I move with the crowd.

The stun-baton is slim, Tribunal-issue, its weight familiar along my forearm. Technically, I’m not supposed to bring it to social functions. Technically, this isn’t a social function. 

My fingers find the activation rune, trace it without pressing. Not yet.

Ahead of me, Cyran vanishes into the deeper glow of the Devotions Hall on Thessen’s arm, the gold at his throat catching one last shard of chandelier light before the darker room swallows him.

One crack in the wrong place, and the whole glass floor comes down.

I square my shoulders and follow.
