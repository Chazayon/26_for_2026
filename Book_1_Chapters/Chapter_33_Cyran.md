# Chapter 33 — *The Object Objects*
### Cyran | POV

Seeing her bleed clarifies things.

Everything else—the chanting, the pressure in my skull, the starving heart of a dead god pulsing overhead—drops away.

There is only a line of red along her forearm where Varos’s blade kisses her skin.

It’s shallow.

Practically nothing, compared to what this room is used to.

The Heart‑Chamber devours bodies for breakfast.

But that line of blood might as well be a blade in *my* flesh.

The chains around my wrists stop being abstract constraints and become an insult.

The magic pinning my limbs stops being a storm and becomes a hand on her.

Logic, for once, is very simple.

Protect *her*.

Obey *him*.

Those two directives slam into each other in my head like bone carts on the same track.

Once upon a time, the second one always won.

Tonight it doesn’t.

Varos lunges again.

I see the whole thing from the altar—him stepping into her space, the white flash of his ceremonial dagger as it arcs, the way Lysa tries to twist aside. She’s not a fighter. She moves like someone used to navigating tight archives and Tribunal corridors, not combat.

The blade catches her sleeve.

Slices through cloth.

Bites skin.

She stumbles on the blood‑slick stone step. Her hand shoots out to steady herself. The scroll almost slips from her grasp.

She makes a sound.

Not a scream.

A sharp, surprised hiss between her teeth.

Varos smiles.

Something in me snaps.

My body has been a battleground since the day they dragged me off that altar, but I have never felt anything like this. It isn’t the feral hunger from feeding, or the cold rage from humiliation, or the dull, grinding resignation of being used again.

It’s clean.

White‑hot.

He touched *her*.

“No,” I say.

Or try to.

It starts in my chest, a word dragged up from somewhere below the old brands and newer scars, below the ritual hooks and the Redemption tally and the temple’s precious *Relic* script.

Protect the donor.

Protect the client.

Protect the one on the other side of the contract.

I carved those words into myself years ago, when I realized no one else here would do it. A safeguard, hidden in metaphors and half‑jokes and false compliance. They thought they were reprogramming me.

I was writing back.

The directive unfurls now like a blade.

Protect *her*.

Not as a duty.

Not as penance.

As choice.

The Obedience clause Varos is leaning on—old, ugly, burrowed into my nervous system—meets that and recoils like it’s hit a wall.

*Constraint invalid,* something in my mind screams.

The word that finally rips out of my throat is not a word at all.

It’s a roar.

Raw, cracked, soaked in magic and blood.

It tears out of my chest with enough force to make the nearest priests clap their hands over their ears. The red‑black threads binding my limbs flare in startled protest.

And the chains move.

They’ve been part of the ritual—iron wrapped in sigils, humming in time with the Heart‑Node. Every time I fought them before, the magic just tightened, smug and unbothered.

This time, when I pull, something *gives*.

Metal groans.

The bolt anchoring my right wrist chain to the altar quivers, then jerks.

Stone cracks.

Hairline fractures spiderweb out from the bolt-hole, racing across the blood‑slick surface under my hand.

I snarl.

Put everything I have—every scrap of hunger and fear and remembered humiliation, every moment Lysa has looked at me and refused to see a thing—into one more wrench.

CRACK.

The bolt rips free.

Broken stone explodes from the altar in a spray of shards.

My right arm launches back, trailing links and sigils.

For a beat, my body doesn’t quite believe it’s free.

Then the magic realizes what just happened.

Too late.

I don’t need all four limbs.

I don’t need two.

I need one.

Varos is still focused on Lysa, driving her back step by careful step up the curve of the dais, his blade worrying at her guard. She’s using the scroll as a shield, which is resourceful but not ideal.

He doesn’t see me move.

To be fair, *I* barely see me move.

One second I’m flat on the altar, stomach heaving, head splitting.

The next, I’m slamming into him like a thrown weapon.

The half‑freed chain on my wrist snaps taut as I launch myself, adding momentum. It’s like being yanked into the air by an invisible puppeteer who just realized his strings aren’t the only game in town.

We hit as a single, messy, satisfying impact.

My shoulder drives into Varos’s ribs.

Air whooshes out of him in a very gratifying grunt.

His dagger goes wide, scraping stone instead of throat.

Lysa tumbles backward against the front of the altar, clutching the scroll to her chest, eyes wide.

We all go down.

Varos and I tumble across the slick floor in a tangle of silk and chain and bone.

The edge of the dais smashes into my ribs.

His elbow drives into my jaw.

My head cracks against stone.

I barely feel it.

Adrenaline and stolen ritual magic are humming in my veins, blurring pain into noise.

We roll until I manage to twist hard enough to pin him.

I end up straddling his chest, one knee digging into his sternum, my chain‑wrapped hand braced on the floor next to his head for balance.

He looks up at me.

For once, the calm, patronizing High Priest mask is gone.

I see the old thing underneath.

Not divine.

Not omnipotent.

Just a man who’s finally realized the weapon he thought he owned has teeth and no leash.

“Don’t,” I snarl.

My voice is unrecognizable—thick with blood, guttural with rage.

“Touch. Her.”

Each word drops like a stone.

The chain around my wrist rattles as I slam my fist, fingers curled, into the bone floor next to his face for emphasis.

Cracks radiate out.

The Heart‑Node overhead shrieks.

Not in sound.

In pressure.

It slams at my mind again, furious at the interruption, at the broken symmetry, at the violation of its neat little script.

Obey.

Submit.

Be still.

I bare my teeth.

“Oh, now you care about the rules,” I hiss up at the red‑black mass, as Varos struggles under me. “You’ve been breaking your own for centuries, sweetheart.”

Varos’s hand flashes.

Light flares between us—white‑red, searing.

He slams his palm into my chest.

Magic explodes through me like a thrown net of molten wire.

It hits every old command phrase scar at once.

My back arches.

I’m ripped off him as if by a giant’s hand, flung backward across the floor.

Bone pillar.

Hard.

My shoulder slams into it first.

Something crunches.

I bounce, spin, hit the floor, skid.

The world spins in red streaks.

Consciousness wavers.

For a second, all I can process is the Heart‑Node’s heartbeat, louder and faster now, like a drum line run wild.

Above us, the massive heart contracts in staccato bursts, angry. Threads of light whip down from it, searching frantically for purchase.

The ritual wants me back on the altar.

Back in place.

Back in chains.

“Cyran!”

Her voice cuts through the noise like a blade.

I drag in a breath.

It tastes like blood and dust and burning sigils.

I open my eyes.

Lysa is there, skidding on her knees across the stone to get to me.

There’s a shallow slice along her forearm, already clotting, but that’s not what makes my stomach lurch.

It’s the look on her face.

No professional mask.

No distance.

Raw, focused fear.

For me.

I push up onto my elbows before she can touch me.

If she lays a hand on me right now, I might break in some different, less reversible way.

“I’m okay,” I say.

It comes out rough.

Half‑lie, half defiance.

I scrub the back of my free hand across my mouth.

It comes away red.

My head throbs in time with the distant godheart.

My ribs protest every breath.

My one unfettered wrist smokes faintly where the Obedience script tried to reassert itself.

I look past her.

Varos is already dragging himself to his feet.

He grips the edge of the altar to haul himself up, robes smeared with blood and dust, dagger clenched in his fist.

His hands shake.

His eyes glow faintly with borrowed power.

Above him, the Heart‑Node pulses faster, lulled by his renewed chant.

The priests, shaken but not shattered, are picking the rhythm back up, voices tight with fear.

They’re trying to shove the ritual back onto its rails by sheer will.

Lysa starts to turn toward them, scroll clutched tight.

I catch her wrist.

Not hard.

Just enough.

“Stay behind me,” I say.

She blinks.

“You can barely stand,” she says quietly.

“Watch me,” I say.

I push to my feet.

The chain on my right wrist drags behind me, links scraping a harsh, metallic line across the stone. The other bonds—ankles, left wrist—are still nailed into the altar, straining, pulling.

Every step I take stretches them, makes old sigils wail in protest.

I ignore them.

They are suggestions.

Outdated clauses.

They don’t own me.

Not anymore.

My vision edges with red as the Heart‑Node screams louder, pissed off that its little living battery has left its appointed place.

Good.

Let it scream.

“You wanted a weapon, Varos?” I say, voice low, carrying. “Here I am.”

He turns fully, dagger ready, face flushed with exertion and fury.

“You are nothing without the script that made you,” he spits. “A feral remnant. A pretty corpse that forgot to lie still.”

He lifts his free hand.

Ritual light crawls down his fingers, gathers in his palm.

I can feel the command in it.

The old phrases.

On your knees.

Obey.

Relic.

My throat tightens, phantom memory of those words dragging my body where I didn’t want it to go.

I move anyway.

Each step feels like walking into a storm.

Magic slams against me, trying to shove me back toward the altar, to restitch torn threads, to put the nice compliant puppet back where it belongs.

I keep walking.

The chain screams.

The bolt at my left wrist rips a little way out of the stone, showering dust.

Something in the Heart‑Node *pops*—a note so high it’s almost inaudible.

Cracks appear along its underside, fine white lines against the red‑black.

“You are violating—” Varos begins.

“Terms and conditions?” I say, baring my teeth. “File a complaint.”

His magic slams into me.

Light.

Heat.

The taste of my own ashes.

For a heartbeat, my legs buckle.

The Obedience script claws desperately for purchase.

Then Lysa’s presence brushes against the edges of my mind.

Not words.

Not exactly.

Just… *there*.

A steadying hand without touch.

An anchor.

She’s standing behind me, scroll held out, Founder clauses burning cold and furious in the air.

Nulla Vincula Sine Consensu.

No chains without consent.

The phrase thrums through the room, through me, through the old scars where other, uglier words used to live.

The prime directive they gave me—Obey—wobbles.

The one I wrote myself—Protect—braces.

I don’t stop.

One more step.

The bolt on my left arm rips free with a shriek of tortured metal.

For one dizzying moment, I’m falling.

Then I catch myself.

Two free hands.

One pair of chained ankles.

Close enough.

Varos splutters.

“This is impossible,” he hisses.

“You were *made* to obey.”

I smile at him.

It feels wrong on my face.

Too many teeth.

“Then someone made a mistake,” I say.

He brings the dagger up.

I lunge.

He swings for my throat, predictable as a parishioner’s tithe.

I drop my head.

The blade slices across my shoulder instead, burning hot, laying open muscle.

Pain flares.

Sharp.

Clean.

I ride it.

Use the momentum.

We collide, again, but this time I’m ready.

I grab his dagger wrist with my right hand, chain wrapping around his forearm, iron links biting into his skin.

My left hand closes around his throat.

I drive him backward, off the dais, across the slick floor until his back hits one of the bone pillars with a thick, satisfying *thud*.

The impact knocks the breath from him.

His fingers spasm on the dagger.

It slips from his grasp.

The blade hits stone.

Spins.

Clatters to a stop halfway between us and the altar.

I barely resist the urge to go for it.

I don’t need a knife to kill him.

But it might be nice to have options.

Varos claws at my wrist, nails digging into my skin.

“Release me,” he chokes.

We’re close enough that I can see the fine tremor at the corner of his mouth, the sheen of sweat on his upper lip.

“Motion denied,” I say.

The phrase pops out on instinct, a little courtroom joke mangled through fangs and fury.

It’s wildly inappropriate.

Perfect.

His eyes widen.

“Who do you think you are?” he rasps.

It’s almost funny.

Only now, at the end, does he bother to ask.

For centuries, that question had only one answer.

Relic.

Asset.

Property of the Temple.

I remember Lysa in the graveyard, cool and stubborn, placing the chisel in my hand.

*Carve who you want to be.*

I lean in, close enough that he can see the cracks in my mask, the raw thing underneath that refused to die no matter how many times they bled it.

“I’m Cyran,” I say softly.

Not a shout.

A statement.

A verdict.

The chains on my ankles rattle.

The Heart‑Node above us keens.

It can feel the name shift in its ledgers, the little line item it thought it owned wriggling out from under its thumb.

Behind me, I hear Lysa’s breath catch.

She doesn’t say anything.

She doesn’t have to.

Her silence is a spotlight.

A witness.

I release Varos’s throat with a shove that leaves him gasping, hands scrabbling at the pillar.

Then I turn.

The floor under my bare feet is slick and cold, every old carved sigil twitching like a wounded insect as I step over them.

The dagger lies where it fell.

Bone handle.

Sigil‑etched blade.

Still humming with the ritual’s power.

I bend.

Pick it up.

The metal is warm.

Not from Varos’s hand.

From the god.

The Prime Contract’s attention prickles along my skin like a thousand tiny teeth.

I look up at the Heart‑Node.

At the pulsing, furious knot of crystalflesh and bone and badly written law.

The temple wanted me wired into that thing like a replacement organ.

A living fuse.

A pretty filter.

Instead, here I am on the floor, bleeding and furious and holding the High Priest’s own knife.

You wanted a weapon, Varos?

Here I am.

“But,” I add, turning the dagger so the light catches along the edge, “I’m pointing the wrong way.”

The Heart‑Node beats faster.

Louder.

Somewhere deep in the stone under our feet, the older heart—the dead god’s original—answers, a low, displeased thrum like distant thunder.

The chamber vibrates with it.

Priests flinch.

Guards duck.

Bone dust sifts from the ceiling, turning the air pale.

Behind me, I hear the soft drag of Lysa’s boots as she moves closer.

Not in front of me.

At my back.

Her presence there settles something in me I didn’t know was still shaking.

Varos straightens, pushing off the pillar, eyes fixed on the dagger.

“Careful, child,” he says.

His voice tries for soothing.

It cracks on the last word.

“That blade—”

“Belongs to you?” I cut in.

I twirl it once, theatrical, letting the chain on my wrist clink for emphasis.

“Does it come with a contract, or is theft acceptable when *you* do it?”

He bares his teeth.

“The Seal answers to the god, not to you,” he snaps.

“Even now, you are only—”

“—what you made me,” I finish.

“And you did such exquisite, thorough work, didn’t you? You carved me into the perfect vessel. The perfect weapon. The perfect pretty thing.”

I tilt my head.

Smile.

This one is smaller.

Sharper.

“But you made one tiny, fatal mistake.”

His eyes narrow.

“Oh?” he says.

“I learned from you,” I say.

“And I am very good at reading the fine print.”

The Prime Contract’s presence presses closer.

Interested.

Hungry.

Waiting to see where this goes.

The dagger vibrates faintly in my hand, eager for its usual function—commanding, compelling, sanctifying obedience.

I wrap my fingers tighter around it.

Choose.

Lysa’s words echo, soft and implacable.

*Choose what you want. I’ll stand with you either way.*

I choose this.

I take one deliberate step forward, placing myself fully between Varos and Lysa, between the High Priest and the Old Law scroll she’s holding like a drawn sword.

Blood trickles warm down my arm from my shoulder.

Drips from my fingers.

Spatters the stone in little, bright points.

Each drop hits an etched sigil and hisses.

The magic in the room shivers.

For the first time in a very long time, all of it—Temple ritual, Prime Contract law, Founders’ Covenant, my own battered will—is pointed in a direction I chose.

No one owns me.

Not anymore.

I lift the dagger.

Raise my head.

And smile at the god’s heart.
