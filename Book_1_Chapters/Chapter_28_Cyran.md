# Chapter 28 — *Gravity and Grace*
### Cyran | POV

I am breathing.

It’s a voluntary action for the first time in… gods, centuries.

In the Heart‑Chamber, breath was something dragged in on command—ordered by ritual rhythm, by a High Lord’s voice, by the throb of a dying god. In the temple cells, it was borrowed, measured out between punishments, a luxury that could be revoked.

Here, in the Necropolis, leaning against a stranger’s tomb while the sky grays toward dawn, I am breathing because my body feels like it and no one has told me otherwise.

Novel.

Unnerving.

Rather marvelous.

The dead make for quiet neighbors.

I appreciate the privacy.

Cold stone presses between my shoulder blades, rough and damp where moss has found purchase in old carvings. Dew seeps through the seat of my ruined trousers. The air smells of wet earth, old bone, and the faint, metallic tang of last night’s blood. Mine. Thessen’s. Hard to tell where one ends and the other begins.

My burned shoulder throbs in time with my pulse.

Every beat is a small, insistent announcement: you are still here.

“Hold still,” Lysa mutters.

Her voice is thinner than usual, the edges scraped raw. Exhaustion, adrenaline crash, the not‑minor detail of watching me nearly be turned into a divine lightning rod.

Her hands are covered in dust and Thessen’s blood.

I have never seen anything more beautiful.

“Darling,” I say, as she braces one knee in the dirt and leans over me, “I am literally propped against a corpse to keep from falling over. I could not be holding stiller without active necromancy.”

“Stop talking,” she says. “You’re wasting energy.”

“On the contrary. My wit is self‑sustaining.”

She gives me a look.

The kind that in Tribunal hearings has made lesser men wilt and confess to financial irregularities they didn’t even commit.

Unfortunately for both of us, I’ve built a certain tolerance.

“Fine,” she says. “Talk. Just don’t move.”

Satisfied compromise.

I tilt my head back against the sarcophagus and let her work.

She’s torn a strip from the hem of her robe—practical, efficient, visibly painful to her bookkeeper’s soul—and is using it to wipe away the worst of the char and residue from my shoulder. The blast caught me high, above the heart, searing through fabric and flesh. The skin is a mess of blistered red and blackened patches, ringed by an ugly bruised halo where divine backlash hit.

It hurt at the time.

Now it’s more of a deep, nauseating ache.

Her fingers are firm but as gentle as circumstances allow.

Every so often, when the cloth sticks to half‑scabbed skin, she hesitates, lips pressing together, and tugs.

I wince.

“Sorry,” she says immediately.

“Don’t be,” I answer, surprising myself with the lack of dramatics. “Pain means I’m here. It’s when I stop feeling it you should worry.”

She huffs softly through her nose.

“That may be the least reassuring thing you’ve ever said,” she mutters. “And that’s a competitive category.”

“High praise, coming from you.”

A bird calls somewhere in the ribs overhead.

Another answers, sleepily offended.

The Necropolis is waking up. Hints of color bleed into the bone‑pale horizon—gray into blue into the faintest wash of pink along the gaps between ribs. Dew beads on the grass around the tombs, catching what little light there is and throwing it back in tiny, stubborn sparks.

My hand rests on my thigh.

Steady.

Streaked in ash, scorched in places where fire licked the veins.

I look at it.

At the fingers that once knew exactly how to hold a ritual knife at the perfect angle to catch altar‑light. At the palm that has worn chains, gloves, donors’ lipstick, priestly rings like they were all the same—implements of use.

Thessen’s blood still pools under my nails in a dark crescent.

He is dead.

The thought settles into me with the slow inevitability of water finding a lower level.

I flex my hand.

“I killed him,” I say.

The words feel obscene and exhilarating on my tongue, both.

Lysa wrings out the makeshift cloth, dark water dripping onto the grass. Her own hands shake now that we’re not running or dying. She wipes them on what’s left of her hem, then sits down beside me, shoulder to shoulder, back to the same stone.

“You executed a traitor,” she says.

“Ah, semantics,” I murmur. “My favorite kink.”

She gives me a sidelong look that could curdle blood.

“It matters,” she says quietly. “Words matter. He tried to kill you, Varos, the donors, half the Chamber. He tried to use the Prime Contract as a weapon. You didn’t murder him in an alley because you were bored, Cyran. You stopped him. There’s a difference.”

“Is that what your inevitable report will say?” I ask.

“I wrote the audit in my head while we ran,” she says dryly. “It helped me not think about the ceiling falling on us.”

Of course she did.

There’s something almost obscene about how much comfort I take in that—her mind turning chaos into prose even as the temple tried to come down around our ears.

“You and your paperwork,” I say.

It comes out softer than I intend.

A crack shows through the joke.

My hand moves almost without my permission, seeking hers where it lies on her thigh. Dust smears where our fingers meet. She looks down, surprised, then lets me entwine our hands.

Her pulse jumps under my thumb.

She doesn’t pull away.

He’s dead, I think again.

My master.

My jailer.

My abuser, though that word still feels like putting a mortals flimsy curse on something carved in god‑bone.

Gone.

The world does not tilt.

The Heart does not stop.

The dome cracked, but the city still stands.

Some childish part of me always expected the sky to fall the moment his did.

Instead, the sun is rising.

“I killed a Lord,” I say, testing the shape of it out loud.

“A Contract Lord,” she corrects. “A specific, singular, particularly odious one. Try not to generalize.”

“Always so precise,” I murmur.

“Someone has to be,” she says.

Silence settles for a moment.

Not empty.

Full.

The birds grow louder as more of them join the dawn chorus, their calls bouncing strangely off the bone architecture. The distant heartbeat of the temple—once a constant, oppressive presence—has faded to a faint, irregular thud barely audible out here.

I realize, distantly, that I am smiling.

It feels foreign on my face.

Not the curated half‑smile, carefully slanted to show teeth and promise sin.

Something smaller.

Almost… soft.

Lysa notices.

Of course she does.

“You’re terrifying me,” she says. “Is that a genuine smile?”

I turn my head toward her.

Her hair is a wreck—half‑fallen braid, escaped strands sticking to her temples with sweat, dust streaked through the dark. There’s a smear of black along her jaw where someone’s blood—probably mine—caught her. Her robe is torn, knees grubby from the tomb‑yard. Dark circles bruise the skin under her eyes.

She’s the most radiant thing I’ve ever seen.

“Don’t be melodramatic,” I say.

“That’s your job,” she says. “I’m the one who writes things down after.”

“That’s why I like you,” I say.

It slips out.

Unvarnished.

She blinks.

“Because I do my job?” she asks.

“Because when the god’s voice tried to drag me to my knees, you didn’t pray for mercy,” I say. “You didn’t weep or beg or try to throw yourself between us again. You told me to choose.”

She goes very still.

There, in the Heart‑Chamber, with Thessen’s command wrapped around my throat like a noose, I remember her face—fierce and afraid and absolutely unyielding.

*Choose what you want. I’ll stand with you either way.*

She put the knife in my hand and then stepped back.

Not because she didn’t care which way I swung it.

Because she cared *that it was mine*.

“I didn’t know if it would work,” she says now, voice low.

I can hear the truth in it.

The fear.

“I thought… if the Command Seal had enough reach left, if the Prime Contract still saw you as bound, my order would just—” She breaks off. Swallows. “I gambled with you.”

“You gave me something no one else ever has,” I say. “A choice that had nothing to do with what they wanted. That… loosened something. Cut something. It worked because it was you.”

She looks at me.

Not as an Auditor.

Not as a woman steeling herself against an attractive monster’s tricks.

Like a person who is being handed something heavy and fragile and has not yet decided if she’s allowed to keep it.

“That’s an awful lot of responsibility to lay on one mortal scribe,” she says, attempting levity.

Her hand is still in mine.

Her fingers tighten.

“Don’t worry,” I say. “I’ll ensure you’re properly cited in the epilogue.”

She snorts, a tiny, helpless sound.

The laugh cracks something open in my chest.

Suddenly, I can feel *everything*.

The ache of my wounds.

The residual, echoing terror of almost losing myself back to a god I thought I’d escaped.

The weight—new and unbearable and glorious—of the fact that for the first time since I was dragged into that temple bleeding and gasping, there is no leash around my soul.

No one to call me back to heel.

The Prime Contract hums in the bones under us, but I am no longer wired directly into its will. The Seal shattered like glass. The old words slid off my skin like water instead of digging in like hooks.

I am… unmoored.

Autonomy, it turns out, is not the buoyant, liberated state I once imagined.

It is weightless and heavy all at once.

Terrifying.

“Cyran,” Lysa says.

There’s something in my face.

Must be.

Her voice has shifted—softer, cautious in the way one would approach a cornered animal.

I realize I’m clutching her hand too tightly.

I loosen my grip.

“Apologies,” I say.

“Don’t apologize for having feelings,” she says. “You just killed your abuser and broke a divine contract in the same hour. If you weren’t at least a little destabilized, I’d be questioning your psychological health.”

“Only now?” I ask.

“Well,” she says, “it would move from *concerning* to *alarming* on my internal scale.”

“Good to know there’s a rubric.”

“Of course there is,” she says. “I’m a scribe.”

I laugh.

It’s a cracked, wet sound.

Half hysterical.

It feels like something tearing, then like something healing in its place.

She watches me, eyes steady.

She doesn’t flinch.

Doesn’t look away.

Doesn’t try to shush the ugly sounds into something prettier.

When I can breathe again without feeling like I’m choking on ash, I realize I’ve leaned closer to her, our shoulders pressed fully together now instead of just brushing. Our joined hands rest in the space between us, dust and blood drying in the lines of our skin.

Her face is right there.

So close I can see each individual lash.

The faint scar at the corner of her mouth where she must have bit through the skin once, years ago, and never thought about it again.

The way her pupils widen slightly when she notices me looking.

Careful, a voice in me says.

Old, familiar.

Every kiss you’ve ever given has been either an order or a down payment.

Every touch a transaction.

You don’t know how to do this honestly.

I do not, in fact, know how.

But I know how not to.

So I take a breath.

Voluntary.

Slow.

And I say, “Lysa.”

“Yes,” she answers.

Not *what*.

Not *hmm*.

Yes.

Consent built into the grammar of her attention.

“May I kiss you?” I ask.

Her eyes widen.

Of all the things I could drop on her in this dawn‑lit graveyard—confessions, jokes, the autograph of a dead Lord—I don’t think she expected that one to come properly framed.

“You… what?” she says.

“Traditional display of affection between two people who have been circling each other for an embarrassingly long time,” I say. “Mouths. Possibly hands. Minimal bloodshed. Highly recommended in most mortal cultures, I’m told.”

She blinks once.

Twice.

Heat rises along her cheekbones, cutting through the grime.

She glances, involuntarily, at my mouth.

Ah.

There it is.

Evidence.

“I—” She stops. Takes her own breath. “Why are you asking?”

It’s a fair question.

I could deflect.

Call it a quirk.

Make a joke about Trial Periods and Terms of Service.

Instead, I tell the truth.

“Because I don’t want this to be another thing taken from you without your full understanding,” I say. “Because I have kissed a great many people for a great many reasons, and I would prefer this not resemble any of those. Because for the first time, I am in a position to choose something only for myself, and I find that what I want, infuriatingly, is you. But if that isn’t what you want, I need to hear it now while I still have enough adrenaline to survive the answer.”

My voice goes quiet by the end.

No pet names.

No performance.

Just me, stripped of altar‑light and theater, holding something out and waiting to see if she takes it or leaves me standing here with my hands empty.

Her throat works.

She looks down at our joined hands, then back up at me.

There is fear in her eyes.

Not of me.

Of herself, maybe.

Of what this means.

“We nearly died tonight,” she says, very precisely. “You almost became a god’s puppet again. I almost watched you burn. We escaped through a secret priest‑passage while the temple fell down.”

“Yes,” I say lightly. “Ten out of ten on the drama scale. Would not recommend as a regular date night, but for a first outing it did leave an impression.”

Her lips twitch.

Then flatten.

“This isn’t… adrenaline,” she says quietly. “At least, not only. I wanted to kiss you in my quarters weeks ago. I wanted to kiss you when you laughed in the Vein Markets and scared that smuggler into confessing everything. I wanted to kiss you when you carved your name into bone and looked at me like you’d done something transgressive and holy all at once.”

I suck in a breath.

She holds my gaze.

“But,” she continues, “I wasn’t sure if you ever wanted anything from me that wasn’t also… payment. Or survival. Or performance. Tonight, when you chose not to kill for them—” Her jaw tightens. “That changed something. For me. So yes. You may kiss me. If *you* are sure it’s what you want and not another script you’re trying to please someone with.”

I don’t realize I’ve moved until we’re closer.

I do it slowly.

Excruciatingly.

Giving her room to lean in or lean away.

My free hand lifts, hovers near her face before I commit, fingers brushing a streak of dried blood along her jaw. Her skin is warm under the grime, pulse beating hard in the delicate column of her neck.

“I have followed scripts for longer than you’ve been alive, love,” I say, voice very soft. “This is the first time I have ever had the chance to improvise.”

We both lean in.

Gravity does most of the work.

Our lips meet like an exhale.

No altar, no audience, no blood bargain humming in the back of my skull dictating the angle of my head.

Just the press of her mouth to mine.

It’s messy.

We’re both exhausted, lips chapped, breath not exactly mint‑fresh. There’s dust in the corner of her mouth that my tongue accidentally catches. She makes a small, surprised sound, then a different one when I adjust, deepening the kiss.

Heat rolls through me.

Not the forced, artful arousal of a performance I’ve practiced a thousand times.

Something fiercer.

Less controlled.

I angle my head; she tilts hers the opposite way, accommodating. Our noses bump. We laugh against each other’s mouths, the sound breaking the seal, then reforming it as we dive back in, hungrier now that the first hesitation is past.

Her hand comes up to my cheek.

Fingertips light against the old, pale scars there, thumb tracing the line of one I hate. I flinch.

She doesn’t pull back.

She kisses me harder, like she’s arguing with every ghost that ever told me my face was their creation, their property.

I kiss back.

Her lips part beneath mine.

The taste of her—tea and dust and copper and something wild—is a revelation and a homecoming all at once. My fangs, ever a hazard, brush against her lower lip; I jerk back on instinct.

She follows.

Doesn’t flinch.

Her tongue slides against mine.

My careful control fractures.

I make a sound I would never, ever perform for anyone else.

Something small and involuntary, dragged up from a place I didn’t realize still had a voice.

I shift, turning more fully toward her, our joined hands caught between us, pressed to my chest. I free them only long enough to slide my arm around her waist, pulling her closer, careful of her own scrapes and bruises. She comes willingly, knees bumping mine, body tucking into the curve of me like it’s the most obvious thing in the world.

We kiss until the birds have fully claimed the dawn.

Until my lungs burn worse than my shoulder.

Until the world narrows to the wet heat of her mouth and the soft, desperate sounds she makes and the way her fingers keep drifting up into my hair, clutching there like she’s afraid I’ll evaporate if she lets go.

Eventually, we have to breathe.

We pull apart by slow degrees.

Lips lingering.

Noses brushing.

I rest my forehead against hers, eyes closed, trying to steady my heart—or whatever passes for one. Her breath ghosts over my lips, fast and uneven.

I can feel her smile in the way her cheeks lift against my own.

For a moment, I let myself exist only here.

No Temple.

No Prime Contract.

No Redemption ledgers, no Tribunal hearings, no donors, no Command Seals.

Just a man who refused to become a god.

Just a woman who told him he could.

Just their mouths, tasting of dust and blood and life.

“So,” I say at last, because if I don’t say *something* I might say too much. “What now?”

Her eyes open.

We’re so close she’s a blur of brown and gray and gold, all the colors of law ink and lamplight and dawn.

“Now,” she says, voice steadying as she speaks, “we clear your name.”

I huff a breath that might be a laugh.

“Ambitious,” I say. “Given the file Thessen no doubt left behind painting me as the city’s favorite monster and you as my corrupt handler.”

“All lies leave traces,” she says. “He had to base them on something. We’ll follow the threads. Expose the edits. Varos owes us a favor now—several, in fact. We will use that.”

“And after that?” I ask.

“And then,” she says, “we find out who Thessen was working for. He was too methodical, too… thorough to be acting alone. Contracts that deep don’t sign themselves.”

Her jaw sets in that way that tells me some Contract Lord, somewhere, should start losing sleep.

I study her face.

The mess.

The determination.

The absolute refusal to step back now that she’s stepped in.

“Partners, then?” I say.

Her grip on my hand tightens.

“Partners,” she echoes.

The word settles over me like a cloak cut to my actual size instead of some grotesque ornamental thing draped on me for spectacle.

Not property.

Not weapon.

Not ornament.

Partner.

I tilt my head.

Brush my nose along hers.

“And lovers,” I add, because if we are improvising, I intend to be thorough.

Color flares high on her cheeks.

“Yes,” she says.

No flinching.

No hedging.

“Yes.”

I kiss her again.

Because I can.

Because I chose to.

Because the sun is climbing over the rim of the dead god’s ribs, painting the Necropolis in thin, hard light, and for the first time it feels less like exposure and more like evidence.

Of survival.

Of choice.

Of us.
