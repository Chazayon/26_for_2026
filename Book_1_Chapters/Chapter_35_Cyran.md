# Chapter 35 — *The Heart Moves On*
### Cyran | POV

The heart of a god is a noisy thing.

It drowns out everything else—voices, screams, the frantic scrabble of boots on bone—as it tries very hard to explode.

The Heart‑Node hangs over the altar like a swollen lantern, and it is no longer beating so much as *thrashing*. Veins of red‑white light bulge along its surface, pulsing too fast, too bright. Cracks lace through the crystalflesh, leaking brilliance the way a wounded animal leaks blood.

Each convulsion hits the chamber in waves.

The floor shudders.

The bones groan.

Air tastes like iron filings and static.

Everyone is running.

Priests in their ruined robes.

Guards who signed away their lives for the privilege of dying in *other* people’s messes, not this one.

Even the zealots know when to cut their losses.

The only ones not moving are Varos—crumpled where he fell, breathing but blissfully quiet—and us.

Lysa and me.

She has one hand fisted in the back of my torn shirt, the other still clutching the Old Law scroll against her chest, knuckles white. Her pulse is a little rabbit under my fingertips where I stole the contact a second ago.

We just decided not to kill a man.

Now the god he served is considering doing it for us, plus a few hundred thousand of our closest neighbors.

“How bad?” I ask.

My voice comes out rough.

It feels like I’ve smoke‑inhaled pure ritual.

Lysa glances up at the Node, then around the Chamber.

A fracture opens along the far wall with a wet crack.

She winces.

“Bad,” she says.

Technical, as ever.

“Can we… leave?” I suggest.

Half‑joking.

Half not.

She gives me a look.

“You’re still anchored,” she says. “It’s reacting to you. If we abandon it, it will discharge into the city.”

“Ah,” I say.

The Heart‑Node writhes above us, threads of light whipping wildly now, lashing the air, searching for a ground.

My skin prickles.

The old sigils along my spine and ribs, the ones that used to answer every whisper from this place, flare in sympathetic panic.

It feels, intimately, like being in bed with someone you’ve broken up with, and realizing they still think you’re together.

Lysa squeezes my arm.

“We disarmed the priest,” she says.

Her eyes are dark, steady.

“Now we disarm the bomb.”

We.

Not *you*.

It shouldn’t matter, that pronoun, not with a god about to go critical and cook us in our own marrow.

It matters.

“Of course,” I say lightly.

I tilt my head back.

Study the mess hanging over us.

The glow makes it hard to focus, a migraine in physical form. Light bleeds at the edges of everything, turning Lysa’s face into a stark chiaroscuro of ink and bone and stubbornness.

Somewhere under the noise of the Node, the older heart—the one buried deeper in the dead god’s chest, the one I used to hear in my bones walking the Ribcage at night—thumps once.

Slow.

Displeased.

Watchful.

Solen’s voice floats up from memory, sardonic and distant.

*Cut the throat.*

“Close enough,” I mutter.

“What?” Lysa asks.

“Nothing, love,” I say.

I take a step forward.

The Node notices.

Oh, does it notice.

Energy snaps out like a tongue, a whip of bright red‑white that slams into my chest.

My back hits Lysa.

We both stagger.

The contact burns and tingles at the same time, like touching my own brand and biting tinfoil.

“Cyran,” she says sharply.

Concern.

Warning.

I pat her hand where it clamps instinctively around my side.

“I’m all right,” I lie.

She doesn’t call me on it.

Which is considerate.

I peel myself off her and step forward again, more cautiously this time.

The sigils carved into the floor under the Node are a riot of light, all their careful geometry torn and rerouted by too much power trying to squeeze through too small a script. Glyphs flicker, die, flare back, overlapping like someone stacked a dozen contracts on top of each other and lit the pile.

It’s feeding off everything Varos summoned, and now that he’s not here to conduct, it’s improvising.

That never ends well.

“Any bright ideas?” I call back over my shoulder.

The air hums.

A piece of bone falls from the ceiling, smashes to fragments a few meters away.

“No one’s ever let it get this far,” Lysa says.

Translation: No precedent. No guideline. No careful legal structure to wedge myself into and wriggle sideways.

How rude.

“Of course not,” I say.

I step into the direct spill of its light.

Heat slams into me.

Instinct curls me in on myself, shoulders hunching, old body‑memory expecting leather straps and hands to hold me down.

I straighten.

One breath.

Two.

I look up.

“Hello, darling,” I say to the screaming heart of a dead god.

The connection hits like a tidal wave.

I don’t even have to reach.

It reaches for me.

Grabs.

Drags.

In an instant, I am no longer standing on a slick bone floor in a collapsing chamber. I am nowhere. Everywhere. Suspended in red darkness that isn’t dark at all, full of threads and lines and sigils stretching away into infinity.

The Prime Contract in full view.

It is not a text.

It is a living geometry, a vast, predatory lattice of agreements and blood‑marks and obligations, all feeding into this pulsing knot above my head. Every contract signed in this Chamber, every oath sworn under its arches, has left a little spark in this network.

I can feel them.

A thousand tiny hooks in a thousand hearts.

Vigil oaths.

Redemption marks.

Binding seals.

Intimacy clauses.

Blood keys.

They all lead here.

To this one, overstuffed point.

Hello, *Relic,* something whispers.

It doesn’t speak in words, not really.

It speaks in sensation.

In clauses.

In the taste of ink and blood on my tongue.

But my mind translates it anyway.

Relic. Asset. Designated Conduit.

We lost you.

Found you.

Warmth floods my limbs, syrupy and heavy.

Power.

It tastes like honey and iron.

Thick.

Sweet.

Wanting to be drunk.

If I wanted to, I realize, I could reach out and pluck a dozen threads at once.

I could cancel debts with a thought.

Rewrite Redemption tallies.

Erase entire categories of obligation.

I see a thousand people suddenly breathing easier.

Guards dropping their weapons.

Vigil wardens waking up without that constant, grinding, *must atone* hotwire in their skulls.

Shrines going dark as promised tithes evaporate.

I see myself, too.

Not on the altar.

On a throne.

Bone and ruby, high above the Ribcage.

The Chamber around me repaired, but different—less rotted sanctum, more glossy court. The Heart‑Node, no longer thrashing, hangs obedient and calm above my head, its light a corona rather than a threat.

The city below is… tidy.

Orderly.

No one starves unless I let them.

No one dies for a Contract unless I say they should.

No more Varos.

No more Contract Lords.

Just me.

I see Vigil wardens kneeling, Redemption marks softening in relief as their debt is forgiven at my whim.

I see donors lining up, offering their wrists with smiles, not fear, because I am *benevolent*. I take only what I need.

Sometimes a little more.

Because I can give it back, can’t I?

I see Lysa.

She is there, at my side, of course.

Dressed in something far too fine for Tribunal work, bone‑white silk and scales of inked law, my arbiter‑queen. Her eyes are sharp, but there is a line of silver around her throat that glows faintly in time with the Heart.

A contract.

Not like the others.

Personal.

Private.

Binding.

I see the cage.

Not iron.

Not bone.

Gold.

Script shaped like filigree, like jewelry.

A prettier shackle.

She’s safe there.

No one can touch her.

No one can threaten her with career or censure or blade.

No one can take her away from me, because she never leaves the gilded perimeter of my protection.

All it costs is the city.

And me.

Alone.

At the center of an endless web of obligation.

Every cry for help routed through me.

Every injustice mine to name, to ignore, to fix.

Every failure my personal sin.

I thought I knew what chains felt like.

This is heavier.

“Absolutely not,” I say.

Or try to.

My mouth doesn’t move in this vision space, but the refusal runs down every line they ever carved into me.

Somewhere very far away, my body laughs.

The Node does not like that.

The honey‑iron taste turns bitter.

You could fix it, the pressure whispers.

You could erase their names from our pages. You could give him back his childhood, give her back her trust, give those children back their lives.

It shows me Pila, grubby and fierce, standing over me in a dark alley, refusing to run when told.

It shows me Vigil wardens on patrol, exhausted and hollow, praying to a dead god because that’s what they were told you do with fear.

It shows me myself, younger and stupid and hopeful, signing away my corpse on an altar because the alternative was being dead without any chance of being useful.

You could fix it.

“‘Fix’ is doing a lot of work in that sentence,” I say.

Anger gives my mind teeth.

“How many people had to die for you to get this hungry? How many contracts did you eat without giving anything back?”

The geometry shudders.

Old clauses flare in protest.

Nulla vincula sine consensu, the Old Law hums in my bones, cool and unwavering.

No chains without consent.

I feel Lysa at the edge of all this.

Back in the bleeding Chamber.

Her emotions leak through our bond—fear, focus, a thin, sharp filament of determination that glows silver through all this red.

She is hauling Varos’s unconscious body away from the spreading cracks, cursing under her breath as she goes.

Of course she is.

My ridiculous, infuriating, breathtaking Auditor is saving the man who tried to kill her while I flirt with godhood.

If I take this power, she will not stop me.

She won’t kneel.

Won’t flatter.

She’ll stand at my side and argue policy until the end of the world.

Until she realizes I am the system now.

And systems do not love people.

They use them.

The vision shifts.

I see myself centuries from now, still perfect, still beautiful, still in control. The city below has changed and changed again. Buildings crumble, are rebuilt, bones settle, new shrines rise.

Lysa is nowhere.

She lived her mortal span.

Died.

Was mourned.

I survived.

Of course I did.

That’s what gods do.

I am on my throne, surrounded by devotees, petitioners, supplicants, and I have never been more alone.

The thought punches something small and fragile and stubborn in the middle of my chest.

No.

Not like this.

Power is just another word for debt in this place.

I am so very tired of debt.

The Heart‑Node feels my refusal.

Rage lashes through the geometry, red streaks turning white‑hot.

Threads tighten around my limbs, trying to pull me deeper, bind me tighter.

You cannot walk away, it surges.

You were made for this.

“Yes,” I say.

For once, no joke.

No pet name.

Quiet.

Plain.

“I was. And I am choosing something else.”

It howls.

The entire network flares—an impossible, blinding pattern of triangles and circles and hooks that stretches out beyond the city, into the ribs, the spine, the skull, the markets, the courts.

Everywhere the Prime Contract has sunk a tooth.

For a heartbeat, I see it all.

Exactly how far its reach goes.

Exactly how much damage it can still do.

And exactly where its weakest point is.

Right here.

Suspended above my head.

Overfed.

Overconfident.

Bad at reading its own fine print.

A laugh bubbles up in me.

It sounds a little hysterical.

“I reject the terms,” I tell it.

Then I open my eyes.

The Chamber slams back into place.

Noise.

Heat.

Bone dust in my mouth.

The Heart‑Node towers above me, swollen and cracked, white‑hot now rather than red, light so bright I have to squint.

Lysa is halfway across the floor, dragging Varos’s limp weight toward the nearest, least‑cracked archway. Her hair is plastered to her forehead with sweat and dust. The cut on her arm has reopened; a fresh smear of red trails down to her wrist.

She is screaming something at me.

I can’t hear it.

All sound is drowned under the Node’s shriek.

I catch her gaze.

She stops.

Just for a second.

Standing there with an unconscious High Priest at her feet, the Old Law scroll clutched to her chest, bone‑dust falling around her like monochrome snow, she looks like some furious, ink‑stained saint dragged into the wrong myth.

I smile at her.

A small, sad thing.

Apology.

Promise.

She shakes her head.

Violent.

No.

Her lips form my name.

I raise the hand still wrapped in the chain.

The dagger Varos dropped—my dagger now—waits obediently in it, sigils along the bone handle burned nearly black from overuse.

“That’s quite enough,” I tell the god.

And I jump.

The Node meets me halfway.

Light slams into my skin in hot, hungry waves.

My old brand sears, trying to reassert ownership.

I bare my teeth and plunge the knife in.

The Heart‑Node’s flesh isn’t really flesh at all.

It’s calcified blood and crystallized magic, hard and slick.

The dagger doesn’t care.

It sinks in with a horrible, wet crunch, burying itself to the hilt.

For a fraction of a heartbeat, nothing happens.

Then everything does.

The Node *screams*.

The noise is not sound.

It is pressure and light and memory all at once, blasting through my skull with enough force to make me drop to my knees, both hands locking convulsively around the buried hilt.

Energy pours into me.

Not in neat lines, not like the slow, careful warmth of a feeding.

This is a flood.

Molten.

Blinding.

It hammers down my arms, into my chest, rips through the old pathways the ritual carved—those obedient little channels meant to carry devotion one way and power the other.

They are not built to handle this much.

Neither am I.

For a moment, I am nothing but fire in a man‑shaped container.

Bones creak, threatening to crack under the pressure.

Sigils along my ribs, my spine, my throat flare white, then blacken, edges burning away as the overload scrubs them clean.

The Prime Contract’s geometry rises around me again, shuddering, trying desperately to channel this surge back into itself, to keep its lattice intact.

No.

Not this time.

I grit my teeth.

Scream my refusal into the torrent.

The words are meaningless here.

The intent isn’t.

I grab hold of the energy.

It’s like trying to grip lightning, like trying to hold on to a river with my bare hands.

It doesn’t want to be held.

It wants to climb.

To root itself in the Node.

To spread.

Behind me, through the bond, I feel Lysa like a hand on my back.

No touch.

Just presence.

A steady pressure saying: *Here. Here. Down, not up.*

Grounding.

Right.

The floor.

The god’s bones.

Solen said: cut the throat.

He didn’t say which direction to bleed it.

I wrench.

Instead of letting the energy rise, I drag it down.

Through my arms, through my ruined channels, into my chest, my gut, my legs.

Every inch of me feels like it’s being flayed and reknit with hot wire.

I shove my bare feet against the slick stone.

The sigils under me sear.

Prime Contract script flares in intricate, writhing patterns around my ankles, up my calves, trying to redirect everything back into its tidy, predatory system.

“Absolutely not,” I snarl.

I imagine each thread not as a claim on me, but as a vein leading *away* from the Node, into the city.

Into the streets and homes and shrines where people sleep, bleed, love, *live*.

I sever those veins one by one.

Cut, cut, cut.

Consent revoked.

Obligations nullified.

Too fast, too crude for proper legal poetry, but the intent holds.

The lattice above me spasms.

The beautiful, terrible geometry of the Prime Contract outlines itself in lines of white fire in the air—a massive triangle enclosing a web of smaller shapes.

For one breathtaking second, I see every clause.

Every loophole.

Every bit of ugly brilliance that let a handful of men own an entire city’s worth of lives.

Then the lines crack.

Shatter.

The triangle fractures at its apex.

The shock runs down into the Node and out through me.

The light changes.

Goes from red‑white to something paler.

Harsher.

Then to gray.

Color bleeds out of the Heart‑Node as if someone pulled its plug.

Its convulsions slow.

Stutter.

Stop.

The once‑gleaming surface turns matte, then ashy, fissures widening into full‑on craters.

For the first time since we stepped into this Chamber, I can hear myself breathe.

Then the Node collapses.

Not in one dramatic explosion.

In a series of small, bitter implosions as the overgrown crystal heart breaks apart, chunk by chunk, dissolving into fine gray dust that rains down around me.

The last of the energy slams through me in a single, brutal wave.

It rips the dagger from my hands.

Throws me backward like a toy.

Bone.

Wall.

Impact.

Everything crumples at once.

I hit the stone hard enough that the world whites out.

For a moment, I am sure my spine has given up, that this is it, that I’ve finally been reduced to so much pretty scattered bone, like the Node.

Then sensation drips back in.

Pain.

So much pain.

My hands feel flayed.

Every nerve screaming.

My clothes smoke.

There is a disturbingly acrid scent that I eventually, reluctantly, identify as *me.*

The Chamber is… quiet.

So quiet it makes my ears ring.

No heart.

No god.

No shrieking ritual.

Just the groan of settling bones and the distant, bewildered clamor of a city that just had something very old cut out of it.

I blink.

Twice.

My eyelids feel heavy, gritty with dust.

Bone ash drifts down from the ruined ceiling, pale against the red stone.

Where the Heart‑Node used to hang, there is now only an empty, blackened cavity.

No light.

No heartbeat.

Just absence.

“Well,” I croak.

“Problem… mostly solved.”

Stone is cold under my back.

I let it hold me.

Just for a second.

A shadow looms over me.

Then knees hit the stone at my side, hard enough that I feel it through the floor.

“Cyran!”

Lysa’s voice wobbles on my name.

Then steadies.

Of course it does.

Her hands are on me a second later—hovering first, then landing, cautious, checking for damage the way she checks for errors in contracts.

Her fingers brush my blistered palms.

I hiss.

She flinches.

“Sorry,” she says.

She sounds wrecked.

Raw.

Utterly, devastatingly alive.

I peel one eye open.

She’s close enough that all I can see is her face—smudged with ash, streaked with sweat, hair a mess, eyes wide and glossy.

Possibly the most beautiful thing I have seen all night.

“Did I…” I try.

My tongue feels thick.

Tasted too much god.

“Did I… quit?”

For a heartbeat, she just stares.

Then she laughs.

It bursts out of her like something sharp breaking, half‑sobbing, half‑hysterical.

“You’re fired,” she says.

Her voice shakes.

She leans down.

Presses her forehead to mine.

Every part of me protests the pressure.

Every part that isn’t screaming remembers the vision—the gold cage, the throne, the endless, lonely worship.

I close my eyes.

Breathe her in instead.

Ink and sweat and bone dust and the faint, copper‑sweet trace of her blood.

Not as currency.

As context.

As proof she is here.

With me.

Not *under* me.

Not owned.

Chosen.

Warmth that has nothing to do with divine residue curls in my chest.

“Well,” I rasp.

“Saved the city. Got sacked. Very productive evening.”

Her breath hitches against my mouth.

“Shut up,” she murmurs.

Her hand finds my cheek.

Thumb brushing the bone there, careful, reverent.

“You did it,” she says.

“No one—no one can ever say you were just their weapon again.”

I open my eyes.

“Good,” I whisper.

“Because I was rather hoping to spend the rest of my existence as *your* problem instead.”

Color rises in her cheeks.

Even now.

Even here.

“You already are,” she says.

Soft.

True.

I smile.

It hurts.

I don’t stop.

Then the edges of the Chamber blur.

The ceiling tilts.

Her face swims in and out of focus.

Somewhere, someone shouts orders.

Boots pound.

Bone creaks.

The city adjusts around the new absence in its chest.

I focus on Lysa.

On the way her fingers curl around my wrist, careful not to touch the worst of the burns.

On the way her eyes track every tiny change in my expression, as if she can hold me here by sheer observation.

“Lysa,” I murmur.

She leans in.

“Yes?”

I want to say a lot of things.

You kept your promises.

You’re the reason I’m still here.

For the first time, I chose to live as a man at your side instead of as a god alone, and I would do it again.

What comes out is,

“Next time we go on a date, can we pick somewhere with less… structural collapse?”

She snorts.

A lock of hair falls into her face.

“I’ll see what I can do,” she says.

Her voice is steadier now.

Comfortable.

Wrecked.

Real.

“Good,” I say.

Relief rolls through me.

Deep.

Bone‑deep.

There is still so much to do—trials, audits, rebuilding, explaining to an entire city why their god suddenly went silent.

But that’s later.

Right now, I have one last, selfish thought.

“If anyone from the Vigil asks,” I mumble, words slurring as the darkness finally pulls at me with comforting hands, “tell them I’m… taking a leave of absence.”

Her thumb strokes my cheekbone.

“You’re retired,” she says.

“It’s in the record.”

“Oh,” I say.

“That’s… nice.”

The ceiling fades.

Her face blurs.

The last thing I feel before the dark claims me is her leaning down, her lips brushing my brow in a touch that is not owed, not demanded, not taken.

Given.

Chosen.

Mine.

Then nothing at all.
