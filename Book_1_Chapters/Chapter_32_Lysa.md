# Chapter 32 — *The Auditor Arrives*
### Lysa | POV

The door explodes inward.

I always wanted to make an entrance.

The shockwave hits me like a wall—heat, dust, bone splinters. The scream of strained marrow‑glass. A gust of air rushes out of the Heart‑Chamber, wet and hot, like the breath of something that should have stayed buried.

I duck on instinct, arm crossing over my face.

Shards of calcified bone ping off the corridor behind me, skittering across the floor.

When the ringing in my ears eases enough that I can hear my own breath, I straighten.

Smoke boils in the doorway, thick with pulverized bone and the acrid aftertaste of the reagent I poured along the seals.

Marrow acid.

A gift from a man Ilan insists is an enemy with excellent manners.

I’ll audit that claim another day.

The room beyond is a smear of black and red through the haze.

My heart is beating too fast.

Not from the explosion.

From what waits on the other side.

Varos.

The Heart‑Node.

Cyran.

I tighten my grip on the scroll in my left hand until the old leather binding creaks.

The Old Law hums against my palm, warm and faintly eager, like a blade that hasn’t tasted blood in a long time.

I step through the smoke.

The Heart‑Chamber hits all at once.

Sight.

Sound.

Smell.

The ceiling arches high overhead, a cathedral of ribbed bone slick with condensation and thin runnels of congealed blood. The walls pulse, slow and nauseating, in time with the massive heart suspended from the ceiling by thick cords of petrified vessel and crystallized sinew.

The Heart‑Node dominates everything.

It hangs over the central altar like a grotesque lantern, swollen with dark light. Veins of red‑black energy throb through it, feeding sigil‑lines etched deep into the stone floor, spiderwebbing out under my boots. Every beat sends a ripple through the air, warping it, making the edges of the room shimmer.

It smells like a butcher shop inside a cathedral.

Copper and ozone and incense.

And underneath it all, sharp as a cut: blood, burned.

On the altar, chained spread‑eagle on slick stone, is Cyran.

For a second, the world tunnels down to the line of his body.

Naked to the waist, skin slick with sweat, every old scar and brand lit from within by the ritual. His throat brand glows a furious red, overlaid by the faint, silver ghost of Redemption Marks trying desperately to answer. New blood trickles from his nose, tracks down his temples, gathers in the hollow of his ear.

His back arches as the Heart‑Node pulses.

His eyes are open.

Too wide.

Too bright.

They find me through the smoke and the red light like a needle threading a specific bead in a shaking hand.

He looks at me like I am a hallucination.

Fair.

I feel like one.

At the foot of the altar, Varos whirls, hand still planted on Cyran’s shoulder from where he was pressing him down.

He looks at me like I’m a typo he needs to erase.

For a heartbeat, we just… stare at each other.

High Priest.

Ex‑Auditor.

Temple.

Tribunal.

Every overheard hymn and whispered complaint and carefully redacted report that led us to this room hangs in the air between us.

Behind him, the circle of priests stutters.

The chant falters.

The Heart‑Node’s rhythm, impossibly, misses half a beat.

Thump— —thump.

I lift the scroll.

“Stop the ritual,” I say.

My voice carries.

Not because I shout.

Because I project.

Auditor training carved my diaphragm the way the Temple carved its Relic.

Varos’s eyes narrow.

He lets go of Cyran’s shoulder.

“Kill her,” he snaps, without looking away from me.

Guards move at the periphery—Vigil armor, Temple livery, faces I recognize from a hundred reports. Men and women who signed Redemption contracts of their own, price tags hanging invisible around their necks.

They surge forward a single step—

—and freeze.

The scroll in my hand pulses.

Not visually.

Not yet.

I feel it.

Like a small, insistent heartbeat against my palm.

Founder law, Solen said.

Older than the god.

Older than these halls.

Magic respects the oldest law in the room.

I loosen the leather tie.

The parchment unrolls with a dry crackle that sounds unnaturally loud in the charged air.

The ink glistens.

It shouldn’t.

It’s older than anything else here.

But it drinks the red light greedily, lines darkening until the words look carved rather than written.

I inhale.

The air burns in my throat.

“I invoke the Founders’ Covenant,” I say.

The words are familiar now.

I practiced them until my tongue learned their weight.

“Section Four. Paragraph Eight. Nulla Vincula Sine Consensu.”

The language is older than Tribunal Latin, but the translation rises in my bones.

“No chains without consent,” I add, for the benefit of every trembling guard who has ever been told their suffering was legally binding.

The effect is immediate.

The air shimmers.

Not metaphorically.

Literally.

The red‑black light from the Heart‑Node hits an invisible plane in front of me and ripples across it like water against glass. The lines of the ritual circle on the floor flare—then distort, their neat geometry bending, fraying, trying to rewrite themselves around an equation they didn’t expect.

The guards jerk like marionettes with cut strings.

The compulsion in their eyes—Temple sigils, Vigil glyphs, little coils of magic that make disobedience taste like pain—flickers.

One of them, closest to me, stumbles.

He looks down at his sword hand like he’s surprised to see it holding a weapon.

“What—?” he breathes.

I don’t have time to reassure him.

The Heart‑Node’s pulse, which has been hammering so hard it made my bones vibrate, slows.

One heavy thump.

A long, stretching pause.

Another.

Slower.

Uncertain.

The whole chamber inhales with it.

Varos goes very, very still.

He doesn’t look at me.

He looks up.

At his god’s broken heart hesitating mid‑beat in the sky.

Fear flickers across his face.

Real fear.

Then he smooths it away.

“That law is dead,” he says.

His voice echoes, courtesy of the chamber’s geometry.

“A relic of a relic. The Founders are dust. The Prime Contract supersedes their little scribbles. You have no standing here, Auditor.”

The scroll hums louder.

My wrist aches with the weight of it.

“The ink is dry,” I say.

My own voice drops, low and precise.

It cuts cleaner than a shout.

“The precedent stands.”

I start walking.

Step.

Step.

The marks of the ritual circle under my boots flare as I cross them, then glitch, lines scrambling to redraw around the Old Law sigils glowing faintly beneath my feet. The guards flinch back, as if I’m made of fire.

Maybe I am.

My hand tightens on the satchel strap across my chest, feeling the familiar reassuring weight of the Blood Ledger copy and other pilfered documents.

Paper armor.

Better than nothing.

I keep my gaze fixed on the altar.

On Cyran.

His chest heaves.

Every breath looks like a battle.

Blood streaks his teeth where he’s bitten his tongue.

His eyes are still on me.

You came back, he mouths.

I want to stop.

To go to him now.

Unroll the shackles with my hands.

Put my palm over his burned throat and feel his pulse.

Tell him I’m sorry I let it get this far.

We don’t have time.

“You idiot,” I say instead.

Affection edges the words.

“Of course I did.”

His mouth curves.

Barely.

It’s as much smile as he can manage with half his face numb from divine pressure and pain.

It’s enough.

Varos moves to block the base of the dais, stepping between me and the altar like a judge taking the bench.

“You have no authority here,” he says.

“The Tribunal does not rule in the Heart‑Chamber.”

“I’m not here as Tribunal,” I say.

I stop three steps below him.

Close enough that I can smell the incense oil on his sleeves, the faint, stale tang of sanctified blood dried into the hem of his robes.

My fingers flex around the scroll.

The founders’ ink flares in answer.

“I’m here as representation,” I continue.

“Of whom?” he sneers.

I lift my chin.

Meet his eyes.

Cyran strains against his chains behind Varos, veins standing out in his neck, teeth clenched.

“The accused,” I say.

I tip the scroll toward the altar.

Toward the man bound there.

“And the accused pleads ‘Not His Property.’”

Varos’s mouth twists.

“No one here accused him,” he says.

“That is the point of this ritual,” he adds, as if to a particularly slow child. “He has been found wanting. We are not *punishing* him. We are repurposing him. Refining. Redeeming.”

“You are stripping his personhood and plugging the shell into your god like a replacement organ,” I say.

“Let’s call it what it is.”

My voice doesn’t rise.

It sharpens.

A few of the priests flinch.

Words can be weapons.

They taught me that early.

They just didn’t expect me to aim them at them.

“The contract upon which you base this Binding,” I go on, lifting the scroll slightly, “was signed when Cyran was dead. In the eyes of the Founders, that makes it void. Every clause you’ve built on it is theft. Every ritual you’ve performed with it is fraud. Every soulmark you’ve hung around his neck in the name of Redemption is an error in your ledger.”

The Heart‑Node pulses once, sharply.

A noise vibrates through the chamber, too low to be sound.

It feels like bone grinding against bone.

The Prime Contract is listening.

Good.

Let it.

Varos’s eyes flash.

“Your Founders are bones under our feet,” he says.

“Their law fed into the Prime Contract generations ago. What they wrote, we absorbed. What we absorbed, we improved.”

I take another step up.

We’re nearly level now.

“You didn’t improve it,” I say.

“You weaponized it. You took ‘No chains without consent’ and taught a generation to call coercion devotion. You dragged a corpse back to screaming half‑life just so he could sign a piece of paper you’d already forged his name on. That’s not improvement, High Priest. That’s malpractice.”

Soft laughter ripples from somewhere in the shadows of the ceiling.

High up.

Near one of the ribs.

Every hair on my arms stands on end.

I don’t look up.

I don’t have the bandwidth to deal with curious triangles right now.

“Enough,” Varos snaps.

He gestures sharply to the circle of priests.

“Resume,” he commands.

They try.

They open their mouths.

The chant stutters on their tongues.

The Heart‑Node beats, once.

Twice.

The ritual lines flare, surge—

—then hit the Old Law clause hanging in the air like a blade and… slip.

The conflict is almost visible.

Prime Contract script, all neat sigils and predatory clauses, ramming headlong into something older, rougher, carved with bone knives and bloody fingers by people who thought law should protect the living, not feed the dead.

The magic shudders.

Feet of the nearest guards skid on the stone as the compulsion jerks them forward, then back, like someone tugging leashes in opposite directions.

One drops his sword.

It clatters against the floor, breaking the last of whatever performance‑awe was holding the room in its thrall.

Varos bares his teeth.

He looks… less like a priest now.

More like what he’s always been.

A man in a very nice robe who has mistaken a long position of power for divinity.

“You are interfering with holy ordinance,” he says.

His voice has lost its smoothness.

“Do you understand what you’re doing, girl?”

“Yes,” I say.

“I am introducing reasonable doubt.”

I lift the scroll higher.

Speak the Old Law phrase again.

Slow.

Clear.

Each syllable drops into the red‑lit air like a stone into water.

“Nulla Vincula Sine Consensu.”

The scroll’s sigils blaze.

The light from them is cool, somehow, even in this blood‑warm room—sharp white, like moonlight on bone. It slices through the crimson haze, cutting a path straight to the Heart‑Node.

The massive heart stutters.

Stops.

For a breath.

For two.

The entire chamber holds its breath with it.

Priests freeze mid‑syllable.

The red glow dims.

Dark veins in the Node pulse sluggishly, confused.

Its hooks, which were reaching for Cyran, hesitate inches above his chest.

He sucks in a gasp like he can finally breathe.

His eyes slam shut.

Open again.

Find me.

Varos sees it.

Sees his god’s hesitation.

Sees his ritual faltering.

Sees me standing where no mortal should stand, scroll in hand, law older than his god hanging in the air like a verdict.

He panics.

He hides it badly.

“You think you can sue a god,” he says.

His tone is almost incredulous.

“Yes,” I say.

“If the god runs on contracts.”

Something huge and unseen shifts under the floor.

The dead god’s heart—a different one, buried deeper than this Node, the one the city was built around—thumps in slow, offended counterpoint.

A whisper brushes the inside of my skull.

Not words.

Interest.

Attention.

Someone—something—is leaning in to listen.

Good.

Witnesses matter.

Varos’s hand drops to his side.

When it comes back up, it’s holding a dagger.

Ceremonial, yes.

Ornate, yes.

Still a knife.

Bone handle.

Blood‑groove down the center of a double‑edged blade worked with tiny, intricate sigils that mark it as both sacrament and weapon.

“Then the court is adjourned,” he says softly.

He moves faster than I expect.

Age hasn’t dulled him.

He lunges.

The dagger arcs for my throat, edge catching the white light from the scroll and turning it briefly, beautifully scarlet.

There’s a sound from the altar.

Not words.

A raw, hoarse sound that tears itself out of a throat not meant to shout under this much pressure.

“LYSA!”

Cyran’s chains shriek against stone.

Metal bites metal as he strains, every muscle standing out, old scars whitening under the force of it.

For a heartbeat, everything slows.

Varos’s eyes, cold and intent.

The dagger, point glinting as it comes for me.

The scroll in my hand, humming like a struck chord, Founder sigils flaring in furious, ancient answer.

Cyran, bleeding and bound and still somehow capable of spending what’s left of his strength not to save himself, but to warn me.

This is what they wanted to erase.

This.

Choice.

Love.

The refusal to see a body as a tool when it can be a person instead.

I feel strangely calm.

My world contracts to three facts.

One: if the dagger lands, he kills me and the Old Law goes with me.

Two: if I flinch, I lose any authority I had in this room.

Three: I promised him I would stand with him, even if his choice broke us both.

I tighten my grip on the scroll.

Set my feet on the slick stone.

And instead of ducking, I step forward to meet the High Priest’s blade.
