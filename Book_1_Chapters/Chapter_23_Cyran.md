# Chapter 23 — *The Smear Campaign*
### Cyran | POV

Rumors in the Ribcage don't spread like fire; they spread like rot. You don't see the flames. You just wake up one morning and find the foundations of your life have turned to black sludge overnight.

It is early evening, the light bleeding out of the sky and leaving the bone-white arches of the district looking bruised and gray. I am walking a standard patrol route through the lower tooth-market, trying to force my heart rate down into something resembling a mortal rhythm. The memory of the tuning fork from this morning’s assembly still vibrates in the hollows of my teeth, a phantom ache that won't fade. 

But it’s Lysa’s face I can’t stop seeing. The way she stepped in front of me. The way her perfectly pressed Scribe uniform caught the blood dripping from my chin. The terrible, ruinous realization that she had just painted a target on her own back to save me.

I need to focus. I need to be a Vigil warden right now, not a man actively unraveling.

I turn the corner onto Weaver's Row. Usually, this street is loud—hagglers shouting over the clack of looms, merchants tossing coppers, children weaving underfoot. Usually, my presence earns a wide, respectful berth and a few cautious nods from the shopkeepers who know I keep the feral god-spawns out of their cellars.

Today, the street goes quiet as I step onto the cobblestones. It is not the silence of respect. It is the heavy, suffocating silence of fifty people stopping what they are doing to stare at a train wreck.

A woman pulling a cart of dyed wool abruptly crosses the street to avoid walking past me. Two men standing outside a tavern stop talking, their eyes tracking me with open, sneering disgust. 

I keep my face perfectly blank, the "ornament" mask slipping into place out of sheer survival instinct. My boots hit the stones in an even, measured rhythm. 

I reach the stall of Elias the baker. For the last six months, Elias has traded me fresh, unburnt flatbread for the rare, semi-legal herbs I occasionally procure during my deeper cistern patrols. He is a pragmatic man.

"Elias," I say smoothly, coming to a halt before his wooden counter. "Two loaves of the dark rye, if you please. And before you tell me the oven ran cold, I can smell it from three streets away."

Elias doesn't reach for the bread. 

He looks at me. His face is pale, his eyes wide, but there is no fear in them anymore. Only revulsion. 

Elias spits onto the cobblestones directly in front of my boots.

The sound of it is louder than a gunshot in the quiet street. 

The mask slips. I blink, genuine confusion piercing through the practiced arrogance. "Elias?" I ask, my voice dropping the playful timber. "Is there a problem?"

"Get away from my stall," Elias says, his voice shaking with a sudden, vicious anger. "I don't serve your kind. I don't serve Temple whores."

The words hit me like physical blows. 

I don't move. I can't. The slur itself isn't new—I've heard it whispered behind my back for centuries—but nobody in the Ribcage has dared say it to my face since I earned my Vigil leathers.

"What did you say?" I ask, my voice dropping into the absolute, dead-quiet register I only use right before I kill something.

Elias flinches, but he doesn't back down. Instead, he reaches under his counter. He doesn't pull a weapon. He pulls a crumpled, heavily printed sheet of cheap paper and throws it at my chest. 

It bounces off my armor and flutters to the dirt.

I look down at it. Then, slowly, fighting the sudden nausea roiling in my stomach, I stoop and pick it up. 

It is a broadsheet. The cheap, mass-produced ink rubs off on my leather gloves. The headline is printed in massive, stark type: 

**THE CORRUPTED SCRIBE & THE TEMPLE WHORE: HOW A MONSTER SEDUCED THE TRIBUNAL**

My lungs stop working. 

I smooth out the coarse paper. The text isn't rumor. It isn't gossip. It is a highly redacted, expertly weaponized compilation of my official temple records. It lists the exact dates of the "offerings" I was forced to participate in. It details the precise nature of the empathic feeding rituals. 

But it takes the truth of my abuse and twists it. It frames the centuries I spent as a prisoner as a willing, power-hungry seduction. It paints me not as a victim of the High Priests, but as a manipulative, vampiric parasite who uses sex and blood magic to enthrall my "masters."

I force my eyes to keep reading, my vision tunneling so hard the edges of the paper turn black. 

The second paragraph is worse. It details the assembly this morning. It describes Scribe Lysa stepping between me and Contract Lord Thessen. 

It doesn't describe an auditor upholding the law. It describes a "bewitched, legally compromised woman, driven mad by the corrupted blood of her paramour, abandoning her sacred oath to protect a monster."

Below the text is a crude, hand-drawn sketch. It shows Lysa kneeling before me, her gray uniform torn, offering her throat. The caption beneath it reads: *The Fall of Impartiality.*

The broadsheet trembles in my hand. 

They took the worst hundred years of my life and distilled it into a two-penny pamphlet to destroy the woman I love.

A harsh, ugly laugh breaks my focus. 

I look up. Two junior Vigil wardens—men I technically outrank, men I have bled beside in the deep cisterns—are walking past the stall. One of them, a broad-shouldered brute named Kael, bumps his pauldron deliberately hard against my shoulder as he passes.

"Excuse me, *Relic*," Kael sneers, putting a sickening emphasis on the word. He looks me up and down, his eyes lingering on my hips, stripping away every ounce of my martial dignity. "Careful not to get your armor dirty. Though I suppose when you're fucking your way out of a contract, you spend more time on your back than your feet anyway."

The second warden sniggers. 

The rational part of my brain—the part that survived the temple by enduring, by retreating inward and turning to stone—screams at me to walk away. It tells me that reacting proves them right.

But the anger is too vast. It is older and hotter than reason.

I move before the thought fully forms. 

I drop the broadsheet. My hand shoots out, faster than human eyes can track. I catch Kael by the heavy iron collar of his breastplate. I don't just grab him; I lift him. All two hundred pounds of him, hoisted dead off his feet with a single hand.

I slam him backward into the bone wall of the nearest building. The impact cracks a rib of the titan. Kael gasps, all the sneering bravado punched out of him in an instant. 

I step into his space. I don't bother hiding the monster. I let it out. I feel my fangs lengthen, pushing past my lip. The glamour I keep over my eyes slips, the silver flaring with a violent, predatory luminescence. 

"Say it again," I hiss, my face inches from his. "Say her name again, Kael, and I will rip your throat out and feed it to the god-spawns."

Kael’s eyes are wide, terrified, staring not at a fellow warden, but at death. The second warden scrambles backward, his hand fumbling uselessly for his sword hilt.

Around us, the street erupts into gasps and screams. People are actively running away now.

I look at their faces. The baker. The merchants. The second warden. 

I see the abject terror. I see the confirmation of every line printed in that broadsheet. 

*I am proving them right.*

The realization hits me like a bucket of ice water. The murderous rage drains out of me in a single, sickening rush, leaving nothing but absolute, crushing shame.

I violently shove Kael away. He crumples to the cobblestones, gasping for air. 

I don't look back. I turn and run. 

I sprint out of the square, moving too fast for the mortals to follow. I weave through the Ribcage, diving into the narrowest, darkest capillary-alleys I can find, fleeing the light and the eyes of the city. I don't stop until I am completely alone, deep in a damp trench of bone where the sun hasn't reached in centuries.

I hit the slick wall of the alley, my hands bracing against the greasy stone.

I dry-heave. 

There is nothing in my stomach but a few sips of water, but my body tries to purge the horror anyway. I slide down the wall until my knees hit the muck. I bury my face in my hands, my breath tearing ragged and harsh from my throat. 

*I ruin everyone I touch.*

The words aren't a fear anymore; they are a verified, documented fact, printed on a thousand broadsheets across the city. Lysa had a spotless record. She had respect. She had a future. And in one morning, because she chose to love me, the Tribunal took her entire life and set it on fire. 

If she stays with me, they will burn her down to the ash. If she fights them, they will use my history as the fuel. 

Footsteps splash quietly in the puddle at the entrance to the alley. 

I flinch, my hand dropping instantly to the hilt of my dagger, my head snapping up. 

It's Warden Vesper. My patrol partner. 

He stands at the mouth of the alley, his dark armor blending into the shadows. He looks exactly the same as he always does—tired, pragmatic, entirely unimpressed by drama. He doesn't look at me with disgust, or terror. He just looks incredibly tired.

Vesper walks forward. He doesn't draw his weapon. He unhooks a heavy iron flask from his belt and tosses it to me. 

I catch it reflexively. It's warm. 

"Copper-laced water," Vesper grates out, his voice a low rumble. "Drink it for the shock, or pour it over your head. Up to you."

I unscrew the cap and take a long, burning pull. The metallic tang cuts through the nausea. I lean my head back against the wall, refusing to look at him. "Did you see it?"

"Whole city has seen it by now," Vesper says, leaning against the opposite wall of the narrow alley, crossing his arms over his chestplate. "Tribunal runners distributed them. Coordinated hit job. Whoever wrote that garbage had access to the sealed Archives."

*Thessen.* The name tastes like bile.

"Where is she?" I ask, the words barely a whisper. "Where is Lysa?"

"Last I heard, she was barricaded in her office in the Spine. The junior scribes are shouting in the halls outside her door. They're demanding she step down from the audit." Vesper pulls a crumpled cigarillo from his pocket and lights it, the brief flare of the match illuminating the deep lines of exhaustion on his face. "If she walks out of that building with you, Cyran, they'll lynch you both."

Suddenly, the air in my lungs vanishes again. 

The magical pull—the hook Varos buried in my chest at the festival—snaps taut. It isn't a long, sustained drag this time. It is a sharp, agonizingly precise yank. 

*See, child?* Varos’s voice slinks through the marrow of my bones, rich with paternalistic pity. *I told you they would never accept you. Come home.*

I squeeze my eyes shut, a low groan escaping my teeth as I bury my fist into the center of my own chest, trying to press the pain away. The magic doesn't just hurt; it validates every toxic thought I've had today. It feels like the only truth left in the world. 

"What is it?" Vesper asks, his tone sharpening. He steps forward. "Are you hit?"

"No," I drag the word out, opening my eyes. The alley comes back into stark, miserable focus. "Just... an old wound."

I look down at my hands. The leather gloves are stained with the cheap ink from the broadsheet. No matter how many times I wash them, I will never be clean. I will always belong to the Temple, and I will always drag anyone who tries to pull me out of it back down into the dark with me. 

"I have to leave her, Vesper." The words sound dead. 

Vesper blows a thin stream of gray smoke into the damp air. "Don't act like a martyr, Cyran. It doesn't suit you. You fight back. You and the Scribe, you know the law. You take them to the central court."

"Did you read the pamphlet?" I ask, my voice rising, fracturing. "It's not a lie! They didn't invent the rituals. They didn't invent what I had to do in that temple. It's all true! If we fight this in court, every single degrading, horrific thing that was done to me becomes public record. They will drag Lysa through the filth to defend me. They will make her stand there and justify loving a whore." 

I drop my head back against the stone, the fight draining entirely out of my limbs. 

"I won't let them do that to her," I whisper to the dark. "I won't let my past destroy her future."

"So what?" Vesper asks, his voice harsh. "You just roll over and die?"

I close my eyes. I think of Lysa’s warm room. I think of the amber light, and the way she held me, and the impossible, desperate hope that maybe, just maybe, I was allowed to be a person. 

"No," I say softly. "I surrender."
