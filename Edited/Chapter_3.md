## Chapter 3: First Blood (Meet-Cute)

I smell the ink before I smell the fear—which is rare in my line of work. Most often, the scent of fresh terror hits me first, a metallic tang on the frigid night air that sings of an easy meal or a convenient distraction. It’s a primal, visceral signal that usually precedes the frantic thrum of a trapped heart, the desperate scramble of clawed feet on slick stone. But tonight, it’s the dry, almost sweet scent of parchment and iron gall, clinging to the shadows like a second skin, that prickles the back of my throat. It’s a scent that shouldn’t be here, not on *my* beat, not so close to the forbidden blood cistern, where only the rank copper of vital fluids and the acrid tang of ozone should dominate.

My patrol route is a precise dance through the Ribcage District’s jagged architecture, a labyrinth of calcified ribs and fossilized vertebrae that loom like skeletal giants against the bruised, star-dusted sky. Each shadow is mine, each gust of wind a whisper from a familiar ghost. I’ve been here for centuries, long enough to know the rhythm of this dead god’s body, the way the night breathes in its hollowed-out spaces. The Ribcage is a place of contradictions: life teeming in the shadow of death, whispered prayers mingling with the guttural cries of the Vein Markets, the cold, hard reality of bone softened by the flickering warmth of countless illicit hearths. I am a part of its contradiction, a living ornament in a city of the dead, a creature of violence tasked with maintaining a fragile peace.

Tonight’s rhythm is off. The usual symphony of distant shouts, the clatter of carts, the mournful cries of the lost—it’s all muted, overshadowed by a single, discordant note.

There’s a figure, dark against the deeper dark, silhouetted by the faint, arterial glow that pulses from the forbidden cistern below. They move with a furtive grace, too slow for a common thief, too deliberate for a lost soul. My eyes, honed by ages of twilight, pierce the gloom, dissecting the silhouette. Robes. Hood up. Hands, nimble and precise, exploring the etched runes on the bone-wall near the cistern’s lip. *Curiosity.* A dangerous trait in these parts, especially when paired with such a blatant disregard for the posted warnings.

My fangs prickle, a familiar sensation, a sharpening of instinct. This isn’t the clumsy desperation of a street urchin, nor the bold, drunken arrogance of a Guildsman. This is something else. Something *new*. The scent of ink, now stronger, confirms my initial assessment. This isn’t a predator, not in the way I understand it. But it is an intruder, and that makes it *my* problem.

I melt into the deeper shadows clinging to an ancient pillar of petrified cartilage. The heavy *thrum-thrum* of the blood in the cistern below vibrates through the ground, a constant, low growl that most mortals learn to ignore, a sound they interpret as the city’s heart, not a pool of raw, living essence. To me, it’s a lullaby, a promise of power, a reminder of my own nature. It also masks my approach, muffling the faint scrape of leather, the subtle shift of air.

There are three ways to catch a spy in the Ribcage, and my favorite involves gravity. I scale the gnarled bone structure with the silent ease of a spider, my leather-clad hands finding purchase in cracks and fissures that have been there since the god first fell, ancient wounds in its colossal form. The cold, rough texture of the bone is familiar beneath my fingertips, a constant anchor in the shifting currents of the night. From above, the figure below is a tiny, insignificant shadow, utterly oblivious to the silent hunter descending upon them. My lips curve into a slow, predatory smile. A delicious meal, perhaps? Or a more entertaining game?

I drop.

It’s not a fall, not really. More like a controlled descent, a whisper of displaced air, a silent, lethal impact that jars my bones just enough to remind me I’m still tethered to this mortal coil, still bound by the laws of physics, however tenuously. My boots hit the stone with barely a whisper, a sound swallowed by the omnipresent thrum of the cistern, by the chilling autumn wind that whips through the Ribcage’s hollowed spaces.

Before the figure can even register the sudden chill behind them, the subtle shift in the air that heralds a presence, I’m there. My left hand clamps down on their shoulder, a grip of iron that spins them around, forcing them off balance, their dark robes swirling. My right hand, faster than thought, brings the cold kiss of honed steel to their throat. The blade—my favorite, a sliver of obsidian-dark metal, sharp enough to part air—rests perfectly against the pulse point, a delicate whisper of death. I can feel the frantic beat of their heart, a hummingbird trapped beneath my thumb.

"Lost, little shadow?" I purr, my voice a low rumble, laced with the predatory satisfaction of a hunt well-executed. I lean in, close enough to feel the sudden, frantic beat of their heart against the tip of my blade, to inhale the heady mix of fear and that peculiar ink-and-parchment scent. Her breath hitches, a tiny gasp, a sound almost swallowed by the wind. Not a scream. Interesting. Most people beg when they feel the blade. She just stiffens, her body rigid, but her breathing, though rapid, remains controlled. A flicker of surprise ignites in my chest. This one is different.

Then, slowly, deliberately, a hand rises. Not to fight, not to plead. It holds up something small, rectangular, glinting in the faint, bloody light that spills from the cistern. The movement is slow, almost defiant, as if she knows I won’t strike.

A badge.

The predator mask, the one I’ve worn for centuries to survive, to be *necessary*, to be the monster they demand—it slips. My brows furrow. A blink. *Tribunal Inquiry.* The words are etched in stark, official script, glowing with a faint, almost imperceptible arcane light, a testament to the binding power of the city’s laws. A shiver, not of cold but of annoyance, traces its way down my spine, quickly followed by a spark of genuine irritation.

A bureaucrat. Of course. Only a bureaucrat would be so utterly devoid of self-preservation as to poke around a forbidden zone at this hour, armed only with… paperwork. And the audacity to hold it up to a creature with a blade at her throat. Unbelievable. My carefully constructed evening, my quiet patrol, utterly disrupted by a woman with an ink-stained badge.

With a sigh of theatrical exasperation, a performance already beginning, I step back, sheathing the dagger with a flourish that sends a whisper of metal against leather. The *snick* is satisfyingly final, a punctuation mark on the end of my failed ambush. "My mistake," I say, my voice shifting, losing its dangerous edge, becoming something silkier, more charming, a honeyed balm designed to disarm. "You didn't look like a bureaucrat. Too much… *spirit* for such a dreary profession."

She adjusts the dark, rough wool of her cloak, pulling it tighter around her, a gesture of quiet defiance. Her hood has fallen back, revealing a sharp, intelligent face framed by dark hair, pulled back severely, practical. Her eyes, even in the gloom, are direct, unwavering, the color of deep river stone. Annoyed. "And you," she retorts, her voice low, steady, a surprising contralto that cuts through the night’s hum, "don't look like a protector. More like… a threat."

I laugh then, a sharp, theatrical sound that echoes off the bone walls, a sound designed to charm and intimidate in equal measure. "I'm the Blood Relic, darling," I say, spreading my hands in a gesture of mock humility, a theatrical bow. "I look exactly like necessity. I am what they made me. What else would I be? A gardener? A poet? My purpose is etched into my very being, into the contracts that bind me to this city and its… less savory denizens."

She ignores the line. Utterly. My charm, usually so potent, so universally effective, washes over her like water off a gargoyle’s back. No flutter of eyelashes, no nervous smile, no coy glance. Nothing. My jaw tightens, but my smile remains fixed, a mask of practiced indifference. "I'm Auditor Voss," she states, her gaze sweeping over me, assessing, dissecting, as if I were merely another ledger entry. "I'm reviewing your patrol, and specifically, the efficacy of your… *protection*."

An auditor. Here. Now. I knew the Tribunal would send someone to poke their nose into my affairs, but I hadn't expected them to show up with such… *vigour*. And at knifepoint, no less. This isn't how I usually meet my auditors. Usually, they cower behind desks, or offer me sickly sweet wine in an attempt to curry favor. This one, however, seems to relish the direct approach.

I begin to circle her, slow and deliberate, invading her personal space, testing the boundaries of her composure. My eyes track her, searching for the tell-tale flicker of fear, the subtle shift of a foot, the involuntary clench of a muscle. I watch her pulse in her neck, a steady rhythm now, no longer frantic. She stands her ground, her head turning to follow me, her gaze steady, unwavering, almost challenging. No flinch. No retreat. Not prey behavior at all. My fascination, a dangerous spark, begins to ignite, mingling with a growing sense of frustration.

"Here for the view?" I ask, gesturing grandly to the pulsating red pool below. The thick, vital liquid reflects on the high bone arches, making them glow with an internal fire, like the beating heart of the city itself. A mist, thick with the scent of copper and ozone, rises from its surface, swirling around us, clinging to the ancient bones. "It's lovely this time of night. If you like gore, of course. A connoisseur's delight, some might say."

She takes a step. Not away from me, but *closer* to the edge of the cistern, her gaze fixed on the viscous, thrumming blood. Her focus is not on me, but on the macabre beauty of the scene. "I'm here for the evidence," she says, her voice devoid of inflection, a flat statement of fact. "Three bodies found here last week. All dead. All drained."

I join her at the precipice, leaning on a jutting bone-pillar, affecting a casual pose, as if discussing the weather. My pale skin, stark against the dark leather of my attire, catches the ruddy glow, making me seem almost ethereal, a ghost in the blood-light. "Ah, yes. God-spawn. Messy eaters." It’s a lie, of course, a convenient deflection, one that the Vigil readily accepts. Easier to blame a ravenous beast, a mindless horror born of the god’s decaying flesh, than to admit something far more insidious is at play, something that speaks of ritual and purpose.

She turns her head slowly, her eyes searching my face, piercing through the practiced nonchalance, the carefully constructed mask of boredom. "The reports said the wounds were... precise. Not torn, not gnawed. Precise."

The word hangs in the air between us, heavy with unspoken accusation, a bell tolling a truth I desperately wish to keep buried. *Precise.* Not the rending, savage work of a ravenous beast. Not the frantic, desperate cuts of a street thug. Precise. Like a ritual. Like a carefully planned sacrifice. Like… temple magic. The hair on my arms prickles, a cold dread seeping into my carefully cultivated composure. She’s too sharp, too quick. She sees too much.

I lean further back against the bone-pillar, crossing my arms, my pose carefully constructed to convey boredom, arrogance, anything but the sudden jolt of unease. "I'm a precise killer, Auditor Voss. It's why they pay me." I offer her my most charming, most dangerous smile, all teeth and sharp angles, a flash of predatory beauty. "To be precise. To ensure the sanctity of these hallowed grounds, even from its own... less savoury denizens. And sometimes, the most precise kills are the most necessary."

She doesn't smile back. Her lips remain a thin, unyielding line. Instead, she pulls a small, leather-bound notebook from a pocket in her robes and a slim stylus. The faint scratching sound as she makes a note is louder than it should be in the echoing silence of the Ribcage, a grating intrusion on my carefully orchestrated performance. "Arrogance noted," she murmurs, her gaze still on the page, as if I am merely a collection of data points.

My smile falters. She’s *writing* this down? My performance, my carefully crafted persona, reduced to a line item in a ledger? My centuries of existence, my forced service, my very essence, distilled into a bureaucratic observation. I feel a flicker of something hot and unfamiliar—a blend of annoyance and something akin to… offense. No one has ever dismissed me so utterly, so casually. "Is 'charming' on that list?" I ask, my voice a little tighter than I intended, a faint tremor of genuine pique.

"No." She closes the book with a soft snap, the sound surprisingly final, tucking it away with practiced ease.

The single word, delivered without a hint of irony or malice, cuts deeper than any blade. *No.* My usual tactics, my two most reliable weapons—intimidation and seduction—have both been blunted, rendered useless by this infuriating woman. She didn't scream at the knife. She isn't swooning at the charm. She's simply... observing. And judging. And writing it all down.

A new strategy is required. I shift, my movements fluid, graceful, shedding the skin of the arrogant predator for something more subtle. I soften my voice, lowering my lids, allowing a hint of vulnerability, of illicit invitation, to creep into my expression. The Seducer. The one who offers comfort, understanding, forbidden pleasures. "This isn't the place for official business, Auditor Voss. These bones hold too many echoes, too many secrets. The wind carries too many whispers, and the blood… the blood is always listening. My quarters are nearby. We could conduct a more... *proper* interview there. Wine? Fire? A comfortable chair, perhaps, instead of this rather draughty tomb, where every word feels like a sacrilege?"

Her gaze lingers on me, sharp and calculating. I watch her carefully, sensing the subtle shift in her pulse, the fractional widening of her pupils. I hear the faint quickening of her breath, a fleeting moment of human reaction. She considers it. A slow, deliberate assessment, weighing the risks, the benefits. "An interview," she finally says, the words measured, almost clinical. "Yes. Wine... optional. But a comfortable chair would be appreciated."

A small victory. She's coming to my territory. Where I control the light, the scents, the atmosphere. Where I can truly work my magic, where my charm can finally take root. I offer her my arm, a gesture of old-world gallantry, a silent invitation to take my lead, to be guided.

She ignores it. Completely. Her eyes, still fixed on some point beyond me, beyond the cistern, beyond the Ribcage, simply move. She turns and begins to walk, her footsteps light but purposeful, her dark robes blending seamlessly with the shadows, leading the way back toward the shadowed passages that lead deeper into the city. She doesn't even glance back.

I watch her go, a strange, unfamiliar sensation stirring in my chest. *Unsettled.* She walked past me. She didn't take my arm. She didn't even acknowledge the gesture. She is not the one being led. She is leading.

My hand rises, almost unconsciously, to touch my own neck, where the ghost of a collar might be, where Varos once had me bound, a shimmering thread of arcane energy that kept me in line. The phantom weight presses, a chilling reminder of who truly holds the leash in this city, and how easily it can be snapped back into place.

No. She isn't prey. She's something else entirely. Something dangerous. Something… intriguing. And for the first time in centuries, I feel a flicker of something beyond boredom, beyond the endless, weary cycle of my existence.

I follow.
