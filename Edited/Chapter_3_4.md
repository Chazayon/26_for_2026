## Chapter 3: First Blood (Meet-Cute)

I smell the ink before I smell the fear—which is rare in my line of work. Most people near the forbidden cistern reek of sweat and desperation, a delicious cocktail of terror and trespass. This one, however, carries the faint, clean scent of parchment and dried pigment, a strangely incongruous aroma for someone lurking in the Ribcage’s shadows.

The figure is cloaked in the deep indigo of the Tribunal, hood drawn low, but the cut is all wrong for a Watchman. Too slim, too careful. She pauses near the cistern’s edge, a hand reaching out to trace a rune carved into the colossal bone wall—a protection ward, long since defunct. *Curious.*

My usual approach involves gravity. There are three ways to catch a spy in the Ribcage: lure them, outrun them, or drop from above like a falling shadow. My favorite is always the most dramatic. Tonight, the heavy *thrum-thrum* of the blood-heated cistern below masks the sound of my descent. I land, silent and lethal, a mere breath behind her. The cold stone of the wall kisses my back.

My hand clamps onto her shoulder, spinning her before she can react. The glint of polished steel at her throat is an extension of my will, the blade’s edge a cool promise against the thrumming pulse in her carotid. "Lost, little shadow?" I whisper, leaning close enough to taste the ozone clinging to her robes, to hear the sudden, frantic drum of her heart against her ribs.

Most people scream. Or beg. She does neither. Her body stiffens, a rigid defiance, but no sound escapes her lips. Instead, a hand, steady as an undertaker's, pushes through the folds of her cloak, holding something out. A badge.

It catches the lurid, pulsing red light of the blood pool below, glinting with the etched sigil of the Tribunal. *Inquiry.* My predatory mask, perfected over centuries, slips for a micro-second. An auditor. Here? Now?

"My mistake," I say, stepping back, the dagger vanishing into my wrist sheath with a practiced flourish. The *snick* of metal is the loudest sound in the suddenly quiet night. "You didn't look like a bureaucrat."

She adjusts the dark wool of her cloak, her movements precise, annoyed. Her hood falls back, revealing a sharp, intelligent face, eyes the color of a storm-tossed sea. "And you don't look like a protector."

I laugh, a theatrical, sharp sound that echoes off the bone walls. "I'm the Blood Relic, darling. I look exactly like necessity." My gaze sweeps over her, lingering on the subtle tension in her jaw. She doesn't flinch. Doesn't even blink.

"I'm Auditor Voss," she states, her voice low, even. "I'm reviewing your patrol."

I begin to circle her, slow and deliberate, invading her personal space, testing the boundaries. This is my territory, my rhythm. Most would back away, eyes darting, searching for an escape. She stands her ground, her gaze tracking me, unwavering. Not prey behavior. Interesting.

I gesture to the pulsing, crimson depths of the cistern. "Here for the view? It's lovely this time of night. If you like gore."

She ignores the bait, walking *closer* to the edge, peering into the swirling depths. The copper smell, thick and metallic, intensifies. "I'm here for the evidence. Three bodies found here last week."

I join her, leaning against a jutting bone-pillar, striking a pose I know is both dangerous and alluring. "God-spawn. Messy eaters." It’s a lie, a deflection, but a convenient one.

She looks at me, her storm-grey eyes searching my face, cutting through the performance. "The reports said the wounds were... precise."

"I'm a precise killer," I purr, a hint of danger lacing my tone. "It's why they pay me."

She pulls a small, leather-bound notebook from her robes and makes a note. *A note.* "Arrogance noted."

I bristle, a low growl forming in my chest. She’s *writing*? About *me*? "Is 'charming' on that list?"

"No." She closes the book with a soft *thwip*, tucking it away.

My usual intimidation tactics are bouncing off her like pebbles off a granite wall. It's time for a different approach. I soften my voice, lower my lids, letting the Seducer emerge. "This isn't the place for official business, Auditor. My quarters are nearby. Wine? Fire?"

She considers it, her gaze still direct, unnervingly analytical. "An interview. Yes. Wine... optional."

I offer an arm, a familiar gesture of escort, of possession. She ignores it entirely, walking past me, heading towards the mouth of the alley.

I watch her go, a strange, unsettled feeling stirring in my gut. She didn't flinch. She didn't beg. She didn't even take my arm. She is not the one being led here.

My fingers trace the skin of my own neck, right where the blade had rested, right where I imagine a collar might be. Then, I follow.
