The chanting is a heartbeat, deep and slow. My own heart is a hummingbird.

Left foot.

Right foot.

White silk whispers around my ankles as I walk the aisle.

To them, I am a miracle.

The redeemed Relic. The Vigil’s favorite pet, given back to the temple like a peace offering. A symbol of cooperation between law and faith, all wrapped in pretty flesh and a perfectly pressed robe.

To me, I am a corpse on strings.

The Heart looms ahead, filling the far end of the chamber, a vast mass of petrified muscle suspended in a cage of ribs. Red light pulses under its surface with every sluggish beat, washing the hall in blood‑warm glow. The floor hums in time, slick under my bare feet.

The air tastes of incense and old blood.

I keep my eyes forward.

They want me to look up, I know. To glance at the balconies, at the donors leaning over the railings in their best bonecloth, at the gleam of gold pins and Command sigils, at the ghost of my old life in their hungry stares.

My body doesn’t indulge them.

My gaze is fixed, steady, on the altar.

My mind, on the other hand, is everywhere.

*Turn around. Run. Fall. Bite your own tongue off, choke on it, do something, anything—*

My feet keep moving.

Left foot.

Right foot.

The Key sits in me like a second spine.

Obedience is the only Virtue.

The words echo in the curve of my skull, not as sound now but as rule. A script. Every command Thessen laid on me in the prep room was a line in that script, and my body followed it without pause.

Stand.

Dress.

Take the blade.

Now: walk.

I do.

Smooth, unhurried, like a groom on his way to the altar.

The comparison hits me uninvited and makes something twist in my throat. The white, the aisle, the heartbeat drums, the gathered witnesses leaning in to see how the union plays out.

A wedding, except the vows are carved in blood and the honeymoon ends in a shallow grave.

Left foot.

Right.

The crowd parts ahead of me where the floor rises in a gentle slope toward the dais. Temple acolytes line the edges, heads bowed, candles in hand, faces a blur of shaved skulls and white paint in the Heart’s glow.

I search them for anything familiar.

A flash of brown hair.

Ink stains on fingers.

A stubborn chin.

Nothing.

The bond in my chest is still dulled to a smear of sensation, like I’m trying to feel her through wool.

My feet find the first step of the dais without me looking down.

The stone is warmer here.

Closer to the Heart.

Varos stands at the altar, arms upraised, his thin hands spread as if he could hold the entire city in his palms.

He is exactly as I remember.

Old and sharp, bone‑white hair bound at his nape, face a map of fine lines carved by centuries of pious cruelty. His robes are heavier than mine, weighed down with gold thread and sigils, the hem marked with dried stains from a thousand rituals.

His voice rises and falls in the old tongue, each syllable amplified by the Heart’s power.

“—sacrifice made, debt paid, life renewed—”

I hate that I still know the translations without thinking.

To his left, a little back from the altar, stands Thessen.

He has changed robes since the prep room, traded Tribunal cut for Temple‑appropriate finery, but he still looks like what he is: a bureaucrat playing at priest. His posture is perfect, his hands loosely clasped, his expression composed with just the right amount of solemnity.

Only his eyes betray him.

Cold.

Hungry.

Watching me.

I reach the top step.

My body stops an arm’s length from Varos, turns smoothly to face the Heart.

My hand—*my* hand—rests lightly on the obsidian dagger at my hip.

Sweat crawls down my spine under the white cloth.

*Don’t touch it,* I think. *Drop it. Let it clatter. Let the blade skitter across the stone and into the crowd. Make a scene any other way.*

The fingers do not twitch.

“Child of the Heart,” Varos says, his amplified voice curling around me like smoke. “Approach.”

That old name.

Child.

As if he sired me.

As if being carved out of my own life by his rituals makes me his.

I step forward.

Kneel.

The motion is fluid, back straight, head bowed at the precise angle to display submission without groveling.

My throat burns.

If I close my eyes, I can see another version of this moment, years ago, kneeling on the same stone as warm blood dripped off my chin onto the floor, donors gasping in delighted horror as Varos spoke about purification through suffering.

*You are not that boy,* I tell myself. *You got out. You chose.*

His hand descends.

Cold, papery fingers settle on the crown of my head.

“Look upon this redeemed one,” Varos intones to the crowd. “Once a weapon of wrath, now a shield of mercy. Once a sinner, now a servant. Behold the power of the Heart to tame even the most wayward—”

*That wasn’t redemption,* I think. *That was coercion. Lysa’s voice, in my head now, precise and furious. You were a person. They made you a thing.*

Something flickers along the bond.

A spark.

Closer.

Varos’s fingers tighten fractionally, as if in benediction.

“I name you my son,” he says.

For a moment, the sound drops out of the hall.

The drums, the chanting, the rustle of robes—all of it blurs into a dull roar behind my ribs. There’s only that phrase, echoing in the chambers of a heart that isn’t the god’s.

My son.

He has used it before.

In private.

In quiet corners where donors couldn’t hear, where he could play kindly father and wounded shepherd all in one breath.

I hated it then.

I hate it more now.

Not because it isn’t true—gods forbid—but because he believes it is.

As if his hand on my head, his mark on my skin, his words in my veins make him anything but my jailer.

*I am no one’s son,* I think. *Not yours. Not the god’s. Not Thessen’s. Mine.*

My mouth opens.

It wants to say the words the script expects: *Father. I am honored.*

Silence comes out instead.

Varos doesn’t seem to notice.

He lifts his hands from my head, turning back to the Heart, arms rising again.

The attention of the crowd follows him.

He believes the show has moved on.

Behind him, Thessen takes a small step forward.

Clears his throat.

I don’t hear words.

Not properly.

The Key doesn’t need them spoken aloud now.

It has my measure.

A single syllable threads under the rhythm of the chant, almost lost in the drones and drums. Not a word so much as an intent, snapped like a command seal into the lattice of my nerves.

*Strike.*

My body responds.

No pause.

No hesitation.

I rise from my knees in one smooth, trained motion, like a dancer hitting the beat she’s practiced a thousand times. The dagger slides from its sheath at my hip with a soft, obscene *shhh*, the sound of steel kissing leather somehow louder than the entire congregation.

The altar stones remember that sound.

They’ve heard it before.

Varos does not turn.

His voice rises, caught up in some particularly self‑aggrandizing verse.

“—we offer not only blood, but will, that all may see the Heart’s dominion—”

My feet carry me forward.

One step.

Two.

I move behind him, into his blind spot.

My own blind spot, once; I spent years performing in this exact arc, circling donors, moving just out of easy touch, always visible, always inaccessible unless invited.

Now I am an invitation with teeth.

*Stop,* I think.

My arm lifts.

The dagger comes up, smooth, the obsidian blade cutting a black arc through the red‑lit air.

Sweat beads along my hairline.

I can feel each individual muscle fiber as it engages: shoulder, bicep, forearm, the fine tendons in my wrist. It is like watching someone else’s anatomy lesson from the inside out.

*Stop. Stop. STOP.*

The arm trembles.

Just a little.

A quiver, barely there.

Enough for me to feel it like an earthquake.

A gasp ripples through the front rows.

They can see me now, the donors and acolytes and lesser priests. They see the Relic behind the High Priest, the blade poised where a sacrificial knife never should be.

Varos hears that.

He begins to turn, slow, puzzled.

I seize on the hesitation like a starving man on a scrap of bread.

I push.

Every fiber of will I have, every scrap of defiance honed on a hundred little rebellions, slams itself against the Key’s script.

My jaw locks.

My breath comes in ragged pulls, loud in my own ears.

The dagger shakes harder.

It’s pathetic, really.

A tiny tremor in a sea of obedience.

But it’s mine.

*Drop it,* I think, as hard as thought can be. *Drop it, drop it, drop it—*

Pain lances down my arm.

Like someone has reached into my bicep and twisted.

Thessen’s lips move.

Not loudly enough for anyone else to hear.

Just enough for the Key.

“Obey,” he murmurs.

The word slides like oil down the curve of my spine.

The tremor in my arm smooths out.

The pain doesn’t go away.

It just gets filed somewhere the Key doesn’t care about.

My grip tightens.

The dagger steadies.

Varos has fully turned now.

He sees me.

For a heartbeat, his eyes are only startled.

Then understanding dawns.

Not about Thessen, not about the coup, not about his own impending death.

About me.

About the weapon he forged now angled at his throat.

He opens his mouth—to command, to plead, to invoke some last clause in the old contract that he thinks might save him.

The Key is faster.

The script completes itself.

My arm begins the downward arc.

The Heart’s pulse seems to slow to watch.

Red light flares behind my eyes.

The dagger descends toward the space between Varos’s ribs.

*I don’t want this,* I think.

Not the way he means.

Once, I wanted him dead.

I fantasized about it, in the long, dull hours between rituals and beatings. I pictured sinking my teeth into his throat, tearing through his sanctimonious words with fangs and claws, making the temple watch their High Priest die at the hands of his favorite ornament.

This is different.

This is murder as performance, as policy.

This is Thessen’s ambition, wearing my skin.

*Forgive me,* I think, and I’m not entirely sure who I mean.

Varos?

Lysa?

Myself?

The dagger falls.

Halfway down, something cuts across the chamber.

Not magic.

Not drum.

Not chant.

Sound.

A clear, hoarse, furious sound that slices through the thick, red‑lit air like a scrivener’s knife through parchment.

“CYRAN!”

My name.

Full volume.

No title.

No pet name.

No temple epithet.

Just me.

The bond in my chest flares.

Like a struck match in a dark room.

The world jerks.

So does my arm.

It stops.

Just… stops.

Obsidian hangs an inch from Varos’s chest, point kissing the fabric of his robe hard enough to dimple it, not hard enough to break skin.

My muscles scream.

Every tendon in my shoulder feels like it’s trying to rip itself free.

The Key pushes.

Lysa’s voice shoves back, not with magic, but with memory.

*You can say no to me. I’ll believe you.*

*Choose what you want. I’ll stand with you either way.*

I drag in a breath.

The sound of it is ragged in the sudden, stunned silence.

The chanting has faltered.

The drums have.

The Heart’s beat stutters, just for a fraction of a second, as if the god’s corpse itself is confused by the interruption to its favorite pattern.

Varos stares at me, pupils blown wide.

Thessen’s face, in the corner of my vision, twists in shock and fury.

My arm shakes, dagger vibrating against thin cloth.

Slowly, fighting for every inch of control, I open my fingers.

One by one.

Thumb.

First.

Second.

Third.

The hilt slips in my sweat‑slick grip.

The point scrapes down Varos’s robe, leaving the faintest line.

I open my eyes.

I don’t remember closing them, but I must have.

The first thing I see is the blade, suspended, not falling.

The second is Varos’s throat.

I can see the vein there, fluttering rapid under thin skin.

I know exactly how it would pop.

The third is the aisle.

Far back, near the great doors, a figure stands out against the crush of bodies: filthy, bleeding, hair wild, white of a prison shirt peeking under temple‑soaked grime, one arm pressed to her side, the other hand cupped around her mouth.

Lysa.

Our eyes lock across the distance.

The bond between us snaps taut, bright and terrifying.

For a heartbeat, all the other sounds in the chamber drop away.

The only things that exist are my hand on the dagger, his throat, her voice echoing around my ribs.

I feel something crack.

Not in my arm.

In the script.

In the assumption that obedience is the only Virtue.

There’s room, suddenly, for something else.

Choice.

I don’t move yet.

Can’t.

The Key and Thessen’s command and centuries of conditioning are still a weight on my limbs.

But my arm is no longer moving downward.

And for the first time since this began, a vicious, impossible hope claws its way up my throat.

*She’s here,* I think, breath shuddering out.

And the smile that tries to twist my mouth this time?

This time, it might actually be mine.
