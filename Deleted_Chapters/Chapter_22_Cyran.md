# Chapter 22 — *The Puppet Strings*
### Cyran | POV

My hand is not my hand. It is a tool, and he is holding the handle. 

Stone bites the back of my skull.

That’s the first sharp thing.

Cold, unyielding god‑bone under my spine, ribs, heels. The altar in the Heart Temple is carved from a single slab of the Titan’s heartwall—white shot through with faint red veins that pulse when the Great Pulse nears. It used to feel… not safe, never that, but familiar. A stage I knew my marks on. 

Now it feels like a dissecting table.

Incense burns too thick in the air—layers of sweet resins and copper, the smell they use to cover up old blood and new fear. My tongue is thick with it. Every breath scratches. 

Leather straps pin my wrists and ankles.

They’re not Vigil issue.

These are temple things, older, crafted with that obsessive piety that makes cruelty look like love. They cross over my forearms in a neat X, buckles cinched so tight my hands prickle.

I pull anyway.

Muscles strain.

The altar doesn’t move.

The leather creaks.

A small sound in a room full of listening stone.

*Move. Come on. You’ve broken worse than this. You’ve bled out of worse than this. Move.*   

Nothing.

The Bells did their work.

My head still hums with the aftertaste of sound—Vein Prison’s walls, Voss’s face, Cyran, don’t follow, the crack of bone‑metal on stone. That’s all behind me now, traded for white stone and red velvet and the slow, steady boom of ritual drums from somewhere beyond the door.

They’re rehearsing my cue.

“How nostalgic,” I mutter, breathless. “Strapped to the furniture again. Really takes a man back.”

No one laughs.

The prep chamber is empty but for me.

No acolytes fussing. No donors peeking in to make sure the goods are polished. Just the altar, a low rail of candles, a stand holding folded white robes, and a table with something on it that gleams darkly even in the low light. 

Obsidian.

Of course there’s obsidian.

The drums thud once, twice, then settle into a slow heartbeat.

The city answers under my back.

The Heart Temple sits directly over the god’s heart‑chamber; you can hear it when it starts to wake. A faint, subterranean stirring, like someone turning over in their grave and remembering, dimly, what it was to beat. 

*Lysa is coming.*

I cling to that.

She’ll have broken out. She’ll have stolen keys, dissolved locks, talked some poor Guard into untying his own shoelaces and then tripped him with them. She’s too stubborn to stay in a cell. 

Unless they—

No.

Don’t finish that sentence.

The door opens.

Light knifes in from the corridor—a warmer gold than the marrow‑lamps, setting the incense smoke aglow. Footsteps: measured, unhurried, expensive.

Thessen enters like he owns the room.

He thinks he owns everything.

He’s traded his street‑ambush practicality for full ritual finery: layered robes of white and red, embroidered with sigils of Office and what he’d like you to think is Sanctity. The Prime Contract’s little abstract sigil gleams at his throat. His hair is still perfect. His smile is small, sharp, pleased. 

“Stop struggling,” he says mildly. “It’s undignified.”

“Funny,” I say. “I was just thinking that about your face.”

He ignores it.

Villains always do. They like their own monologues too much to let anyone else improvise.

He approaches the foot of the altar, hands folded behind his back, gaze traveling over me like he’s checking inventory.

“Back where you belong,” he muses. “On the stone. In the heart.” He sighs, as if burdened. “You have no idea how difficult it was to get access to this chamber on short notice. The High Priest guards his toys jealously.”

“But you’re going to share,” I say. “How generous.”

His lips twitch.

“I’m going to take,” he corrects. “There’s a difference.”

He produces a scroll.

Not the skin‑book from the Archive; that’s ash and acid in some screaming ward’s memory now. This is its twin: a neat copy, the kind a donor buys for an obscene sum when they want their own set of keys. 

My stomach turns.

He unties the red cord with reverent fingers.

Unrolls it.

The sigils along the edges shimmer faintly, reacting to the carved wards in the heartstone. Magic recognizes its own.

My hands pull against the straps again.

Pointless.

He holds the scroll where he can read and where I can see the precise, hateful line at the bottom. The ink is dark, fresher than the Archive’s dried brown script. Someone took care, copying this. Every loop and angle perfect.

COMMAND PHRASE: Obedience is the only Virtue. 

My throat closes.

Not from magic.

Not yet.

From memory.

The temple days come back in ugly flashes: my knees on this stone, my mouth forming sounds I didn’t understand, High Priest Varos’s hand on my shoulder, heavy and possessive. The donors watching from their cushioned seats, eyes bright, waiting to see what trick their pretty toy would do next. 

I used to think that was the worst it could get.

I was wrong.

Thessen clears his throat.

He looks up at me.

“You should feel honored,” he says softly. “You’re about to make history. Again.”

“Let me guess,” I say. “You already wrote the dedication page.”

He smiles.

“Don’t worry,” he says. “You won’t have to sign it.”

He reads.

“Obedience,” Thessen says, enunciating each syllable like it’s part of a prayer, “is the only Virtue.” 

The words hit like a thrown net.

Not a strike—no pain, no dramatic crack of lightning. Just… a settling. Threads of magic in the air wake, flare, and then dive, seeking their marks.

They find them.

Along my spine, where the Obedience lattice was fused to bone and nerve centuries ago. In the sutures of my skull, where sigils were etched into marrow. In the scar tissue on my wrists, old binding points humming in recognition. 

SNAP.

The world goes gray at the edges.

Color leaches.

Sound muffles, as if I’ve been plunged underwater. The drums outside become a distant, distorted thud. The incense smell dulls to a background taste.

What’s left is him.

And the phrase.

Obedience is the only Virtue.

The words loop, echoing, stripping away context, meaning, choice, until all that’s left is the imperative at their core: Obey. Obey. Obey.

My muscles go slack.

Not limp.

Relaxed.

Perfectly arranged.

Every unnecessary tension bleeds from my limbs. The old training rises, smooth as oil poured over stone: how to lie beautiful on an altar, how to hold still while hands move you, how to be an object taught to approximate devotion. 

*No.*

The thought is bright, sharp, furious.

It does nothing.

“Stand,” Thessen says.

My body stands.

There’s no gap between command and compliance.

One heartbeat I am flat on the stone, straps biting.

The next, I am upright, unbound. Feet find the floor as if I meant to, as if I’d chosen to swing them over the side and rise in one fluid motion.

My head swims.

Not from dizziness.

From the sudden, nauseating disconnect.

*Sit down,* I tell my legs.

They don’t listen.

They shift my weight evenly, knees soft, posture perfect. My shoulders square. My back straightens.

To anyone watching, I look composed.

To me, I feel like a marionette who’s suddenly realized the strings weren’t metaphors.

Thessen watches my face, fascinated.

“How does it feel?” he asks.

“Wonderful,” my mouth says.

The voice is mine.

The inflection is mine.

A touch of amused lilt, just how I’d say it if I were putting on a show.

Inside, I am screaming.

*Shut up. Shut up. Don’t say that. Don’t—*

“You see?” Thessen murmurs, delighted. “Seamless. The temple did good work. They left you so very… malleable.”

He gestures.

“Dress,” he says.

My body turns.

On the stand nearby, white robes wait.

They’re not the Vigil’s practical blacks. These are pure temple cloth: bone‑white, soft as sin, edged in red. The fabric smells faintly of incense and starch. Old days.

My hands lift.

Fingers close around the cloth.

*Drop it.*

They don’t.

They handle the robe with care, shaking it out, sliding the fabric over my shoulders. My head ducks at exactly the right angle. Arms slide into sleeves. The motion is slow, deliberate, an echo of old liturgy: how to vest a Relic properly for maximum devotional impact.

Button by button, my fingers work.

Dexterous.

Perfect.

I feel each small, traitorous touch: cool bone buttons under my fingertips, the drag of cloth against my skin. The sensation maps cleanly. It is my body. It is not my choice.

I want to tear the robe.

Rip it.

Instead, my hands smooth it down my front, erasing wrinkles with practiced grace.

Thessen steps aside.

A mirror waits against the opposite wall.

Of course there’s a mirror.

The faithful like to see the spectacle from every angle.

My feet carry me to it.

My reflection looks back.

White robe, red edging.

Hair combed back from my face, still damp from whatever unpleasant cleaning they did while I was unconscious. Skin pale, eyes a little too bright. If I didn’t know better, I’d say I looked…

Holy.

Deadly.

Like a weapon dressed up as a saint.

*This is not me.*

“It suits you,” Thessen says, coming to stand just over my shoulder. Our eyes meet in the glass. “The donors always did have excellent taste.”

“You’re too kind,” my mouth replies, lips curling. “Really, you shouldn’t flatter me. It goes straight to my head.”

Inside, I bite down hard enough on the thought of his throat that it almost feels like a physical bite.

He reaches past me.

On the table, the obsidian dagger waits.

The blade is a sliver of night, polished to a mirror, its edges so fine they almost disappear. The hilt is wrapped in white leather, designed to blend with the robe when I hold it just so, so the crowd sees a line of black death emerging from purity.

Thessen lifts it with something like reverence.

“Take the blade,” he says.

*No.*

My hand extends.

Slow.

I watch, helpless, as my fingers close around the hilt.

Obsidian is always cold at first touch.

Then it warms to the hand, drinking in heat.

It feels heavier than it looks.

“Tonight,” Thessen says, voice soft and hungry, “you kill High Priest Varos.”

My fingers tighten.

Not because I want to.

Because he said it.

Kill High Priest Varos.

The lattice in my spine hums, recalibrating: target, instruction, protocol.

Images flicker: Varos’s face, older now than when he first carved me, eyes full of that patronizing piety. His hand on my neck. His voice in my ear, praising my obedience while he rewrote my bones. 

I always thought, if I killed him, I would enjoy it.

This does not feel like justice.

“And then,” Thessen continues, stepping around to face me, “you kneel. To me.”

He smiles.

“Do you understand?”

“Yes, Master,” my voice says.

It doesn’t hesitate.

It doesn’t choke.

The words fall smooth and obedient from my lips, as familiar as the old temple responses.

*I am going to kill you,* I think at him.

The thought is a blade.

He can’t hear it.

No magic in the world makes him privy to the inside of my skull.

That, at least, is still mine.

He studies me.

“Say it again,” he says.

“Yes, Master,” my mouth repeats.

He exhales, pleased.

“Perfect,” he says. “I was concerned the Tribunal’s little rehabilitation project might have… dulled the edges. But no. Underneath the Vigil’s paperwork and the Auditor’s moralizing, you’re still exactly what they made you: a weapon that answers to the right word.” 

*I am not a thing.*

“I am not a thing,” I think.

My mouth smiles.

“Thank you,” it says.

Rage howls uselessly inside my chest.

I test the boundaries, the way I used to test cages for weak bars.

*Step left,* I think.

Nothing.

My body stays still.

Thessen hasn’t told me to move.

Obedience is the only Virtue.

*Blink twice.*

My eyes blink once.

Normal.

Reflex.

No free will in that, just biology.

The lattice doesn’t care about small rebellions that don’t contradict an order.

There’s the edge of the loophole.

Tiny.

Pathetic.

But there.

He paces once, twice, around me, like an artist admiring his installation.

“You’ll walk out at the peak of the Pulse,” he says conversationally. “The god’s heart beating in sync with the crowd. You’ll kneel with the rest of the clergy. When Varos steps forward to bless the donors, I’ll say the word, and you’ll stand and cut his throat.”

He lifts his hand, fingers resting briefly under my jaw, tilting my head as if checking angles.

His skin is warm, almost gentle.

Disgust roils.

“You will not hesitate,” he says. “You will not speak unless commanded. You will make it look like zeal—like a Relic overcome by the god’s will, striking down a corrupt priest. The people will weep. The donors will gasp. And then,” he smiles, teeth very white, “the Prime Contract will have its martyr. And its new High Priest.” 

He drops his hand.

“You’re overreaching,” my mind hisses.

My mouth says, “As you wish.”

He laughs quietly.

“You’re probably wondering why I don’t use you more directly,” he muses. “Why not just have you kill the donors? The Tribunal? The Creditor himself, if I could find him.” He shrugs. “Smart men don’t waste assets on short‑term theatrics. We build systems. Once I have the Faith under my hand, the rest follows.”

He steps back.

“Move to the procession,” he orders.

My body turns toward the door.

Feet carry me in smooth, measured strides.

The obsidian dagger glints once, then tucks against my side as my hand folds to hide it in the folds of white.

*Stop walking.*

I do not stop.

The room slides behind me.

The corridor stretches ahead, lined in carved heart‑bone, red banners hanging like open veins. Acolytes bustle past, heads bowed. Some glance up, eyes going wide at the sight of me, then drop again, hands making little warding gestures against envy. A few donors loiter in a side gallery, murmuring over seating charts; their conversation falters as I pass. 

They see what they want to see.

A Relic restored to place.

A beautiful monster in white, striding to their entertainment.

None of them see the man inside.

*Lysa. Lysa, don’t let them do this. Please. I know you don’t do prayers, but I’m improvising.*

The drums grow louder with each step.

Boom.

Boom.

Boom.

The Heart stirs underfoot, matching the rhythm.

I feel the Pulse like a low, anticipatory growl in the bone, building toward the once‑a‑year moment when it will slam into full, miraculous life. The air charges with it. Incense thickens. Voices murmur, rising in a tide.

We reach the antechamber.

Other clergy wait there: priests in white and red, junior acolytes with wide eyes and shaking hands, Vigil Wardens lining the walls in their practical blacks. Some of them I know by name. Some know me.

Their gazes skitter over me.

Over the robe.

Over the way I stand too still.

A few remember enough to be afraid.

Good.

Maybe one of them will flinch at the right moment and knock Thessen off balance.

My body takes its place in the line.

Kneel, Thessen thinks at me, unspoken but elegant.

He doesn’t have to say it aloud.

The phrase carved into my spine does the translation for him.

I kneel.

The stone is cold under my knees.

My back is straight.

My hands rest, folded, over the invisible dagger.

*You said you wanted to be a man at her side,* I remind myself. *Not a god. Not a weapon. A man. Men bleed. Men fail. Men get dragged back onto altars. It doesn’t erase the wanting.*

I latch onto that.

Onto wanting.

Desire is choice’s little brother.

As long as I can still want something other than what he tells me, I’m not gone.

Outside, the drums shift tempo.

Faster.

The Great Pulse is close.

When it hits, it will roll through every bone in this carcass‑city, rattling teeth, shaking shrines, making saints out of addicts for exactly one hour.

He thinks it will make me into his perfect puppet on the grandest stage.

Maybe he’s right.

For now.

The vast doors ahead begin to grind open.

Light spills in: the golden glow of hundreds of candles, the shimmer of donor jewels, a sea of faces turned toward the altar. The sound comes with it—a roar of expectation, the shuffling prayers of the faithful, the hushed gossip of the bored rich.

My body rises when the line moves.

Hands hidden.

Blade ready.

Inside, I focus on the smallest things I can still call mine.

The feel of the robe against the back of my neck.

The phantom memory of Lysa’s hand on my side as I slept, steady as a promise. 

The exact cadence of her voice when she said, If we survive, real wine. 

I cannot stop my feet.

I cannot open my mouth and shout the truth.

Not yet.

But I can hold those pieces like teeth around a secret.

Thessen stands at the edge of the antechamber, watching me go.

His eyes are bright with triumph.

“Showtime,” he murmurs.

My mouth smiles.

My heart, such as it is, answers with a single, silent word.

*No.*

I march toward the altar anyway.

Old as the Temple, older than the city. I recognize the pattern; I’ve traced it with my tongue before, slid it down my own skin in rehearsals, in rituals, in acts of carefully calibrated, performative pain.

“No,” I try to say.

My hand reaches.

Fingers curl.

The hilt fits my palm as if it was cast to the curve of my bones.

A chill runs up my arm.

Not metaphorical.

Literal.

Old god‑power, fizzing against the Temple’s newer tricks.

“Very good,” Thessen says, satisfied. “You always did have exquisite form.”

He circles me, assessing: the fall of the robe, the way the dagger hangs in my hand, the line of my throat.

“Tonight,” he says, “you kill High Priest Varos.”

Shock punches through the gray.

*What?*

My body does not flinch.

My fingers tighten just enough on the hilt to read as resolve.

“During the Great Pulse,” Thessen goes on, pacing slowly, enjoying the sound of his own plan. “At the height of the ceremony, when all eyes are on the altar. Varos will be leading the invocation. You will be at his side, the temple’s redeemed pet, the beautiful symbol of cooperation between Tribunal and Faith.”

He stops in front of me.

Looks me in the eye.

“In that moment,” he says, almost gently, “you will turn, and you will drive that blade into his heart.”

The word heart echoes oddly, falling into the deeper thump beneath our feet.

*No. No. I won’t. I—*

“And then,” he says, “you will kneel to me.”

The thought of kneeling to Thessen is somehow worse than the thought of killing Varos.

Varos is a monster.

Varos is the system.

Varos is the man who stood over my bleeding body and called it sanctified suffering.

Killing him would be… satisfying.

But not like this.

Not as a puppet. Not as someone else’s weapon, angled to fit a different man’s ambitions.

I open my mouth to say so.

“Yes, Master,” my voice says.

Soft.

Obedient.

Perfectly pitched to carry just far enough in a chamber, never straining.

I feel the words pass my throat.

They taste like copper and ash in my mind.

*I am going to kill you,* I think, staring at Thessen.

My face does not show it.

If anything, my expression in the mirror softens, lips curving in something like devotion.

The old blood‑magic works on more than muscle.

It paints over my tells.

“They’ll blame you, of course,” Thessen says conversationally, stepping away, adjusting a fold of my collar like a fastidious tailor. “A Relic gone rogue. A failure of Redemption. How tragic. How instructive. The Prime Contract will be appalled. But also… intrigued.”

He smiles.

“I will be there,” he says. “To comfort them. To promise tighter controls, revised protocols, a better leash. Perhaps this very Key, applied more broadly. They’ll give me everything I want to prevent another of you.”

*Another of me,* I think. *You haven’t even finished ruining the original.*

He pats my shoulder.

The touch lands like a brand, but my skin doesn’t flinch.

“Move to the procession,” he says.

My body turns.

Walks toward the door.

The dagger hangs at my side, perfectly balanced. The robe swishes around my ankles. My spine is straight, my breath measured, my gait smooth.

Inside, my mind is a storm.

*Fight it. Find the edge. There has to be a seam. A loophole. Obedience is the only Virtue. Obedience to whom? To what? Can I obey someone else instead? Can I choose which voice gets through?*

The Key doesn’t argue with me.

It doesn’t need to.

It’s not a voice.

It’s a script, burned into my nerves centuries ago. All Thessen did was flip the page back to the beginning.

The prep room door opens before I reach it, on some silent cue.

Temple attendants stand beyond, heads bowed.

They don’t look at me.

To them, I am part of the set dressing.

A moving statue.

A holy threat.

The corridor beyond glows with low red light, the very stone shot through with pulses of the Heart’s energy. The air gets thicker with every step, humidity rising, drums outside growing louder as the city gathers in the great chamber for the show.

My body steps over the threshold.

One foot.

Then the other.

I feel the slightest tug in my chest.

Not the Key.

Not Thessen.

Lysa.

Closer.

Angry as a spark in dry straw.

*Don’t come,* I think again, shoving the thought along that faint tether with everything I have. *He’ll use you. He’ll use me against you. Run. Live. Let this be my last bad decision instead of ours.*

My hand tightens on the dagger.

Thessen’s voice follows me out.

“Smile for them,” he says lightly. “You’re the star tonight.”

My lips obey.

My mind bares its teeth.

The drums swell.

I march toward my own execution, towards someone else’s murder, toward the choice they think has already been taken from me.

Under the calm surface the Key has lacquered over me, a single thin line of thought stays bright and hard.

*I am not broken. I am bound. There’s a difference.*

And if there is any gap, any fracture, any sliver of space between his words and my will, I will find it.

Even if I have to bleed myself through it.
